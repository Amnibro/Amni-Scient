Chat with Multiple AIs at Once

CLAUDE • GROK • GEMINI • A LOCAL MODEL — ONE QUESTION, EVERY ANSWER

If you pay for more than one AI, you have probably done the tab dance: paste the same question into Claude, then Grok, then Gemini, then squint at three browser windows trying to remember who said what. Every model has blind spots. The whole reason to ask more than one is to catch them — but browser tabs make that comparison manual, slow, and easy to skip.

What a multi-AI round table actually is

A round table sends one question to every connected model at the same time. Each AI is a seat. You write the prompt once, hit enter once, and every seat answers in the same conversation. From there you have two ways to read the room:

  • Side by side — every model gets its own column. Best for comparing answers cold: who hedged, who committed, who hallucinated.
  • Sequential — the question passes down the line and each seat reads the answers before it. Models stop repeating each other and start correcting each other. Disagreements surface on their own.

Why "reads the others first" matters

Asking three models blind gets you three first drafts. Letting each model see the previous answers gets you something closer to a working session: the second seat flags what the first missed, the third weighs both and commits. In practice this is where multi-AI chat earns its keep — a single model states its guess confidently; a table of them has to defend it.

The part nobody wants: another metered bill

Most multi-model apps proxy your traffic through their servers and re-meter every token. Braid takes the other route: it drives the official CLI tools you are already signed into, so a seat costs whatever your existing subscription costs. Pay-as-you-go API keys and local models (Ollama, or the bundled Adam-3B) work too — and keys never leave your machine.
