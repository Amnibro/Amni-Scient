FEA VALIDATION

CANTILEVER BEAM • PLATE WITH A HOLE • FIVE MESH LEVELS EACH

DOWNLOAD THE DATA (CSV) ALL VERIFICATION TESTS

SUMMARY

Two textbook cases, each solved by the Amni-CAD stress solver on five meshes from coarse to fine. Every number on this page is the solver’s own output, recorded on November 22, 2026. The full data is in the CSV.

  • Cantilever tip deflection: +0.13 % on the finest adaptive hex mesh and −0.29 % on the finest voxel mesh, against PL³/3EI. Every level of both meshers is within 0.62 %.
  • Cantilever bending stress one beam depth from the wall: +0.25 % on the finest mesh against M(x)c/I, and within 0.28 % at every level.
  • Plate with a hole, peak stress: −1.09 % on the finest mesh against Peterson’s finite-width Kt, and within 3.44 % at every level.

CASE 1: CANTILEVER BEAM WITH AN END LOAD

A square steel bar, fully fixed on one end face, with a downward force spread evenly over the free end face. The bar is built in the CAD kernel and meshed by Amni-CAD; the solver sees the same model a user would.

Bar: L = 200 mm (7.874 in), b = h = 20 mm (0.787 in) Steel: E = 200,000 MPa (29.0 Msi), ν = 0.3, G = E / 2.6 = 76,923 MPa Load: F = 700 N (157.4 lbf) down on the free end face I = b h³ / 12 = 13,333.3 mm⁴ Tip deflection (Euler-Bernoulli) δ = F L³ / (3 E I) = 0.7000 mm With shear (Timoshenko, κ = 5/6) δ + F L / (κ G A) = 0.7055 mm Root bending stress σ = M c / I = F L (h/2) / I = 105.0 MPa (15,229 psi) Bending stress at distance x σ(x) = F (L − x)(h/2) / I

Section stress is the solver’s von Mises value at the top-surface node closest to x = 20 mm (one beam depth from the wall, on the centerline), compared with σ(x) at that node’s exact position. On the outer fiber the stress is uniaxial, so von Mises equals the bending stress. The clamp-edge peak is the largest von Mises value the app reports away from the supports. A fully fixed face also locks the Poisson contraction at the wall, which makes the stress at the clamped edge singular, so that peak keeps rising as the mesh refines. The section one depth away is where beam theory applies.

CASE 2: PLATE WITH A CENTRAL HOLE IN TENSION

A thin steel strip with a round hole through the middle, fully fixed on one end and pulled by an even force on the other. The hole sits 140 mm (two widths) from each end, so the end conditions do not reach it.

Plate: L = 280 mm (11.02 in), W = 70 mm (2.756 in), t = 3.5 mm (0.138 in) Hole: d = 14 mm (0.551 in) at the center, d/W = 0.2 Steel: E = 200,000 MPa (29.0 Msi), ν = 0.3 Load: F = 7,000 N (1,574 lbf) tension on the free end face Gross stress σgross = F / (W t) = 28.57 MPa Net stress σnet = F / ((W − d) t) = 35.71 MPa (5,180 psi) Peterson / Howland finite-width factor, net section: Kt = 2 + 0.284 (1 − d/W) − 0.600 (1 − d/W)² + 1.32 (1 − d/W)³ = 2.519 Peak stress σmax = Kt σnet = 89.97 MPa (13,048 psi)

The peak is the largest von Mises value on the hole surface, which is also the peak the app reports away from the supports for every mesh here. At the hole edge the stress is uniaxial, so von Mises equals the hoop stress that Kt predicts. The swept hex mesh is what Auto picks for this plate.

METHODS

  • Solver: Amni-CAD linear static solver from the Amni-CAD source at commit c3b1d7c5, the same code that runs in the browser, run in Node.js 24 on an 8-vCPU Intel Xeon with the CPU backend. The equations are solved by conjugate gradients with a multigrid preconditioner; every solve converged to a relative residual below 1×10−6.
  • Element: 8-node linear hexahedron with incompatible bending modes (QM6). The adaptive and swept meshers fit the part surface; the voxel mesher fills the part with equal cubes.
  • Refinement: each case runs at the app’s mesh-resolution targets of 7,000, 14,000, 28,000, 56,000 and 112,000 elements. The table lists the element count each mesher actually produced.
  • Stress: von Mises, averaged at the nodes, as shown in the app’s stress plot. Displacements are read at the surface nodes.
  • Loads and supports: a total force spread evenly over a face, and a fully fixed face. Reactions balance the applied force to within 10−5 N in every run.
  • Solve time: wall clock on the machine above. Adaptive and swept times include meshing; voxel times cover the solve.
  • Data: amni-cad-validation.csv holds every run on this page with element count, node count, element size, solver value, textbook value, error, solve time and solver iterations.
