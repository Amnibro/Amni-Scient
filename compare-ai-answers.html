Compare AI Answers Side by Side

SAME PROMPT • PARALLEL COLUMNS • THE DISAGREEMENTS ARE THE SIGNAL

A single AI answer always sounds right. That is the problem. Confidence is free; correctness is not, and without a second opinion you cannot tell them apart. The cheapest hallucination detector available today is another frontier model looking at the same prompt — Claude, Grok and Gemini are trained differently enough that they rarely make the same mistake.

Column mode: comparing them cold

Side-by-side columns put every model's answer to the same prompt on one screen. Read across, not down. In thirty seconds you see who committed to an answer, who hedged, whose citation looks invented, and where all three quietly agree — which is usually the part you can trust.

Sequential mode: making them argue

Cold comparison misses errors that sound plausible to a human. The fix is to make the models read each other. In sequential mode each seat sees every answer before it: the second model flags what the first missed, the third has to pick a side and say why. Disagreement between models is the most reliable "look closer here" signal there is — and it surfaces automatically instead of depending on your skim.

What we see in real sessions

Different seats earn their keep on different jobs — one tends to write the tightest code, another catches edge cases in review, another is fastest to a straight factual answer. The honest summary: no single model wins everything, which is precisely the argument for putting them at one table and letting the question decide.
