Flooring is the highest-leverage DIY trade in the house. A 14×12 bedroom floored in mid-grade luxury vinyl plank costs $850–$1,700 professionally installed; the materials are $600–$900 of that, and click-lock LVP was engineered specifically so a careful amateur can lay it in a day. Hardwood swings wider — $5–$12 per square foot in materials, $8–$18 installed — and tile sits in between on materials but carries the steepest skill premium.

What trips people up is not the install, it is the math: boxes come in odd coverages (24 sq ft here, 19.8 there), waste depends on the laying pattern, tile needs backer board and thinset priced separately, and every doorway wants a transition strip. The free Amni-Floor estimator handles all of it: set the room as a rectangle, draw any polygon in the Draw Shape tab, or trace your actual floor plan in Photo Trace; pick material and pattern; and it returns exact box counts with pattern-correct waste, underlayment or backer/thinset/grout for tile, trim and transitions, a plank-run layout plan, and a bill of materials priced against Home Depot and Lowe's. Everything runs in your browser — no account, no uploads.

What Drives the Cost

  • Material choiceLVP runs $2–$5 per sq ft in materials, laminate $1.50–$4, engineered hardwood $4–$10, solid hardwood $5–$12, porcelain tile $2–$7 plus another $1.50–$2.50 in backer board, thinset and grout. Carpet is $2–$6 plus pad, but stretch-in installation is genuinely hard to DIY well.
  • Pattern and wasteA straight or brick-offset lay wastes 5–8%; diagonal 10–12%; herringbone 15–20% because every course starts and ends with an angled cut. The estimator sets waste automatically from the pattern you pick (leave the waste field at 0 for auto) and recomputes boxes the moment you switch.
  • Subfloor prepFloating floors need flatness of 3/16 in over 10 ft — self-leveler runs $35–$40 per 50 lb bag and a wavy slab can eat several. Concrete below grade needs a 6-mil vapor barrier under LVP or laminate. Tile needs cement board ($12–$16 per 3×5 sheet) or an uncoupling membrane, thinset on both sides.
  • Doorways and transitionsEvery doorway needs a transition: T-molding between equal heights, a reducer to lower floors, stair nose at steps — $20–$45 per piece, $50–$90 for flush hardwood stair nose. The sidebar's doorway count feeds them into the bill of materials.
  • Trim and expansionFloating floors need a 1/4–3/8 in expansion gap at every wall, hidden by quarter round or new baseboard at roughly $1–$2.50 per linear foot.

Pencil Out a Typical Project

Run the tool's default room: 14×12 ft, 168 sq ft, two doorways. Choose 6×48 in LVP at $3.49 per sq ft sold in 24 sq ft boxes, brick-offset pattern. Auto waste at 8% brings the requirement to 182 sq ft — eight boxes, 192 sq ft purchased, $670.

Add the sundries the estimator lists with it: 6-mil vapor barrier over the slab $25, two doorway transitions $65, 52 linear feet of quarter round $70, spacers and a tapping-block kit $25. Total: about $855 at mid-2026 big-box prices. The same room in 3/4-in solid oak at $6.99 per sq ft is roughly $1,270 in flooring alone plus felt paper and cleats — and toggling the pattern to herringbone pushes the LVP order to nine boxes.

A contractor quote for the LVP room typically lands at $1,500–$2,300 installed. The spread is one weekend of your labor, most of it spent on the first three rows.

Common Pitfalls

  • Skipping acclimationSolid hardwood needs 5–7 days stacked in the room it will live in; engineered and laminate typically 48 hours. Skip it and the floor gaps in winter or buckles in summer. LVP is more forgiving but should still install within its temperature spec.
  • Laying over an unflat subfloorClick-lock joints are rated for 3/16 in of deviation over 10 ft. Over a wavy slab they flex, click apart, and squeak within months. Check with a straightedge before buying flooring — self-leveler is cheaper than a re-install.
  • Buying the exact square footageZero-waste orders guarantee a mid-job store run, and the new box may be a different dye lot that reads as a visible stripe. Buy the pattern-correct waste the estimator computes.
  • Pinning down a floating floorQuarter round nailed into the flooring, cabinets set on click-lock LVP, transitions screwed through planks — all of these lock a floor designed to move, and it buckles in response. Trim nails go into the wall; cabinets get flooring cut around them.
  • Tiling over a flexing substrateTile is rigid; its substrate must be too. Deflection beyond L/360 cracks grout lines first and tiles second. Over wood subfloors use cement board or an uncoupling membrane set in thinset — never screw backer to joists dry, and never tile directly onto OSB.

Straight Answers

How much extra flooring should I buy for waste?
5–8% for straight or brick-offset lays, 10–12% for diagonal, 15–20% for herringbone, and add a few points for rooms with many closets and jogs. The estimator applies these automatically by pattern. Also keep one spare box — dye lots change and later purchases rarely match.
Do I need underlayment under LVP?
Most mid-grade LVP now ships with an attached pad — adding a second foam layer under it voids warranties and makes joints fail. What concrete slabs do need is a 6-mil vapor barrier if the plank lacks one built in. Laminate without attached pad needs a foam underlayment at $0.30–$0.60 per sq ft.
Can I install LVP over existing tile or vinyl?
Yes, if the old floor is flat, well-adhered and clean — skim grout lines wider than about 1/4 in so they do not telegraph. Watch the added height at doorways and appliances: doors may need undercutting and a dishwasher can get trapped under a countertop.
Which direction should the planks run?
Convention: parallel to the longest wall or the main light source, and for nail-down hardwood, perpendicular to the joists. The layout plan draws the run — stagger and end-cuts visible before you buy — and the main-wall setting flips it in one click.
Is herringbone worth attempting as a first project?
It is the hardest common pattern: 15–20% waste, angled cuts on every course, and the first two rows define every line in the room. Toggle the pattern in the estimator to price it honestly, then dry-lay the first courses before clicking anything together.
