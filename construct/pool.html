No backyard project has a wider price spread than a pool. The Big Cost Decisions

  • Pool type sets the budget tierAbove-ground steel or resin kits run $1,800–$6,500 plus $1,000–$3,000 of site prep, and assembly is genuinely DIY-able. Vinyl-liner inground kits run $12,000–$22,000; fiberglass shells $20,000–$40,000 before the crane; gunite is contractor-only.
  • Size and depth profileGallons = length × width × average depth × 7.48, with the average set by your depth profile. Stretching the deep end from 6 ft to 8 ft adds excavation, wall height, tons of water, and a permanent heating surcharge.
  • Excavation and haul-offA typical residential dig is $1,500–$6,000, more in rock or tight access. Soil swells about 25% when excavated: a 16×32 pool displaces roughly 95 bank cu yd, closer to 140 once overdig is included. The estimator reports the yardage so every excavator bids the same hole.
  • Pump and filterUS DOE rules have required variable-speed technology on most residential pumps since 2021: budget $700–$1,200; it repays itself in electricity within two seasons. Filters: sand $350–$650, cartridge $400–$900, sized so the system turns the pool over in 8–10 hours.
  • HeatingA 400k BTU gas heater is $2,500–$4,000 and heats fast but burns money; a heat pump is $3,000–$6,500, cheap to run, slow in cool air. Required BTU is gallons × 8.34 × desired rise, divided by the hours available — the heater checkbox and temp-rise input do this arithmetic before a salesman does.
  • Barrier, bonding and inspectionsNearly every US jurisdiction requires a 48 in minimum barrier with self-closing, self-latching gates (latch at 54 in) per the IRC AG105 lineage, plus NEC 680 equipotential bonding and GFCI protection. Fencing at $25–$60 per linear foot is the line item people forget; the permit panel surfaces it early.

What a Typical Project Costs

Price a 16×32 ft rectangular inground, 42 in shallow end sloping to 78 in: average depth 5 ft, 2,560 cu ft, about 19,100 gallons. An 8-hour turnover needs 40 gpm — a variable-speed pump at $900 and a sand filter at $450. The DIY-able scope: 2 in PVC plumbing loop with skimmers, returns and valves — $700–$1,000; equipment pad, bonding lugs and conduit — $500–$800 plus a licensed electrician's $1,500–$3,000 hookup; 130 linear feet of code-compliant barrier fence — $3,900–$6,500; optional 140k BTU heat pump for a 20-degree rise — $4,500.

The contractor scope the tool flags honestly: dig and haul-off of roughly 140 loose cu yd — $3,500–$6,000; the vinyl kit with walls, liner and installation help — $14,000–$20,000; or a gunite shell at $35,000–$60,000 — at which point you are hiring a pool builder, full stop.

Realistic all-in for the vinyl DIY-assist path: $28,000–$40,000 including concrete decking, against $55,000–$75,000 turnkey quotes for the same pool. The spread is project management and sweat, and the estimator's CSV export is the bid sheet you hand each sub.

Mistakes That Cost Real Money

  • Budgeting the shell and forgetting the everything-elseDecking, fencing, the electrical run, landscaping repair, and even the fill water (19,000 gallons is $400–$1,000 trucked, or days on a hose plus a utility sewer-credit form) routinely add 30–50% to the first number written down. The estimator itemizes them up front.
  • Treating the barrier as a punch-list itemThe fence is a life-safety code requirement — drowning is the leading cause of accidental death for US children aged 1–4 — and an unfinished barrier fails the final inspection every time. Price and schedule it with the deck, not after.
  • Oversizing the pumpA bigger pump is not a better pump: oversized flow wastes electricity, cavitates plumbing and shortens filter life. Size from turnover math — gallons over 8–10 hours — and let a variable-speed unit run low and slow.
  • Digging on hopeGet utility locates and the soil story before the excavator mobilizes — high water tables need dewatering, expansive clays may need an engineer, and rock turns a $3,000 dig into a $10,000 one. Put unit prices for rock and overexcavation in the dig contract, not a handshake.

FAQ

How do I calculate how many gallons my pool holds?
Length × width × average depth × 7.48. Average depth on a straight slope is (shallow + deep) divided by 2. The tool computes it from the drawn shape and both depths, even for traced freeform outlines.
Can I really build an inground pool myself?
Parts of one. Vinyl-liner kits are sold to owner-builders who sub out the dig; plumbing, pad, deck and fence are honest DIY. Gunite and fiberglass setting are not — anyone saying otherwise is selling something. The estimator keeps those columns separate on purpose.
What does a pool cost to run each year?
Figure $120–$300 in electricity with a variable-speed pump (3–5× that on an old single-speed), $300–$800 in chemicals, and heating from $0 to several thousand depending on climate and fuel. A solar cover is the best dollar-for-dollar upgrade on any heated pool.
Do above-ground pools need a fence too?
Usually — but walls 48 in or taller can count as the barrier themselves when ladders are removable or lockable. Deck-mounted above-ground pools need gated guardrails. Amendments vary — the permit panel gives your state's baseline; your inspector rules.
What size heater do I need?
BTU per hour = gallons × 8.34 × desired temperature rise, divided by the hours you will wait. The 19,100-gallon example needs about 133k BTU/hr for a 20-degree rise in 24 hours — a 140k heat pump, or a 400k gas unit for fast weekend heat. The temp-rise input sizes it for you.
