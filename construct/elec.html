Electricians run $80–$150 an hour in 2026, a new 20 A circuit quotes at $300–$800, and a whole-house rewire lands at $8,000–$20,000. The materials underneath those numbers are modest — $50–$120 for that circuit, $1.50–$3 per square foot for a whole house — but copper is the wildcard: after the 2025 price run-up, 12/2 NM-B (Romex) sits at $0.80–$1.20 per foot cut to length. Wire is routinely half the materials bill now, making an accurate footage takeoff worth real money.

Amni-Elec attacks the estimate from both ends. The sidebar runs a live NEC Article 220 dwelling load calculation — square footage, bedrooms, bathrooms, range, dryer, HVAC amps, water heater, dishwasher, disposal, microwave — and turns it into a service size (100–400 A), a branch-circuit list and a breaker schedule. The Layout tab is the takeoff: photograph your room (optional), place the panel, drag receptacles, switches and fixtures onto the plan, and cable runs route and total in real feet with NEC validation — receptacle spacing, GFCI zones, AFCI coverage — flagging as you go. One honest line up front, which the Code & Permits panel repeats: planning and estimating is the safe DIY layer; pulling wire needs a permit and often a license, and panel interiors and the utility side are professional territory, full stop.

What Moves the Number

  • Service sizeThe NEC 220.82 load calc decides it: 100 A is obsolete for anything all-electric, a heat pump plus induction range plus EV charger pushes most houses to 200 A, and large all-electric builds go 320/400 A. A service upgrade runs $2,500–$4,500 — the utility connection makes it non-negotiable licensed work.
  • Copper cableMid-2026 big-box pricing: 14/2 at $0.55–$0.85 per foot, 12/2 at $0.80–$1.20, 10/2 at $1.50–$2.20, 6/3 for a range at $4–$6. Buying 250 ft rolls saves 25–40% over cut lengths; the measured footage tells you whether a roll clears the job.
  • AFCI and GFCI everywhereNEC 210.12 puts arc-fault protection on nearly every 15/20 A dwelling circuit, and 210.8 wants GFCI in baths, kitchens, garages, laundry and outdoors. Combination breakers are $40–$60 against $8 for a plain one — on a 30-circuit panel the breakers can out-cost the panel itself.
  • Circuit count is set by code, not preferenceNEC 210.52 receptacle spacing means no point along a wall more than 6 ft from an outlet, kitchens need two 20 A small-appliance circuits, baths a dedicated 20 A, laundry its own, plus one per fixed appliance. The sketch flags spacing gaps before drywall does.
  • Open framing versus fishingRough-in wiring in open studs moves at hundreds of feet a day. Old-work in finished walls means fish tape, old-work boxes, and cutting-and-patching — the labor multiplier that makes remodel quotes sting.
  • Panel and device hardwareA 200 A 40-space main-breaker load center is $200–$350; boxes $1–$3; spec-grade receptacles $3–$5; GFCI receptacles $18–$25; dimmers $10–$25. Small numbers that multiply — a typical house carries 80+ devices; the takeoff counts every one from the sketch.

A Real-Numbers Example

A 400 sq ft basement finish — bedroom, rec room, small bath: two 15 A AFCI general circuits, one 20 A GFCI bath circuit, one 20 A workbench circuit.

Cable: a 250 ft roll of 14/2 ($140–$200) covers the general circuits; a 250 ft roll of 12/2 ($200–$280) covers bath and bench with slack. Devices and boxes: 22 boxes at about $2, sixteen receptacles, two GFCIs, six switches, cover plates — $150–$190. Breakers: two AFCI at $50 and one 20 A GFCI combination at $50–$55 — $150–$155. Staples, nail plates, wire nuts and a smoke/CO head — $70.

Materials: about $710–$895. An electrician quotes the same scope at $2,500–$4,000. A realistic middle path: pull a homeowner permit, rough the boxes and cable yourself, and pay a licensed electrician for the panel terminations — the footage and breaker schedule make that a clean handoff.

Where DIYers Go Wrong

  • Opening a live panelFlipping the main breaker off still leaves the service lugs and the line side hot — there is no de-energized state a homeowner can create inside a panel. This is the bright line where DIY ends. Plan and rough the branch wiring; let a licensed electrician land it.
  • Overfilled boxesNEC 314.16 box-fill math is real: each 12 AWG conductor counts 2.25 cubic inches, devices count double, and a crowded switch box fails inspection and cooks its connections. Size boxes up front — the bigger box costs pennies more.
  • Skipping AFCI to save on breakersTen circuits of arc-fault protection is $400–$500 and it hurts, so people quietly install standard breakers. The panel is the first thing a final looks at; swapping breakers later costs the same money plus a re-inspection fee.
  • Ignoring voltage drop on long runsA 20 A circuit on 12/2 to a detached garage 100 ft away drops past the 3% the NEC recommends — tools bog and LEDs flicker. Size up a gauge on runs much past 75–100 ft; the sketch shows the true measured length, always longer than the mental estimate.

Questions People Actually Ask

Is it legal to do my own electrical work?
Many states and cities issue homeowner electrical permits for owner-occupied single-family homes, always with inspections; others, and nearly all rental or multi-family property, require a licensed electrician. The utility side and meter equipment are never DIY. The tool's permit panel gives your state's stance — your local AHJ decides.
What service size do I need?
Run the load calc: NEC 220.82 takes 100% of the first 10 kVA and 40% of the rest, with HVAC at full value. Most modern houses land on 200 A; adding a heat pump, induction range and EV charging is what pushes past it. The sidebar computes this live as you toggle loads.
How accurate is the wire takeoff?
The footage comes from cable runs drawn on your actual room layout, plus a service allowance per drop — not a per-square-foot guess. Prices come from catalog estimates you can overwrite with pasted-in live store pricing, so the total tracks checkout.
Do bedroom outlets really need AFCI breakers?
Yes. Since the 2014 NEC cycle, 210.12 has covered virtually all 120 V 15/20 A circuits in living spaces — bedrooms, living rooms, kitchens, laundry. Combination AFCI breakers at $40–$60 are the standard fix; the breaker schedule includes them wherever required.
Can I wire the circuits and have an electrician connect the panel?
That split is common and often the smart play: you rough boxes and cable on a homeowner permit, the licensed electrician terminates the panel and signs off the hot work. Print the E-1 sheet and materials list — a clean circuit directory is what makes that handoff cheap.
