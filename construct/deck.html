A backyard deck is one of the highest-return DIY projects a homeowner can take on. Built professionally, a 12×16 ft pressure-treated deck runs $8,000–$14,000 in most US markets; the same deck built yourself costs $3,500–$5,500 in materials — the labor you supply is worth roughly 60% of the contractor price. Composite decking (Trex, TimberTech, Fiberon) roughly doubles the decking line item but cuts maintenance to a wash-down, and most boards now carry 25–50 year fade-and-stain warranties.

The hard part of a DIY deck is not the carpentry — it is the takeoff. Undercount joist hangers and you make a second store run mid-pour; overcount lumber and you eat a 15% restocking fee on a full bunk. What Moves the Number

  • Decking materialPressure-treated 5/4×6 runs $2.50–$4 per linear foot; cedar $4–$7; entry composite $5–$8; premium capped composite or PVC $9–$14. On a 192 sq ft deck that swings the decking line from roughly $1,100 to $4,500 before a single joist is bought.
  • Height and foundationA grade-level deck on deck blocks needs no inspection in many jurisdictions. Past ~30 inches of height most codes require frost-depth concrete footings, guardrails, and a permit — footings add $30–$80 per pier in concrete and hardware, plus the digging.
  • Attachment methodA ledger-attached deck saves one beam and a row of posts but demands proper flashing and structural lag/bolt fastening into the rim joist per IRC R507.9. Freestanding costs slightly more in lumber and avoids the most common deck failure point: a rotted ledger connection.
  • Joist size and spacing2×8s at 16 in on center is the workhorse for spans to ~12 ft. Composite decking laid diagonally needs 12 in spacing — a third more joists and hangers. The estimator recalculates the framing count instantly when you change either knob.
  • Stairs and railingEach stair stringer set with treads and hardware adds $150–$400. Code-compliant railing runs $30–$60 per linear foot in wood and $80–$150 in aluminum or composite; on an elevated deck, railing can quietly become 25% of the whole budget.

A Real-Numbers Example

Take the most common build in the tool: a 12×16 ft pressure-treated deck, 3 ft off grade, ledger-attached, 2×8 joists at 16 in OC, one 48-inch stair run, railing on three sides.

Framing: one 2×10 ledger, a doubled 2×10 beam on three 6×6 posts in concrete footings, thirteen 2×8 joists with galvanized hangers, rim and blocking — roughly $700–$900. Decking: 32 courses of 5/4×6×16 ft PT at about $22 a board plus 5% waste — $740. Stairs: three stringers, treads, and hardware — $220. Railing: 36 linear feet of wood post-and-baluster — $1,250. Fasteners, joist tape, flashing, concrete — $450.

Total materials: about $3,400–$3,600 at mid-2026 big-box prices — less if you trim the railing scope or catch lumber on sale. The estimator prices your exact configuration line by line, and every row links to the store search so you can lock in real SKUs before you commit. A contractor quote for the identical deck typically reads $9,500–$12,000 — the spread is your labor, two weekends of it for a first-timer with a helper.

Where DIYers Go Wrong

  • Skipping ledger flashingThe number-one cause of deck collapse is a ledger that rotted the rim joist behind it. Flash the ledger with a proper Z-flashing plus self-adhering membrane; never rely on caulk. IRC R507.2.4 requires corrosion-resistant flashing at the house connection.
  • Setting posts in dirt or shallow concreteFootings must bear below local frost depth (6 in in Florida, 42+ in in Minnesota). A heaved footing racks the whole frame. The permit panel in the tool flags your state's typical frost line, but verify with your inspector.
  • Using interior fasteners outdoorsModern pressure-treat chemistry (copper azole) eats plain and electro-galvanized steel. Everything touching PT lumber must be hot-dip galvanized (G185) or stainless: hangers, bolts, and every screw.
  • Overspanning the joistsA 2×6 at 24 in OC feels fine to walk on the day you build it and bouncy inside a year. Follow the span tables baked into the estimator — they track IRC Table R507.6 — and go one size up if you plan a hot tub corner.
  • Buying decking to length without a cut planA 12×16 deck decked with 16 ft boards has near-zero waste; decked with 12 ft boards across the 16 ft dimension it generates a cut on every course. The estimator's BOM assumes the efficient orientation — match it when you buy.

Questions People Actually Ask

Do I need a permit to build a deck?
In most US jurisdictions, yes — commonly triggered when the deck exceeds 200 sq ft, sits more than 30 inches above grade, or attaches to the house. Grade-level floating decks often fall under an exemption. The estimator's permit panel summarizes your state's rules; your local building department is authoritative.
Is composite decking worth the extra cost?
If you plan to keep the house 7+ years, usually yes. PT decking needs cleaning and re-staining every 2–3 years at $100–$300 in materials per pass, and boards cup and splinter by year 10. Composite's premium buys back that maintenance and holds resale value. Framing is PT lumber either way.
How long does a DIY deck take?
For a first-timer with one helper: a weekend for layout and footings (plus concrete cure time), a weekend for framing, and a third for decking, stairs and railing. Experienced builders compress that by half. Inspections add calendar days — schedule the footing inspection before you pour.
Can the estimator handle a non-rectangular deck?
Yes — the Draw Shape tab accepts any polygon footprint, and Photo Trace lets you trace the real shape of your yard from a satellite image. Framing, decking and railing quantities all follow the drawn geometry.
What is the strongest way to attach a deck to a house?
Through-bolts or structural screws (LedgerLOK, SDWS) into the rim joist per IRC R507.9.1.3 fastener schedules, over continuous flashing. Never lag into siding alone, brick veneer, or cantilevered floor overhangs — those cases call for a freestanding deck, which the estimator prices with one toggle.
