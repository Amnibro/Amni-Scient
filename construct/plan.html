New single-family construction averages $160–$220 per square foot contractor-built in 2026, with coastal metros running well past that. Owner-builders who act as their own general contractor typically keep the 15–25% GC markup, and materials run roughly $90–$130 per square foot of the finished house. But the per-square-foot number is where budgets go to die — a house is not one number, it is a stack of trades that each move independently. A plan heavy in bathrooms breaks the average; a simple rectangle beats a rambling footprint of identical area; and none of that shows up until you price the trades separately.

Amni-Plan is the front door of the Amni-Construct suite, built for that decomposition. List your rooms in the sidebar — name, size, type — and it auto-arranges a floor plan, builds a 3D massing you can drag rooms around in, and keeps a live room schedule: total area, bed and bath counts, footprint and perimeter. The Trade Rollup tab then hands every module its numbers in one click — flooring gets each room's area, roofing gets the footprint, plumbing gets the fixture load your baths and kitchen imply, electrical gets square footage and bedrooms, HVAC gets conditioned area. One honest boundary, stated in the tool itself: this is a schematic layout for totaling the house and driving estimates, not a construction floor plan — adjacencies, hallways, stairs and egress belong to a designer.

What Drives the Cost

  • Area versus perimeterCost scales with square footage, but foundation, siding, and roof edge scale with perimeter and footprint. A sprawling single-story costs meaningfully more per square foot than the same area stacked on two floors. Drag rooms in the 3D massing and watch footprint and perimeter change in the schedule.
  • Wet rooms are the expensive roomsKitchens and bathrooms run $250–$600 per square foot finished against $100–$150 for bedrooms — each full bath is $3,000–$8,000 in materials alone once rough plumbing, fixtures, tile and ventilation are counted. Two plans of identical total area can differ by tens of thousands purely on bath and kitchen count.
  • Region, site and soft costsLabor and permit costs vary 2× between US metros, and the lot itself bites first: clearing, utility connections, septic or well can absorb $10,000–$50,000 before the slab is formed. Permits typically run 1–3% of construction value — the Code & Permits panel gives your state's starting point.
  • How much you self-performSelf-performing finish trades — flooring, tile, paint, trim — is the classic 10–20% saving with low risk. Acting as your own GC saves the markup but costs a year of scheduling subs. Structural work, electrical service, gas and refrigerant are licensed territory nearly everywhere; the trade modules each carry their own permit guidance for the line between.

A Real-Numbers Example

Sketch an 1,800 sq ft single-story — three bedrooms, two baths, kitchen, living, dining, laundry, two-car garage — and let the rollup feed the trade modules.

Framing package (lumber, sheathing, hardware at 2026 prices): $28,000–$45,000. Roof over house and garage, about 26 squares of architectural shingle with underlayment and edge metal: $4,500–$7,000. Plumbing materials for two baths, kitchen, laundry and a water heater: $4,000–$8,000 with mid-range fixtures. Electrical at 200 A with today's copper: $5,000–$9,000. A 3-ton ducted HVAC system, equipment plus ductwork: $6,000–$10,000. Flooring at mid-range LVP and tile: $7,000–$11,000.

Those six lines — the ones this suite estimates directly — total roughly $55,000–$90,000. Foundation, windows, siding, drywall, insulation and finish carry the rest toward the typical $90–$130 per square foot materials figure, and the same house contractor-built at $180 per square foot reads about $324,000. The point of the rollup is that each line came from your actual rooms, and each one opens in its own module for a real takeoff instead of a ratio.

Where DIYers Go Wrong

  • Budgeting on the average, building the exceptionPer-square-foot averages assume a normal mix of rooms. Add a third bath, a bigger kitchen, or tall ceilings and the average quietly stops applying — usually 10–20% into the build. Pricing trade by trade from the rollup is the antidote — wet rooms and spans get counted where they are.
  • Mistaking the schematic for a construction planThe auto-arranged layout totals areas and adjacencies; it does not reason about hallways, stair geometry, or bedroom egress windows. The tool says this about itself in the rollup tab. Use it to size and budget, then pay for design — the design fee is a rounding error against framing a bad plan.
  • Forgetting everything that is not a tradeSite work, utility connections, permit fees, builder's-risk insurance, tool rental and a 10–15% contingency never appear on a materials list, and together they are commonly 15–25% of the project. Carry them as explicit lines next to the rollup total, not as hope.
  • Scattering the wet roomsPlumbing cost tracks pipe run length and stack count more than fixture count. A bath at each far corner of the plan means long DWV runs, extra vents and more holes in the frame. Rearrange rooms in the massing view and re-open the plumb module — the difference shows up in feet of pipe immediately.

Questions People Actually Ask

Is this a real floor plan I could build from?
No, and it says so on the sheet: it is a schematic room layout that totals the house and pre-fills the trade estimators. A buildable construction set needs a designer or engineer for structure, egress, hallway and stair logic, and local energy code. Budget first here, then design.
What exactly does the Trade Rollup pass to the other tools?
Room areas go to flooring; footprint and perimeter to roofing and framing; bed and bath counts drive the plumbing fixture load and the electrical load calc; conditioned area feeds HVAC. Each module opens with those numbers pre-filled and fully editable, so a change to the plan re-flows the whole estimate.
How much of a house build can I actually DIY?
Estimating and planning: all of it, and that is this suite's stance. Physically: finish trades are commonly self-performed, framing is realistic with help and inspections, while electrical service, gas piping and refrigerant work are licensed territory in most jurisdictions. Each trade module's permit panel covers where that line sits in your state.
What does it cost to build a house per square foot?
In 2026, contractor-built typically lands at $160–$220 per square foot with wide regional spread, and owner-builder materials run about $90–$130. But the honest answer is the trade-by-trade one: two houses of equal size diverge by tens of thousands on baths, footprint shape and finish level — exactly what building the estimate from rooms exposes.
