Where the Money Goes

  • Pipe materialHalf-inch PEX-A runs $0.50–$1.00 per foot and 3/4 in about $1–$1.50; type L copper is roughly triple that and demands soldering or a press tool; CPVC sits between. DWV is PVC almost everywhere: about $1.50–$2.50 per foot for 2 in, $3–$6 for 3 and 4 in.
  • Fixture count and WSFUEvery fixture carries a water supply fixture unit value per IPC Appendix E; the total converts to peak-demand GPM, which sets the main and trunk sizes. A typical 2.5-bath house lands around 20–25 WSFU — a 1 in main, 3/4 in trunks, 1/2 in branch runs. The tool recomputes the math as you drop fixtures.
  • Drain, waste and ventDrainage sizes off DFU counts — a toilet alone is 3–4 DFU and forces a 3 in branch minimum. Every trap needs a vent within its trap-arm limit (roughly 42 in for a 1-1/2 in arm, 5 ft for 2 in under the IPC). Venting is where DIY layouts fail plan review; the sketch flags an unvented or over-long arm as you draw.
  • Open studs versus finished wallsRough-in with walls open is the easy case. Fishing supply and drain through finished walls multiplies labor 2–4× and adds drywall repair. The sketch photo underlay helps you find the shortest real path.
  • Water heaterA 50-gallon electric tank is $550–$900, gas $700–$1,200, and a condensing tankless unit $600–$1,500 plus venting — and gas models often force a larger gas line, which is licensed work. A sidebar toggle adds it all to the takeoff.

What a Typical Project Costs

Take the classic project: a full bathroom addition rough-in — toilet, lavatory, tub/shower — tying into an existing 3 in stack about 20 ft away, in PEX-A.

Supply: roughly 60 ft of 1/2 in and 3/4 in PEX-A at $0.50–$1.20 per foot — $55–$75; expansion fittings, rings and drop-ear elbows — $70–$95; quarter-turn stops and braided supply lines — $40. DWV: 20 ft of 3 in PVC for the toilet run $70, about 50 ft of 2 in and 1-1/2 in branch and vent $90, then the fitting pile — wyes, sanitary tees, closet flange, P-traps, cleanout — $110–$130. Strapping, nail plates, primer and cement add $40.

Rough-in materials total: about $475–$550. A one-day PEX expansion tool rental is $50–$70 (buying a Milwaukee M12 ProPEX runs $300–$400 and pays for itself on a repipe). Finish fixtures — toilet, tub, vanity, faucets — add $700–$1,700. The identical rough-in quotes $2,500–$4,500 from a plumber, and the estimator's materials table gets you within a few percent.

Mistakes That Cost Real Money

  • Building an accidental S-trapA fixture drain dropping straight down past its trap with no vent siphons the trap dry and lets sewer gas in. Every trap needs a vent connection within its trap-arm distance — the sketch flags it, and inspectors always check.
  • Getting the slope wrongDrains 2-1/2 in and smaller need 1/4 in per foot of fall; 3 in and larger can run 1/8 in per foot. Below minimum, solids strand and clog. Check the fall across your longest run against the ceiling space you have before cutting.
  • Mixing metals without a dielectric unionCopper threaded straight onto galvanized steel sets up galvanic corrosion that eats the joint within a few years. Use dielectric unions or a brass fitting between dissimilar metals, especially at the water heater.
  • Skipping nail platesAny pipe within 1-1/4 in of a stud or plate face needs a steel protection plate. A trim nail through PEX leaks in month six behind finished drywall, not on day one. Plates cost under a dollar each; the takeoff includes them.
  • Buying fittings for the wrong PEX systemPEX-A expansion fittings (ASTM F1960) and PEX-B crimp fittings (F1807) are not interchangeable. Pick one system, set it in the tool, and buy off a single list.

FAQ

Can I legally do my own plumbing?
In many US jurisdictions, yes — homeowner permits are issued for owner-occupied single-family homes, with inspections. Rentals, condos and some cities require a licensed plumber, and gas piping is licensed work almost everywhere. The permit panel summarizes your state; your building department confirms.
PEX or copper?
PEX-A dominates 2026 new construction: a third the material cost, freeze-tolerant, far fewer fittings buried in walls, and 25-year warranties. Copper still wins for exposed runs, UV exposure, and buyers who expect it. Most sensible answer: PEX in the walls, copper stubs where it shows — the tool prices both.
What size water main does my house need?
It comes out of the WSFU math: total the fixture units, convert to peak GPM, and size for available pressure and run length. Most 2–3 bath houses land on a 1 in main with 3/4 in trunks. The estimator runs this live from your sketched fixtures per IPC Appendix E.
Does the tool handle venting?
Yes — every fixture is checked for a vent path and trap-arm distance as you draw, and the warnings panel calls out anything unvented. Air admittance valves can substitute where your jurisdiction allows them (not all do) — confirm in the permit panel.
What is a P-1 riser diagram and do I need one?
It is the schematic sheet showing supply and DWV piping with sizes — many plan reviewers require one with a plumbing permit application. The estimator generates it from your sketch in the 2D Plans tab.
