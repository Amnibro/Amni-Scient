Everyone knows the framing rule of thumb — one stud per foot of wall covers 16-in-on-center spacing plus extras — and it is close enough for a napkin and useless for a lumber order. The real count depends on corners, wall intersections, and above all openings: every door and window consumes king studs, jack studs, cripples and a header. At mid-2026 prices a 2×4×92-5/8 precut stud runs $3.25–$4.50 and 7/16 OSB $16–$24 a sheet — and lumber moves enough that a re-priceable takeoff beats any stale per-square-foot figure.

The free Amni-Frame estimator builds that takeoff from geometry: enter a footprint (or trace one in Photo Trace — the perimeter becomes the exterior wall run), set wall height, stud spacing, and the number and width of doors and windows, and it counts studs (corners and opening framing included), plates, headers, OSB sheathing, house wrap, anchor bolts and nails — priced against Home Depot and Lowe's, with a printable FR-1 framing elevation that shows the header/king/jack/cripple layout at each opening. One honest boundary up front: a shed, detached garage or non-bearing partition is fair DIY ground; a wall holding up a roof, or removing one that might be, is permit-and-engineer territory.

What Moves the Number

  • Stud spacing and size16 in OC is the default; 24 in OC is code-legal in many configurations and cuts the stud count by roughly a third. 2×6 walls cost about 60–70% more per stick than 2×4 but carry R-21 insulation instead of R-13–15 — the energy code, not the framing budget, often makes that call on exterior walls.
  • OpeningsEach opening adds two kings, two jacks, cripples above (and below windows), and a header. In a bearing wall the header is sized by span from IRC R602.7 tables; in a non-bearing partition a flat 2×4 suffices. That is why the estimator asks for counts and widths.
  • Plates and the concrete connectionBudget three plate runs per wall with a double top plate — 3× the perimeter in plate stock. Bottom plates on concrete must be pressure-treated (IRC R317.1) and anchored with 1/2-in bolts at 6 ft OC maximum, one within 12 in of each plate end, per R403.1.6.
  • Sheathing and wrap7/16 OSB at $16–$24 per 4×8 sheet, one sheet per 32 sq ft of wall; wall bracing per IRC R602.10 is what actually dictates where structural panels must land. House wrap adds $0.15–$0.25 per sq ft plus tape.

Worked Example

Frame the estimator's default shell: 40×30 ft, 140 lineal feet of 8-ft exterior wall at 16 in OC, two 3-ft doors, six 3-ft windows, double top plate, sheathed and wrapped.

The count comes to about 150 precut studs once corners and opening framing are in — $540 at $3.60 each. Plates: 140 lf of pressure-treated bottom plate $110 and 280 lf of SPF top plate $130. Eight doubled-2×10 headers $130. Sheathing the 1,120 sq ft of wall takes 35 sheets of 7/16 OSB at $19 — $665. House wrap and seam tape $225; anchor bolts, 16d framing nails and sheathing nails $190.

Total: about $2,000–$2,200 in mid-2026 materials for the complete framed and sheathed wall shell. Framing labor typically runs 2–3× the material cost when a crew does it; either way, the CSV export is the exact list a lumberyard needs to quote the package.

What Goes Wrong (and How to Avoid It)

  • Treating a bearing wall like a partitionJoists running perpendicular to a wall, walls stacked floor over floor, anything under a ridge or girder — assume bearing until proven otherwise. Opening or removing a bearing wall needs an engineered beam and a permit. The estimator prices lumber; it does not make a wall non-structural.
  • Ignoring crownEvery stud has a bow. Sight down each one and frame with crowns facing the same direction, and cull the worst sticks for blocking and cripples. A wall with random crowns telegraphs waves through the drywall that no mud coat fixes.
  • Untreated lumber on concreteA standard SPF plate on a slab wicks moisture and rots from below — bottom plates on masonry must be pressure-treated. Use hot-dip galvanized nails and hardware wherever wood is treated; the chemistry corrodes plain steel.
  • Forgetting fire blockingPlatform framing seals plate lines, but furred spaces, soffits, drop ceilings and any concealed vertical-to-horizontal transition need blocking per IRC R302.11 — and inspectors look for it. Blocks cut from stud culls cost nothing during framing; retrofitting after drywall costs a tear-open.
  • Buying 8-ft studs for an 8-ft wallA 92-5/8-in precut plus three plates makes standard 8-ft-1-in ceiling height — no cutting. Buying 96-in studs means a saw cut on every stick and often costs more per piece. Order precuts for 8, 9 and 10 ft walls; save full-length stock for plates and blocking.

Homeowner FAQ

How many studs do I need per foot of wall?
The one-per-foot rule approximates 16 in OC plus corners and waste, but openings break it in both directions — kings and jacks add sticks while the opening itself removes field studs. The estimator counts the actual layout: spacing, corners, intersections and every opening's framing — the number to give the lumberyard.
Is 24-inch stud spacing okay?
Structurally yes in many cases — IRC Table R602.3(5) allows 24 in OC for 2×6 walls and for 2×4 in limited one-story conditions — and it saves roughly 30% of the studs. The tradeoffs are practical: 1/2-in drywall bows slightly between 24-in centers and siding nailing gets sparser. Switch the spacing in the sidebar and let the totals argue it out.
What size header do I need over a door or window?
In a non-bearing wall, a flat 2×4 with cripples is enough. In a bearing wall the header is sized by span and load from IRC R602.7 — a doubled 2×10 handles common 3–4 ft openings, while garage doors and wide patio sliders usually call for LVL. The estimator prices doubled headers per opening; verify sizing against the tables or an engineer for anything bearing.
Do I need a permit to frame a wall?
New structures, additions, garages and anything structural: yes, everywhere in the US. A non-bearing interior partition is often exempt below a cost threshold — but running new electrical inside it triggers a permit anyway. The Code & Permits panel summarizes your state and city; call the building department before the first saw cut.
What does plumb and line actually mean?
After raising, corners get plumbed with a level, then the top plate is pulled straight to a string line and held with temporary braces. It is the least glamorous hour of framing and the one that decides whether cabinets, doors and drywall go in easily; the Build Guide slots it before sheathing.
