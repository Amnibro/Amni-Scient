HOSTILITY AGAINST HOUSES OF WORSHIP

OPEN-SOURCE INCIDENTS AND FBI BIAS COUNTS, 2018–2024

Three published series, kept separate: Family Research Council incidents against churches, FB-ISAO attacks on houses of worship of any faith, and FBI crimes reported as religious-bias hate crimes.

The 2024 church file has 415 incidents at 383 churches in 43 states and the District of Columbia. The revised annual series sums to 1,384 incidents from January 2018 through December 2024, and to 1,196 from 2021 through 2024. Category marks in 2024 sum to 428 because 13 incidents sit in two categories.

What the sources record

Perpetrator religion, political party, medication, and immigration status are absent from the Family Research Council incident file, from FBI Table 1, and from the FB-ISAO year-in-review summary. The state charts use the 2024 resident population, the 2023 poverty rate, and the 2023 foreign-born share. Those are area-level associations. They do not identify a cause. The foreign-born share is a stock of residents, not an immigration rate in the weeks before an incident.

Churches, by year

Family Research Council revised the 2020–2023 totals upward in the August 2025 edition after more open-source incidents were identified. The bars use the revised series: 50, 83, 55, 98, 198, 485, 415. The count is incidents found in news and public reports. It is not a census of offenses, and underreporting is expected.

2024 church incidents by type

Vandalism is 284 of 415 incidents. Arson, including attempts and fires of unknown cause, is 55. Gun-related incidents are 28, up from 12 in 2023. Bomb threats are 14. Other aggression is 47. Thirteen incidents carry two marks, so the five marks sum to 428.

Each bar is a month of 2024. The tallest bar is June.

June is the high month in this file. September through November are the low months. About 22% of the June incidents were described as related to LGBT issues in the Family Research Council narrative. That share is not a column in the spreadsheet.

States

Larger states contribute more incidents. The correlation of the incident count with the log of the July 1, 2024 population is __R_LOGPOP__ across 50 states and the District of Columbia, with seven jurisdictions at zero. Hawaii, Idaho, Montana, Nevada, New Mexico, Wisconsin, and Wyoming have no row in the 2024 file.

Reporting effort, media coverage, and the open-source search also vary by state. None of these coefficients is a cause, and none of them is a perpetrator’s immigration status.

Identified themes in the church file

Most 2024 incidents have an unknown perpetrator and no confirmed theme. The counts below are the themes Family Research Council published. They are not a partition of the 415, and a 2022 anti-LGBTQ+ count is not in the 2025 narrative.

Houses of worship, any faith

FB-ISAO counted 841 open-source attacks on U.S. houses of worship in 2024, down from 1,027 in 2023. Bomb threats fell from 214 to 86 and account for most of that decline. In 2024, 49% of attacks were on Protestant churches (415 in the appendix), 21% on Jewish congregations, 16% on Catholic churches, and 7% on Muslim congregations. Hindu facilities rose from 2 to 14. FB-ISAO compares those shares with a 2022 Duke count of the house-of-worship stock: 85% Protestant, 6% Catholic, 3.2% Jewish, and 0.7% Muslim.

FBI religious-bias hate crimes

These are offenses that participating agencies submitted as motivated by religious bias. A victim can be a person, a religious organization, or the public. The series is not a count of attacks on buildings, and agency participation is voluntary. In 2024, 16,419 agencies covered 95.1% of the population and reported 2,783 single-bias religious incidents.

Bar length is the 2024 incident count for that bias motivation.

Who FB-ISAO could classify

In 62% of the 841 house-of-worship attacks, no perpetrator is identified. Among the identified 38%, FB-ISAO reports about 36% ideological or political, 10% mental illness, 9% homelessness, and 9% insiders (26 incidents). Categories can overlap. Mental illness in that summary is not a medication record.
