HOSTILITY AGAINST HOUSES OF WORSHIP

FBI COUNTS THROUGH OCTOBER 2026

The FBI file refreshed on November 15, 2026 runs through October. FB-ISAO’s latest attack total is still calendar 2025. Family Research Council’s church file still ends in 2024.

From January through October 2026 the FBI file holds 1,932 single-bias religious incidents, 1,071 of them anti-Jewish. The same ten months of 2025 hold 2,057 and 1,311. Those 2026 months are still being filed: population coverage is 91% in January and 56% in October. The last complete FBI year is 2025, with 2,408 religious-bias incidents. FB-ISAO’s latest attack total is 915 in 2025. Family Research Council’s latest church file is still calendar 2024, with 415 incidents at 383 churches. Neither has published a 2026 total.

What the sources record

Perpetrator religion, political party, medication, and immigration status are absent from the Family Research Council incident file, from the FBI religion tables, and from both FB-ISAO year-in-review summaries. The state charts stay on the 2024 church file and use the same calendar year for every covariate: the July 1, 2024 population, the 2024 poverty rate, the 2024 foreign-born share, and the 2024 presidential popular-vote share. Those are properties of the place, not of the person. The foreign-born share is a stock of residents, not an immigration rate in the weeks before an incident. The vote share is the locality’s political affiliation. It is not the perpetrator’s party.

Churches, by year

Family Research Council revised the 2020–2023 totals upward in the August 2025 edition after more open-source incidents were identified. The bars use the revised series: 50, 83, 55, 98, 198, 485, 415. That edition is still the latest church report. The count is incidents found in news and public reports. It is not a census of offenses, and underreporting is expected. The revised series sums to 1,384 incidents from January 2018 through December 2024, and to 1,196 from 2021 through 2024.

2024 church incidents by type

Vandalism is 284 of 415 incidents. Arson, including attempts and fires of unknown cause, is 55. Gun-related incidents are 28, up from 12 in 2023. Bomb threats are 14. Other aggression is 47. Thirteen incidents carry two marks, so the five marks sum to 428.

Each bar is a month of 2024. The tallest bar is June.

June is the high month in this file. September through November are the low months. About 22% of the June incidents were described as related to LGBT issues in the Family Research Council narrative. That share is not a column in the spreadsheet.

Per 100,000 residents, the correlation with the 2024 poverty rate is __R_POV_RATE__, with the 2024 foreign-born share __R_FB_RATE__, and with Harris’s 2024 share of the vote __R_DEM_RATE__. The raw incident count correlates __R_POV_COUNT__ with poverty, __R_FB_COUNT__ with the foreign-born share, and __R_DEM_COUNT__ with the Harris share. The count association with foreign-born share changes sign once population is in the denominator. The vote association is near zero for the count and slightly negative for the rate. Poverty is the strongest of the three rate associations, and it is still weak. Reporting effort, media coverage, and the open-source search also vary by state. None of these coefficients is a cause, and none of them is an attribute of the person who committed the incident.

Two further splits sit in the published national series and disappear when the series are added together. FBI religious-bias incidents are mostly anti-Jewish offenses, and a victim there can be a person, an organization, or the public. FB-ISAO’s building attacks are mostly Protestant churches, where the 2025 review describes theft as the leading motive, and describes hate or bias as the stronger factor at synagogues, mosques, and Hindu temples. In the 2025 FBI offender-race tally, the Black share of records is 32% for anti-Sikh bias and 11% for anti-Jewish bias. That is a difference in the race agencies recorded. It is not a party, a religion, or a cause.

Identified themes in the church file

Most 2024 incidents have an unknown perpetrator and no confirmed theme. The counts below are the themes Family Research Council published. They are not a partition of the 415, and a 2022 anti-LGBTQ+ count is not in the 2025 narrative.

Houses of worship, any faith

FB-ISAO’s 2025 review counts 915 attacks on U.S. houses of worship, an 8.8% increase from 841 in 2024 and below the 1,030 it now prints for 2023. The 2024 review had printed 1,027 for 2023. Incidents with casualties rose from 69 in 2024 to 111 in 2025, with 48 people killed and 154 wounded. In 2025, 63% of attacks were at Protestant churches, 14% at Catholic churches, 12% at synagogues, and 5% at mosques. Latter-day Saint meetinghouses were 2%. Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, Quaker, and Orthodox sites were each under 2%, so this chart does not draw them as a fifth bar. The stock comparison is the 2021 Duke National Congregations Study share that FB-ISAO cites: 85% Protestant, 6% Catholic, 3.2% Jewish, and under 1% Muslim, printed here as 0.7%.

Each bar is FB-ISAO’s later count of attacks. The 2025 review prints 1,030 for 2023; the 2024 review printed 1,027.

Each bar is the 2025 attack share divided by the congregation share. A value of 1 means the faith was attacked in proportion to its share of houses of worship. Protestant churches are 63% of attacks and 85% of the stock, so the ratio is 0.74. Muslim sites are 5% of attacks and 0.7% of the stock, so the ratio is 7.14. The ratio is a comparison of two published shares. It is not a rate per building, and it is not a cause.

FBI religious-bias hate crimes

These are incidents that participating agencies submitted as motivated by religious bias. A victim can be a person, a religious organization, or the public. The series is not a count of attacks on buildings, and agency participation is voluntary. The FBI released the 2025 file on August 14, 2026: 10,881 hate-crime incidents in all, from 16,791 agencies covering about 95% of the population. The religious rows below sum to 2,408.

Motives in 2025, and who could be classified in 2024

The 2025 review prints two motive shares for its 915 attacks: hate or bias 32%, and criminal 36%. Mischief, interpersonal or doctrinal disputes, accidents, and single-issue activity make up the rest, and those remaining shares are not printed separately. About 70% of the motive calls rest on direct evidence, a confession, or a later investigation. About 30% are inferred from the incident. Hate or bias is described as the stronger factor at synagogues, mosques, and Hindu temples. Theft is described as the leading motive at Protestant churches. Party, religion, medication, and immigration status of the person who did it are still not fields in the review.

Other published counts

These series are not the FBI file and not the church spreadsheet. They use different rules, so they are not added to the charts above.

The Anti-Defamation League’s 2025 audit counts antisemitic incidents of harassment, vandalism, and assault, including incidents that are not crimes and incidents that are not at a building. The 2025 total is 6,274, down from 9,354 in 2024. Incidents at Jewish institutions are 1,129, down from 1,702. Bomb threats against Jewish institutions are 59 in 2025, 627 in 2024, and 996 in 2023. Assaults with a deadly weapon rose from 23 in 2024 to 32 in 2025. Israel- or Zionism-related incidents are 2,847 (45%) in 2025 and 5,452 (58%) in 2024. Earlier annual totals are not drawn, because this page does not carry a complete 2018–2023 ADL table.

Who the FBI recorded, calendar 2025

The Crime Data Explorer publishes the race of known offenders for each bias. The categories are the race the agency recorded. Unknown is a category. The bias-specific tally includes multiple-bias incidents that include that motivation, so the number of offender records is not the single-bias incident count. For 2025 the records are 932 anti-Jewish, 231 across the six Christian rows, 215 anti-Islamic, 152 anti-Sikh, and 34 anti-Hindu. Party, religion, medication, and immigration status are still not in this file. The chart is the percent of each bias’s offender-race records.

White, Black, and Unknown are the three largest published categories. Other is Asian, American Indian or Alaska Native, Native Hawaiian or Other Pacific Islander, and Multiple, added together. Anti-Sikh records are 32% Black. Anti-Jewish records are 11% Black and 37% Unknown.
