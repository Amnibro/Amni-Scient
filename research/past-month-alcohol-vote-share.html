Past-month alcohol use and 2024 vote share

Ethanol · NSDUH past-month use · area-level association

Past-month alcohol use, ages 12 and older, joined to the 2024 Democratic two-party vote in the same regional regression as the marijuana note. The coefficient is an area-level residual association. It is not an individual-level effect of drinking on the probability of voting for one party.

Data and method

Region CSV Results JSON Regression script Full method

Method. NSDUH 2021–2023 substate small-area estimates of past-month alcohol use, ages 12 or older (SAMHSA shapefile attribute ALCMON; column alc in the region file), joined to 2024 presidential votes reallocated to ZCTAs by the land-cover method (Harvard Dataverse 10.7910/DVN/Z8TSH3), with census age structure and density as controls. Vote-weighted least squares, HC1 standard errors. The specified alcohol model is alc, log density, percent ages 18–25, and percent ages 12–17. The joint model adds past-month marijuana use (mj, from MRJMON). Keys in results.json: region_alcohol, region_mj_and_alcohol, and the ZCTA fits with standard errors clustered by substate region, zcta_alcohol_cluster and zcta_mj_and_alcohol_cluster.

The marijuana note, including the suggestibility and IQ curves, is separate: Eighteen points: past-month marijuana use, 2024 vote share, suggestibility, and IQ.

Reffelt, A. (2026). Past-month alcohol use and 2024 Democratic two-party vote share. AMNI-SCIENT Research. https://amni-scient.com/research/past-month-alcohol-vote-share

The alcohol coefficient

Past-month alcohol, in the same density and age specification, has a coefficient of 0.66 vote-share points per percentage point of alcohol use (SE 0.09). Alcohol’s regional spread is wider (weighted SD 6.1 points versus 4.0 for marijuana), so the two standardized associations are similar in size. Entered together, marijuana is 0.96 (SE 0.13) and alcohol is 0.49 (SE 0.08).

Coefficients are percentage points of Democratic two-party share per 1 point of past-month use. The alcohol-only row is the specified model. The second row is the joint model. Both are the region-level, vote-weighted least squares fits. N = 382 regions, covering 30,389 ZCTAs and 150.1 million allocated two-party votes. Weights are two-party votes. Standard errors are HC1.

Vote-weighted correlations with Democratic two-party share: log density 0.73, bachelor’s share 0.71, past-month alcohol 0.48, past-month marijuana 0.42.

Intervals, p-values, weighted R², the control-term coefficients, and the ZCTA-level fits with standard errors clustered by substate region are in results.json under the keys named above. They are not restated here. The region-level regression is the preferred one. The use rate does not vary inside a substate region, so a ZCTA regression repeats the same exposure thousands of times. Clustering is required at that level because every ZCTA in a region is assigned that region’s single use rate.

What was joined

This is the alcohol specification of the same join as the marijuana note.

  1. NSDUH 2021–2023 substate small-area estimates of past-month alcohol use, ages 12 or older, from the SAMHSA shapefile attribute ALCMON (393 mapped regions). The specified model uses the 12+ rate, with age structure as covariates. Marijuana in the joint model is MRJMON from the same file.
  2. 2024 presidential votes reallocated to ZCTAs by the land-cover method (Harvard Dataverse file election-results-zcta-2024-landcover-method, DOI 10.7910/DVN/Z8TSH3). Democratic two-party share is Democratic votes divided by Democratic plus Republican votes. The areal-method file is the sensitivity check on the marijuana specification; that check is reported on the marijuana page.
  3. ZCTA internal points from the Census 2020 Gazetteer, used to assign each ZCTA to the substate polygon that contains the point.
  4. ACS 2019–2023 table-based summary file, tables B01001 (age) and B15003 (education), ZCTA rows only. Density is ZCTA population divided by land area from the vote file, then summed to the region before taking the log. Ages 12–14 are approximated as three-fifths of the 10–14 band, and age 25 as one-fifth of the 25–29 band. Replacing those with the exact bands 15–17 and 18–24 does not change the marijuana coefficient. That age-band check is the marijuana specification.

Coverage

The 2024 ZCTA vote file used here has no Alaska or Hawaii rows (highest ZCTA is 99403). Nebraska’s six mapped substate regions are in the NSDUH tables but not in the SAMHSA shapefile that was joined, so Nebraska’s ZCTAs (about 0.6% of two-party votes in the file) are omitted. The estimation sample is 48 jurisdictions: 47 states plus the District of Columbia.

Limits of the coefficient

  • The unit is the substate region. The regression describes co-occurrence of area-level use and area-level vote share after density and age. It does not measure how one person’s vote probability changes with residual cognitive scores, psychosis, or suggestibility.
  • No public file links those individual outcomes to the same person’s ballot. That coefficient was not estimated.
  • NSDUH substate rates are small-area estimates with their own uncertainty. Classical measurement error in the use rate pulls the slope toward zero. No errors-in-variables correction was applied.
  • State indicators absorb legalization status and other statewide factors on the marijuana specification. That within-state result is on the marijuana note. The alcohol models in results.json are the density-and-age specification and the joint model with marijuana.
  • Density and educational attainment correlate more strongly with the 2024 Democratic share than past-month alcohol does (0.73 and 0.71, against 0.48). The alcohol coefficient that remains after density and age is still the residual association of the use rate, shared with whatever else varies with use inside these regions.

Vote join: NSDUH 2021–2023 substate small-area estimates (SAMHSA) and 2024 presidential votes reallocated to ZCTAs by land cover (Harvard Dataverse 10.7910/DVN/Z8TSH3), with age and education from the ACS 2019–2023 5-year file. Alaska and Hawaii are absent from the vote file. Nebraska is absent from the SAMHSA shapefile, about 0.6% of votes in the file.

Data: region_level.csv (column alc), results.json, run_regression.py, method note. Marijuana, suggestibility, and IQ: eighteen points.
