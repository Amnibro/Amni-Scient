NAVIER–STOKES: A COHERENCE PATH

A PROPOSED ROUTE TO THE CLAY PROBLEM, WITH THE OPEN PIECE NAMED

Every static inequality is provably blind to the blowup question. The proof has to be dynamical, scale-covariant, and it lives on one object: how coherently vorticity points where it is being stretched.

Clay (A)–(D): open Component lemmas: 3 peer-closed Braid ceiling: conditional Residue: 1 object, 4 equivalent forms Program: July 2026, ongoing

WHY THIS IS PUBLIC NOW

I'm Anthony Reffelt. I ran this program through the summer of 2026 with two AI systems working adversarially against each other and against me: one proposing, one attacking, every numerical claim pre-registered before the run, every retraction kept on the record. What follows is the state of that work as of this posting. It is not a proof of the Millennium problem, and every page of the underlying ledger says so in its header.

The unforced smooth-data question, A and B, stays open on its own terms, and that is the question this page is about. Until a proof stands full peer review the door is still open, and I'd rather have my route on the table, with its gap stated in plain language, than sit on it. WHAT CLAY ACTUALLY ASKS

The official statement (Fefferman) has four parts. Either show that every smooth, divergence-free, finite-energy initial velocity on \(\mathbb{R}^3\) or \(\mathbb{T}^3\) gives a smooth solution for all time with zero external force (statements A and B), or exhibit one smooth datum together with a smooth, bounded-energy external force \(f(x,t)\) for which the solution breaks down in finite time (C and D). The forcing term in C and D is part of the official text. This program targets A and B, the unforced regularity question, and its blowup lane searched only the unforced case. The working criterion everyone uses is Beale–Kato–Majda: the solution stays smooth exactly as long as

BKM criterion
\[ \int_0^T \|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}\,dt < \infty , \qquad \omega=\nabla\times u . \]

So the whole problem is whether vorticity can become unbounded in finite time. Vorticity grows by stretching, \(\dot{\omega} = S\omega + \nu\Delta\omega\), where \(S\) is the strain. Stretching needs the vorticity vector to point along a stretching direction of \(S\). That alignment is the object this program is built around.

THE PROPOSAL IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Claim of the route. The known blowup mechanism for 3D Euler (Luo–Hou, Chen–Hou, Elgindi) is low-regularity, needs symmetry, and is inviscid; constant viscosity defeats it by a scaling instability (Hou 2024, reproduced independently here). What survives as the genuine open core is the smooth, all-data, possibly non-self-similar case. Three lemmas closed in this program say where that core lives: no static inequality can decide it (GEOM-COMPLETE), the argument must be dynamical (SHADOW), and constant prefactors cannot beat the cubic nonlinearity, so the closing mechanism must be scale-covariant. Put together, the only place a proof can live is a bound on direction coherence of vorticity on the set where it is actively stretched. I give that bound in four equivalent forms below. Any one of them, proved, gives Clay A.

WHAT IS CLOSED

These are component results with complete proofs, each attacked by the second agent under a written protocol before being marked closed. None of them is a regularity statement on its own. Their job is to fence the problem.

LEMMA 1 — FAR-FIELD STRAIN BOUND (PEER-CLOSED)

Let \(\omega\in L^1(\mathbb{R}^3)\) be supported in a ball \(B(x_0,\delta)\) with vorticity mass \(\Gamma=\|\omega\|_{L^1}\), and let \(u\) be the Biot–Savart velocity. Then for every \(x\) with \(d=|x-x_0|\ge 2\delta\),

Explicit constant
\[ |S(x)| \le |\nabla u(x)| \le \frac{6}{\pi}\,\frac{\Gamma}{d^{3}} . \]

Three lines: the kernel derivative bound \(|\nabla K(z)|\le 3/(4\pi|z|^3)\), the support geometry \(|x-y|\ge d/2\), and the integral. No regularity beyond \(L^1\), no smallness. Sharp constant is \(4/\pi\). This closes the far half of the near/far split; the near zone is the whole problem and this lemma says nothing about it.

LEMMA 2 — GEOMETRY COMPLETENESS OF THE ENERGY CLASS (PEER-CLOSED)

Let \(G(u)(x)=g(u(x),\nabla u(x))\) be any scale-normalized pointwise observable (alignment angles, normalized \(Q\)–\(R\) coordinates, stretching efficiency, pressure–strain correlation). For every kinematically admissible value \(v\) and every \(\varepsilon>0\) there is a smooth, compactly supported, divergence-free unit-energy field that realizes \(G=v\) pointwise on a ball, and one that realizes it in enstrophy-weighted average. Proof: prescribe the germ with a linear field \(A+Mx\), \(\operatorname{tr}M=0\); cut off; repair the divergence in the annulus with the Bogovskiĭ operator; the core is untouched.

Consequence. The energy class forces no alignment statistic whatsoever. Every argument of the form "finite energy implies the vorticity cannot align like X" is empty. This retired a whole family of candidate faces at once.

LEMMA 3 — THE PROOF MUST BE DYNAMICAL (PEER-CLOSED)

A shadow construction: for any static inequality of the admissible type, there is a divergence-free field that saturates it while sitting arbitrarily close to a field that violates its intended conclusion. Any argument that closes the problem has to use the time evolution, not the state.

LEMMA 4 — CONSTANT DEPLETION IS INSUFFICIENT

Suppose stretching is depleted by a constant factor \(\rho^*<1\) everywhere. Ladyzhenskaya plus Young still leaves the enstrophy inequality with the cubic term \(Z^3\); the exponent is immune to the prefactor. So whatever closes the problem has to scale with the solution, not multiply it. This is the reason the route below is stated in scale-covariant form.

LEMMA 5 — THE MOHR DISK

With strain eigenvalues \(\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\lambda_3\), the stretching rate \(\alpha=\xi\cdot S\xi\) and the turning rate \(\tau=|P_\perp S\xi|\) of the vorticity direction \(\xi=\omega/|\omega|\) satisfy

Growth and reorientation share a disk
\[ \tau^{2} \le (\lambda_1-\alpha)(\alpha-\lambda_3). \]

Maximal stretching forbids turning and maximal turning forbids stretching. This is free algebra, but it is the pointwise constraint that every dynamical form of the residue is written against.

LEMMA 6 — THE PRESSURE HESSIAN AS HEAT

From \((-\Delta)^{-1}=\int_0^\infty e^{s\Delta}ds\), exactly

Heat identity
\[ \nabla^2 p = \int_0^\infty \nabla^2 e^{s\Delta}\,Q\;ds, \qquad Q=\operatorname{tr}(\nabla u)^2 . \]

Splitting at the coherence heat-time \(s^*=(\theta/K)^2\) reproduces the far-field lemma from Gaussian bounds and puts the Calderón–Zygmund wall in heat coordinates, where the near-field question becomes a statement about a gain of \(K\sqrt{s}\) on the shear sector.

LEMMA 7 — THE COHERENCE IDENTITY (DERIVATION COMPLETE TO SECOND ORDER)

Any regular vortex core is locally solid-body, so it is cyclostrophic: \((p'/\rho)|_{\text{axis}}=\omega_0^2/4\). The direct term of the axisymmetric pressure Hessian at the axis is therefore

Profile-independent brake
\[ (\nabla^2 p)_{12}\;\to\;-\tfrac14\,\omega_1\omega_2 , \]

which is exactly the coefficient that cancels the frame-chase term in the strain evolution on a coherent tube. Measured on tilted tubes in triaxial strain across a fourfold amplitude range the fitted coefficient sits at 0.243 to 0.246, and the first-order correction \([p''-p'/\rho]/(2|\omega|^2)\) vanishes at the axis and is negative off it, which predicts a value slightly under 1/4. The reading: on coherent cores the pressure enforces its own structure's persistence, \(\dot S_{12}\approx 0\), and alignment there is eigenframe enslavement rather than a chase. The remaining hypotheses are slender-tube and near-steady; those are named, not hidden.

THE SPAGHETTI RESULT: BRAIDS AND FILAMENTS

The intense-vorticity regions in a real flow are not clean tubes. They are tangles of flux-coherent strands, spaghetti. Two results on that picture, both tagged conditional in the ledger, both worth having on the table.

THE BRAID CEILING (CONDITIONAL)

Carve a braid \(B\) into \(N\) nearly tube-like strands with circulations \(\Gamma_k\), \(\sum_k\Gamma_k=\Gamma\), under an applied exterior strain \(s\). If each strand is Burgers-slaved at that strain, its peak vorticity is capped at \(\Gamma_k s/(4\pi u)\), so

A tangle cannot out-peak a single tube of the same total circulation
\[ \|\omega\|_{L^\infty(B)} \le \max_k rac{\Gamma_k\,s}{4\pi u} \le rac{\Gamma\,s}{4\pi u}. \]

Splitting circulation across strands only lowers the ceiling: at equal shares the ratio to the single-tube cap is \(1/N\), checked at \(N=1,2,3,5,10,20\). Growth past the ceiling needs accretion or an encounter with another braid, and the same-sign case is self-defeating: merger raises the total circulation and lowers the strand count, which lands you back at one tube with one cap. The open constant is \(C_{br}\) for highly tangled braids, where strands are far from tube-like. That is where a reader could push.

THE CLASSICAL PINCH TERMINATES ON THE SAME OBJECT

Two anti-parallel filaments under Crow's instability tilt, approach at the troughs, and the classical clock gives a finite-time collision, \(b^2(t)=b_0^2-(c\Gamma/\pi)\,t\), so \(t_c=\pi b_0^2/(c\Gamma)\). With viscous cores slaved to the pinch strain the logarithm is exactly constant during the collapse, \(\Lambda=\ln(b/a)= frac12\ln(Re_\Gamma/8\pi)\), so the filament model is self-consistent all the way down and predicts \(u_{\max} o\infty\).

It is singular by construction. The model freezes topology (no reconnection), keeps cores rigid (no core PDE), and its \(\sqrt{t_c-t}\) collapse is precisely the Leray self-similar class that Nečas–Růžička–Šverák excluded. At the contact scale the real equations must either reconnect or break self-similarity, and that fork is the residue below. Everything up to contact genuinely happens, which is why the pinch stays the persistent candidate and why aircraft trails link.

THE RESIDUE, IN FOUR EQUIVALENT FORMS

Everything above locates one object. Write \(K_{CF}\) for the Constantin–Fefferman coherence measure of the vorticity direction on the stretching-active high-vorticity set, \(\theta\) for the local misalignment, \(M\) for the strain amplitude, and \(c_1,c_2,c_\nu\) for constants of the Riccati pair \(\dot M\le(c_1\theta-c_\nu)M^2\), \(\dot K\le c_2\theta M K\). Any one of the following, proved for all smooth finite-energy data, gives Clay A:

FormStatementWhere it comes from
D1 — coherence\(\sup_t K_{CF}(t)\le K^*(E_0,\nu)\) on the stretching-active set.Constantin–Fefferman criterion, dynamical form.
D2 — heatA rigorous \(K\sqrt{s}\) gain for \(\int_0^{s^*}\nabla^2 e^{s\Delta}[\text{BS of }S]\,ds\) on the aligned shear sector, with a \(\Gamma\)-normalized tail.Lemma 6 split at \(s^*\).
D3 — pitchOrbit-averaged \(c_1\theta-c_\nu\le-\delta<0\), uniformly in \(\nu\).Riccati pair; the measured pitch of the (amplitude, coherence) helix.
D4 — Mohr\(\|\nabla(P_\perp S\xi)\|\) subcritical on the shear sector: the gradient layer of the turning rate.Lemma 5 lifted one derivative.

The lemmas say what a proof of any of these must look like: dynamical (Lemma 3), scale-covariant (Lemma 4), near-field (Lemma 1 covers the rest), on the shear sector (Lemma 5), and at gradient level. Every route I tried, helix, transform, heat, Mohr, terminated on this same object. That is the proposal: stop looking for a new inequality and prove one of D1–D4.

The Perelman analogy is exact and it is the spine of the route: Type I is already handled by a Perelman-shaped argument (a critical monotone quantity plus exclusion of self-similar profiles). WHAT THIS PAGE DOES NOT CLAIM

  • It does not claim a proof of any of the four Clay statements.
  • Lemmas 1 to 6 are proven; Lemma 7 is derivation-complete through second order under slender-tube and near-steady hypotheses that are still open.
  • The numerics are at \(\nu\ge 0.002\) on a periodic box. Nothing here extrapolates to the inviscid limit, and the flat corner fraction across Reynolds number is evidence against an easy version of the route.
  • The residue is stated, not closed. Conservation of difficulty held every time: each "last piece" relocated the full problem into D1–D4.

I'll share it with anyone who wants to check or attack a specific item, and I'll post the outcome here either way. The most useful thing a reader can do is one of these:

EXHIBIT THE COUNTEREXAMPLE
