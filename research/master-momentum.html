MASTER MOMENTUM EQUATION

A CLOSED MOMENTUM PDE WITH EDDY VISCOSITY

Twelve equations I wrote between 2019 and 2025 for price momentum, rewritten as a closed momentum PDE with a k-ε-style eddy viscosity. Closed means every variable on the right side is built from p(t), five gains, and two windows I have to pick.

Status, 2026-09-09. The twelve equations and the closure check (every variable in Eq. 12 traces back to p(t)) are derived on this page. Nothing here is measured against flow data or market data. The regularity argument was reviewed from outside in July 2026 and three steps failed; the current v2 proves a Z5/2 dissipation bound for an amended closure (Eq. 11′), the Bakry-Émery step is a heuristic, and the spatial transfer is open. The applications section is a list of directions I haven't tested.

ABSTRACT

The Master Momentum Equation is a set of twelve coupled equations I first wrote for price momentum (the source is REFFELT_MASTER_EQUATIONS.md in my repo, 2019 to 2025). Here p is price, v is its rate of change, and σATR is Average True Range, a volatility measure from trading. The eddy viscosity is built from the system's own variables, so the set closes on itself without a separate turbulence model.

The system builds from primitive observables (velocity, mass, curvature) through intermediate constructions (PID forcing, smoothness, a turbulent kinetic energy analogue) to an eddy viscosity that feeds back into a dynamic resistance field. The last equation (Eq. 12) is a momentum equation with the shape of Navier-Stokes and every term defined above it.

THE 12 EQUATIONS

LAYER 1 — PRIMITIVE OBSERVABLES

EQUATION 1 — MOMENTUM (LOG-RETURN)

EQ. 1
$$v(t) = \frac{dp}{dt}$$

The rate of change of the state p. In the original setting p is a price series, so this is a return per bar; in the solver it is the first difference Δp/Δt.

EQUATION 2 — EFFECTIVE MASS

EQ. 2
$$m(t) = \frac{1}{\sigma_{\text{ATR}}(t)}$$

Mass is the inverse of local volatility. σATR is Average True Range over a window I pick; the trading code uses 14 bars, and sim.cpp shortcuts it to the one-bar |Δp|. High-volatility stretches get low inertia and respond fast to forcing; quiet stretches get high inertia.

EQUATION 3 — STEERING SIGNAL

EQ. 3
$$i(t) = \operatorname{Hilbert\_imag}(p(t))$$

The imaginary component of the analytic signal (via Hilbert transform) extracts the instantaneous phase and amplitude envelope. This provides a 90°-shifted version of p that serves as the steering input for the PID controller.

LAYER 2 — INTERMEDIATE FORCES

EQUATION 4 — PID FORCE

EQ. 4
$$F_{\text{PID}}(t) = k_p \cdot i(t) + k_i \int i \, dt + k_d \cdot \dot{i}(t)$$

A PID controller turns the steering signal into a force. The P-term responds to the current signal, the I-term accumulates its history, and the D-term reacts to its slope. The three gains are inputs I pick. sim.cpp currently hard-codes FPID = 0.5 instead of computing Eq. 4.

EQUATION 5 — SMOOTHNESS

EQ. 5
$$s(t) = 1 - \frac{\operatorname{std}(r_N)}{\operatorname{mean}(r_N)}$$

Smoothness measures how regular the last N returns rN have been. As written s ≤ 1, and the solver clamps it at 0 when std exceeds mean, so the working range is [0, 1]. s near 1 means a low coefficient of variation (laminar); s near 0 means the variation is as big as the mean (turbulent). N is a window I pick.

EQUATION 6 — CURVATURE

EQ. 6
$$c(t) = \frac{\partial^2 p}{\partial t^2}$$

The second derivative of the state field. Curvature captures acceleration/deceleration of the trajectory, serving as both a turbulence indicator and a damping term in the master equation.

LAYER 3 — EDDY VISCOSITY

Eq. 10 is the algebraic νt = Cμk²/ε relation from Launder & Spalding (1974). Eqs. 8 and 9 are my own definitions of k and ε, not the k-ε transport equations, and I haven't validated them against any flow data.

EQUATION 7 — STATIC HOLOGRAPHIC RESISTANCE

EQ. 7
$$R_0(p, \ell)$$

The base resistance manifold from the Holographic Resistance Membrane page. It's an input here, not a result of this page: the static friction landscape before the turbulence correction.

EQUATION 8 — TURBULENT KINETIC ENERGY

EQ. 8
$$k(p, \ell) = (1 - s) \cdot \nabla \cdot \mathbf{M}_{\text{grid}}, \qquad \mathbf{M}_{\text{grid}} := m\,v$$

My turbulent kinetic energy analogue is non-smoothness (1 − s) times the divergence of the momentum field Mgrid = m·v. In sim.cpp that divergence is the one-step difference mivi − mi−1vi−1, clamped at zero. Laminar stretches (s near 1) give k near zero. The proof page uses a simpler pair, k = (1−s)|v| with a corrected ε, to get the closed form in Stage 1 below; the two are not the same definition, and I say so there.

EQUATION 9 — ENERGY DISSIPATION RATE

EQ. 9
$$\varepsilon(p, \ell) = c \cdot \frac{1 - s}{s} \cdot \frac{1}{\ell}$$

My dissipation analogue combines curvature c, the ratio (1−s)/s, and the inverse scale 1/ℓ. The shape is meant to echo an energy cascade from large to small scales. That's the intent; I haven't checked it against a Kolmogorov spectrum or any measured dissipation.

EQUATION 10 — EDDY VISCOSITY

EQ. 10
$$\nu_t(p, \ell) = C_\mu \frac{k^2}{\varepsilon} \qquad C_\mu = 0.09$$

The algebraic eddy viscosity relation with the constant Cμ = 0.09 from Launder & Spalding, "The numerical computation of turbulent flows", Comp. Meth. Appl. Mech. Eng. 3 (1974) 269-289. I took the constant from CFD; nothing on this page justifies it for this system.

EQUATION 11 — DYNAMIC RESISTANCE

EQ. 11
$$R_{\text{eff}}(p, \ell) = \gamma_0 + \nu_t(p, \ell)$$

The effective resistance field adds base viscosity γ0 to the eddy viscosity, so the static R0 gets a term that moves with local turbulence. The v2 proof page works with an amended version, Eq. 11′, that adds a global feedback term; this page shows the original.

LAYER 4 — MASTER EQUATION

EQUATION 12 — MASTER MOMENTUM EQUATION

EQ. 12 — THE MASTER EQUATION
$$\frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{F_{\text{PID}}}{m} + v\frac{\partial v}{\partial p} - s \cdot c - \frac{\partial R_{\text{eff}}}{\partial p}$$

Four terms, and what I mean by each:

Damping−s · cCurvature-smoothness brake (smooth trajectories damp acceleration)
Resistance gradient−∂Reff/∂pTurbulence-corrected friction from the holographic membrane

DIRECTIONS I HAVEN'T TESTED

Each of these is a mapping I'd like to try. None of them has a run behind it.

WEATHER

If s can tell laminar from turbulent air the way it tells quiet from noisy price, Eq. 12 might serve as a closure in a toy atmospheric model; I haven't tried it against any NWP scheme.

DATACENTER AIRFLOW

With p as temperature, v as heat flux and FPID as the HVAC loop, the system could be a cheap thermal predictor; I haven't compared it to a single CFD run.

WING BOUNDARY LAYERS

The s term looks like a transition switch and R0 could carry the geometry; I haven't put it next to any boundary-layer data.

TRAINING DYNAMICS

With p as parameters, m as the inverse gradient variance (like Adam's second moment) and c as curvature, Eq. 12 could be read as a schedule; I haven't trained anything with it.

CRACK PROPAGATION

R0 as a grain-boundary map and the s transition as the yield point is a picture I like; there's no fracture data behind it.

REGULARITY ARGUMENT

I tried to prove global regularity for this closed system. Outside review in July 2026 found three errors in the first version, and I've kept them on the proof page. The current v2 argument proves a Z5/2 dissipation bound and a continuation ladder for an amended closure (Eq. 11′). The Bakry-Émery step is now a heuristic, not a proof, and the spatial transfer is open. This says nothing about constant-viscosity Navier-Stokes.

The six stages below are the v1 argument as I first wrote it. I've left the mathematics in place and marked each step that v2 retracted, so you can see what failed and where. The v2 status per step: spatial Hessian transfer open, weighted Poincaré a conjecture, RicBE > 0 a heuristic, the smin bootstrap retired, and the dissipation exponent Z5/2 rather than Z².

THE V1 SIX-STAGE ARGUMENT

SELF-CONTAINED PDE SYSTEM

Status: derived on this page. v2 works with the amended closure Eq. 11′, which adds a global feedback term to this νt.

The dependency graph above shows Eq. 12 is closed: every variable traces back to p(t) and its derivatives. The argument works on the parameter manifold \(\mathcal{M} = \{(s,|v|)\in(0,1]\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\}\), where s is smoothness and |v| is speed.

EDDY VISCOSITY — CLOSED FORM
$$\nu_t = C_\mu(1-s)(s+\delta)|v|, \qquad C_\mu = 0.09,\;\delta = 0.01$$

This comes from the proof-page versions of Eqs. 8 and 9, k = (1−s)|v| and ε = (1−s)|v|/(s+δ), substituted into Eq. 10. Those are not the Eqs. 8 and 9 shown above (which use the momentum divergence and curvature); PROOF_FORMATION.md swapped them in for dimensional consistency. δ = 0.01 is a regulariser I picked.

SCALING CHAIN

Retracted July 2026, see proof page (erratum E1: the Step A inequality is false; Step C's smin floor is retired). v2 replaces this with a bound under the amended closure Eq. 11′.

To stop blow-up, the effective viscosity has to grow fast enough to beat the nonlinear advection term. v1 tried a lower bound on νt in terms of enstrophy \(Z = \|\nabla v\|_{L^2}^2\).

STEP A — SOBOLEV EMBEDDING
$$\|v\|_{L^\infty} \leq C_{\text{Sob}}\|v\|_{H^1} = C_{\text{Sob}}\left(\|v\|_{L^2}^2 + Z\right)^{1/2}$$
STEP B — LARGE-Z REGIME
$$\text{For } Z \geq Z_0: \quad \|v\|_{L^\infty} \leq C_{\text{Sob}}\sqrt{2Z} \leq 2C_{\text{Sob}}Z^{1/2}$$
STEP C — SMOOTHNESS FLOOR (LEMMA 3.2)
$$s_{\min} > 0 \;\;\text{on compact existence intervals (bootstrap, see Stage 5)}$$
STEP D — COMBINED BOUND
$$\nu_t = C_\mu(1-s)(s+\delta)|v| \geq \underbrace{C_\mu(1-s_{\max})(s_{\min}+\delta)}_{\gamma_1 > 0} \cdot \|v\|_{L^\infty} \geq \gamma_1 Z^{1/2}$$

PARAMETER MANIFOLD GEOMETRY

Retracted July 2026, see proof page. v2 keeps this as a heuristic only; the claim that κ > 0 is attained is wrong, since the second entry 1/v² goes to 0 as v grows, so the infimum is 0.

Define the potential \(\Phi(s,|v|) = -\ln\nu_t\) on the parameter manifold \(\mathcal{M}\). The Hessian of Φ gives the Bakry-Émery curvature tensor (Bakry & Émery, "Diffusions hypercontractives", Séminaire de probabilités XIX, 1985):

BAKRY-ÉMERY RICCI TENSOR
$$\operatorname{Ric}_{\text{BE}} = \operatorname{Hess}(-\ln\nu_t) = \operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{1}{(1-s)^2}+\frac{1}{(s+\delta)^2},\;\frac{1}{v^2}\right) > 0$$

Both diagonal entries are positive on the interior of \(\mathcal{M}\), so the tensor is pointwise positive. That's as far as it goes: v1 claimed a uniform lower bound \(\kappa = \inf_{\mathcal{M}}\lambda_{\min}(\operatorname{Ric}_{\text{BE}}) > 0\) is attained. It isn't. The 1/v² entry has infimum 0 on an unbounded velocity range, so there is no uniform κ, and everything downstream that needed one falls with it.

FROM MANIFOLD CURVATURE TO FUNCTIONAL INEQUALITY

Retracted July 2026, see proof page (erratum E3: the transfer from the parameter manifold to the spatial domain is invalid as a proof). In v2 the weighted Poincaré inequality is a conjecture and the spatial Hessian transfer is open.

The Lott-Sturm-Villani framework converts curvature bounds into analytic inequalities via optimal transport. References: Villani, Optimal Transport: Old and New (Springer, 2009); Sturm, "On the geometry of metric measure spaces", Acta Math. 196 (2006); Otto & Villani, "Generalization of an inequality by Talagrand and links with the logarithmic Sobolev inequality", J. Funct. Anal. 173 (2000).

LSV EQUIVALENCE (VILLANI 2009, STURM 2006)
$$\operatorname{Ric}_{\text{BE}} \geq \kappa > 0 \;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\; \kappa\text{-displacement convexity of Boltzmann entropy along }W_2\text{ geodesics}$$
HWI INEQUALITY (OTTO-VILLANI 2000)
$$H(\rho\|\mu) \leq W_2(\rho,\mu)\sqrt{I(\rho\|\mu)} - \frac{\kappa}{2}W_2(\rho,\mu)^2$$

Optimizing over W2 would give a log-Sobolev inequality, hence a weighted Poincaré inequality on the parameter manifold, if the uniform κ from Stage 3 existed. v1 then used the chain rule to move it to the spatial domain; that step is the one review rejected, and it is where I'm stuck:

SPATIAL TRANSFER (CHAIN RULE)
$$\int|\nabla v|^2\nu_t\,dx \geq \kappa_{\text{eff}}\int|v-\bar{v}|^2\nu_t\,dx, \qquad \kappa_{\text{eff}} = \kappa \cdot \sigma_{\min}(\nabla_x(s,|v|))^2$$

THE V1 ENSTROPHY INEQUALITY

Retracted July 2026, see proof page. The Z² dissipation term rested on the Stage 2 bound and the smin floor, both retired. v2 proves a Z5/2 bound instead, for the amended closure Eq. 11′.

The v1 enstrophy evolution used the Ladyzhenskaya inequality (three-dimensional form \(\|u\|_{L^4}^4 \le C\|u\|_{L^2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^3\); Ladyzhenskaya, The Mathematical Theory of Viscous Incompressible Flow, 1963) for the advection term and the νt ≥ γ1Z1/2 lower bound for dissipation:

FUNDAMENTAL ENSTROPHY INEQUALITY
$$\frac{dZ}{dt} \leq \underbrace{C_1 Z^{3/2}}_{\text{advection growth}} - \underbrace{\gamma' s_{\min} Z^2}_{\text{turbulent dissipation}}$$

If that held, the dissipation exponent 2 would beat the advection exponent 3/2, and for large Z the dissipation term would win:

COMPARISON PRINCIPLE
$$Z(t) \leq \max\!\left(Z(0),\;\frac{C_1}{\gamma' s_{\min}}\right) < \infty \qquad \forall\, t > 0$$

Bounded enstrophy would rule out gradient blow-up, which is the regularity condition. The v2 page has the corrected inequality with \(\tilde{C}_1 := C_1(1+\gamma_1^{-3})\) and the Z5/2 term.

BOOTSTRAP TO GLOBAL EXISTENCE

Retracted July 2026, see proof page (erratum E2: the Morrey claim in Step 2 is wrong; the smin bootstrap is retired). v2 has a continuation ladder for Eq. 11′ that doesn't use smin.

v1 tried to close with a continuation bootstrap:

STEP 1

Short-time existence by standard parabolic PDE theory on [0, T*].

STEP 2

smin > 0 on [0, T*] by Morrey embedding: bounded enstrophy ⇒ Hölder continuous v ⇒ bounded coefficient of variation ⇒ s bounded away from 0. This is the step that fails: in three dimensions H1 does not embed in a Hölder space, so bounded enstrophy doesn't give the continuity the chain needs.

STEP 3

Z bounded on [0, T*] by the Stage 5 inequality, using smin > 0. Falls with Step 2.

STEP 4

Extend past T*: bounded enstrophy provides the a priori estimates needed to restart the short-time existence theorem at T*.

STEP 5

Induction: repeat Steps 1–4 to extend to [0, ∞). With Step 2 gone, this ladder has no first rung.

WHAT V1 CLAIMED, AND WHAT V2 ACTUALLY HAS

The theorem below is the v1 statement. It is retracted (July 2026). I keep it here so the record is visible.

v1: Let \((v, s, \nu_t, R_{\text{eff}})\) solve the 12-equation closure on \(\mathbb{T}^3\) with smooth initial data and \(s(0) \in (0,1]\). Then the solution exists for all time and

$$\sup_{t \geq 0} \|\nabla v(t)\|_{L^2}^2 < \infty$$

v2, what is proved: a Z5/2 dissipation bound and a continuation ladder for the amended closure Eq. 11′. What is not proved: global regularity for the system on this page. The Bakry-Émery curvature is a heuristic, the weighted Poincaré inequality is a conjecture, and the spatial transfer is open.

SCOPE

Everything in this section is about the Master Momentum system with its variable νt. Constant-viscosity Navier-Stokes is a different equation and remains a Millennium Prize Problem; nothing here touches it. The reason the variable case looked tractable is that νt grows with |v|, which adds dissipation the constant case lacks. Whether that is enough is exactly what's still open.

The step-by-step algebra, the three errata, and the v2 argument are on the proof page. PROOF_FORMATION.md in millennium_sim is the working draft behind it.

HOW TO REPRODUCE

The solver is millennium_sim/sim.cpp (one function, step_master_equation, compiled with Emscripten for the solver page). It implements Eqs. 1-12 as written above, with the shortcuts I flagged: one-bar |Δp| for σATR, FPID = 0.5, s clamped at 0, ε floored at 0.01. The argument is in millennium_sim/PROOF_FORMATION.md and the v2 proof page, millennium_sim/navier-stokes-proof.html. The original equations are in REFFELT_MASTER_EQUATIONS.md in my repo.

WHAT ISN'T MEASURED

Nothing on this page has been checked against flow data, and nothing on this page has been checked against market data either. The solver runs; I haven't compared its output to a DNS result, a wind tunnel, or a backtest. Cμ = 0.09 is borrowed, not fitted. The k and ε definitions are mine and untested.
