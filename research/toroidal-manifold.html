TOROIDAL MANIFOLD GEOMETRY

STATE-SPACE TOPOLOGY WITH HOURGLASS PINCH

Fixed-width binary words map onto a torus: angular position from the two half-words, radial distance from bit density, and an hourglass stretch around r = 0.5 that I use as an observation point.

Status, 2026-09-09. T1 to T8 are coordinate definitions and a few weights I chose, not derivations; the only thing that follows from something else is the torus embedding in T5, which is textbook. The two Chart.js plots below are computed live from T3 and T6; the wireframe torus is a display with a made-up path. I haven't measured how many words collide on the surface, how well the 16-digit address separates trajectories, or whether any of the applications hold. The one place I've used these coordinates is the proof-formation notes in the millennium sim folder.

ABSTRACT

The Toroidal Manifold is a way I plot the state of anything that works on fixed-width binary words. Each word gets an angle θ from its two halves, a radius r from its bit density, and a second angle φ from nibble parities. A sequence of words becomes a path on a torus.

The part I care about is the hourglass pinch at r = 0.5, where half the bits are set. A well-mixed word sits near there, so that's where paths crowd together, and T3 stretches that band out so I can tell them apart. I also define a tent-shaped weight Bθ that peaks at the pinch; I call it a field on this page but it's just a weight.

COORDINATE SYSTEM

STEP T1 — ANGULAR COORDINATE

I split each word w into upper and lower halves of Nbits/2 bits each and take the angle of that pair:

EQUATION T1 — ANGULAR POSITION
$$\theta_i = \operatorname{atan2}\!\left(w_{\text{upper}} - 2^{N_{\text{bits}}/2 - 1},\; w_{\text{lower}} - 2^{N_{\text{bits}}/2 - 1}\right) + \pi \qquad \theta \in (0, 2\pi]$$

Subtracting 2Nbits/2 − 1 centers each half-word on zero. atan2 returns (−π, π], so adding π lands in (0, 2π]. For 32-bit words that offset is 215, which is what my code hardcodes; the formula here is the general one so it matches T2.

STEP T2 — RADIAL COORDINATE (BIT DENSITY)

The radial position on the torus measures information density:

EQUATION T2 — BIT DENSITY
$$r_i = \frac{\operatorname{popcount}(w_i)}{N_{\text{bits}}} \qquad r \in [0, 1]$$

For 32-bit words, Nbits = 32. popcount is the Hamming weight (Hamming, Bell Syst. Tech. J. 29, 1950). r = 0 is all zeros, r = 1 is all ones, and r = 0.5 is half the bits set, which is where a well-mixed word tends to sit.

STEP T3 — HOURGLASS TRANSFORMATION

A monotone remap of r that I apply before plotting:

EQUATION T3 — HOURGLASS PINCH
$$\tilde{r}_i = 0.5 + \operatorname{sign}(r - 0.5) \cdot |r - 0.5|^{\alpha} \qquad \alpha = 0.5$$

With α = 0.5 the map pulls states away from r = 0.5 and squeezes the ends toward it. That's the point: I want the crowded middle spread out so I can tell near-maximum-entropy states apart. It's monotone, so ordering is kept. α = 0.5 is a value I picked; the plot below lets you try others.

STEP T4 — AZIMUTHAL LIFT

A third coordinate lifts the torus into full 3D:

EQUATION T4 — AZIMUTHAL COORDINATE
$$\phi_i = \frac{\operatorname{nibble\_parities}(w_i)}{2^{N_{\text{nibbles}}} - 1} \cdot 2\pi \qquad \phi \in [0, 2\pi)$$

nibble_parities is the integer whose bit k is the XOR-parity of the k-th 4-bit group of w, so it has Nnibbles = Nbits/4 bits. It carries some local bit structure that θ and r blur over.

STEP T5 — 3D TORUS EMBEDDING

The standard parametrization of a torus with major radius R and tube radius r (do Carmo, Differential Geometry of Curves and Surfaces, 1976, sec. 2-2):

EQUATION T5 — TORUS SURFACE
$$x = (R + r\cos\phi)\cos\theta \qquad y = (R + r\cos\phi)\sin\theta \qquad z = r\sin\phi$$

R = 2 is a display choice; any R > 1 keeps the surface from self-intersecting. This puts every word somewhere on a torus of tube radius r inside R = 2. It isn't one-to-one; different words can land on the same spot, and I haven't measured how often.

PINCH WEIGHT

I call Bθ a field, but it's just a tent function: 1 at r = 0.5, 0 at the ends, linear between. There's no Maxwell behind it. I use it as a weight for how close a state sits to the pinch.

EQUATION T6 — PINCH WEIGHT
$$B_\theta = 1 - 2|r - 0.5|$$

It falls off linearly with distance from r = 0.5. Summing it along a path, weighted by how far the angle moves each step, gives one number per trajectory:

EQUATION T7 — WEIGHTED ANGULAR SUM
$$\Phi = \sum_{t=1}^{T} B_\theta(t)\, d\theta(t)$$

where dθ(t) is the step in θ between consecutive states, θ(t) − θ(t − 1), wrapped into (−π, π]. Φ is a scalar summary of a path. It can't separate trajectories on its own; two different paths can share the same Φ, and I use it as one feature among several rather than a signature.

EQUATION T8 — ENERGY FUNCTIONAL
$$E = \frac{1}{2}\sum_i (r_i - 0.5)^2 + \frac{1}{2}\sum_i \left(\frac{\phi_i}{\pi}\right)^2$$

E is smallest when every state has r = 0.5 and φ = 0, the pinch equator. My conjecture is that well-mixed processes drift toward low E; I haven't tested it on anything beyond a few hash traces, and "thermodynamic" is a loose word for it.

HIERARCHICAL ADDRESSING

Each trajectory gets a 16-digit code in four groups. Some digits can exceed 8 as written; I need to clip them all to 0-8 before this is really base-9. The example below is illustrative, not from a real run:

COARSE (CCCC) 4 Global manifold region — angular mean, bit density, winding number, radius variance $$C_1 = \min\!\left(8,\left\lfloor\tfrac{\bar\theta}{2\pi}\cdot 9\right\rfloor\right) \quad C_2 = \min(8,\lfloor\bar{r}\cdot 9\rfloor) \quad C_3 = \left\lfloor\tfrac{\sum\theta}{2\pi}\bmod 9\right\rfloor \quad C_4 = \min(8,\lfloor\text{Var}(r)\cdot 100\rfloor)$$

FINE (FFFF) 4 Spectral features — peak FFT frequency, spectral energy, skewness, kurtosis of radius $$F_1 = \min(8,\lfloor\arg\max|\mathcal{F}(\theta)|/10\rfloor) \quad F_2 = \min(8,\lfloor\log(\|\mathcal{F}\|^2+1)\rfloor) \quad F_3 = \min(8,\lfloor(E[(r-.5)^3]+.5)\cdot 9\rfloor) \quad F_4 = \min(8,\lfloor E[(r-.5)^4]\cdot 100\rfloor)$$
PINCH (PPPP) 4 Transit pattern through r = 0.5 — entry timing, dwell time, angular displacement, radial velocity at pinch $$P_1 = \min(8,\lfloor t_{\text{entry}}/8\rfloor) \quad P_2 = \min\!\left(8,\left\lfloor\tfrac{\Delta t}{T}\cdot 9\right\rfloor\right) \quad P_3 = \min(8,\lfloor\tfrac{|\Delta\theta|}{2\pi}\cdot 9\rfloor) \quad P_4 = \min(8,\lfloor|\dot r_{\text{pinch}}|\cdot 50\rfloor)$$

The scale constants (9, 100, 5, 50, 10, 8) are ones I picked; they aren't derived. The min(8, ·) clips are what make each digit a base-9 digit; my code doesn't apply all of them yet. In the table, θ̄ and r̄ are means along the trajectory, dots are first differences per step, tentry is the first step with |r − 0.5| < 0.05, Δt is the number of steps spent inside that band out of T total, and Δθ is the angle turned while inside it.

The idea behind the four groups is hierarchical lookup: match on the coarse digits first, then medium, then fine, with the pinch digits as a separate check. How many candidates each level returns, and how much the next level narrows it, is not measured. I don't know yet whether the fine digits separate individual trajectories.

HOLOGRAPHIC RESONANCE

When I've clustered the same trajectories in several coordinate frames, I stack the cluster centers and sum a Gaussian bump at each one. That's a kernel density sum in the sense of Silverman, Density Estimation for Statistics and Data Analysis (1986), ch. 4, with a fixed bandwidth:

EQUATION T9 — RESONANCE FIELD
$$\text{Resonance}(p) = \sum_{a=1}^{M} w_a \exp\!\left(-\frac{\|p - c_a\|^2}{2\sigma^2}\right) \qquad \sigma = 5.0$$

Here M is the number of cluster centers across all views, ca is the a-th center in torus coordinates (θ, r̃, φ), and wa is the fraction of trajectories in that cluster. σ = 5.0 is a bandwidth I picked; it isn't derived and it's large next to the coordinate ranges, so the field is smooth. Points where several views put a center nearby score high, and I call those resonant nodes. Whether they mean anything beyond "the clusterings agree here" is untested. T10 is the per-view average of the same idea:

EQUATION T10 — INTERFERENCE FIELD
$$I(p) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{v=1}^{M} \exp\!\left(-\frac{d_v(p)^2}{2\sigma_v^2}\right)$$

where M now counts views, dv(p) is the distance from p to the nearest center in view v, and σv is a per-view bandwidth. How σv is set is defined in code, not on this page. The "facade matrices" I've mentioned elsewhere are linear maps from each view's local coordinates back to the shared torus frame, fitted by weighted least squares; the weights and the fitting target are defined in code, not on this page.

WHERE I'D LIKE TO TRY IT

All of this is conjecture. I haven't run the coordinates on any of these problems past a few hash traces, and each card says what I'd expect to see, not what I've seen.

🔬 HASH ROUND STATES

Hash rounds are rotations, XORs and additions on fixed-width words, so the round states plot straight onto the torus. I think a weakly mixing round would show up as a path that lingers away from r = 0.5, and that the 16-digit code could work as a compact fingerprint of a hash's behaviour. I haven't tested either against a known-weak hash, so I can't say the plot shows anything a bit-level test wouldn't.

⚛ QUBIT-LIKE WORDS

I'd like to see whether a bit-string encoding of a small circuit's classical shadow gives readable paths here, with θ playing the role of a rotation and φ a relative phase. r is a bit density, not an amplitude, so this is only an analogy about the shape of the coordinates. I haven't tested it.

🔥 TOKAMAK COORDINATES

A tokamak lives on a physical torus, so the coordinate names line up: toroidal angle, normalized flux coordinate, poloidal angle. The physics doesn't line up the same way. The magnetic axis is at flux coordinate 0, not 0.5, and the safety factor q ≈ rBφ/(RBθ) (Wesson, Tokamaks, 4th ed., sec. 3.4) involves the real toroidal and poloidal fields, which my tent function Bθ isn't. What I'd like to test is whether the hierarchical code can label diagnostic time series by mode; I haven't tried it.

🖥 MULTI-RESOLUTION INDEX

The four-group code is a multi-resolution key, and 94 = 6561 coarse buckets is a plausible first partition for a vector index. I haven't tested it against any vector database, and the address space of 916 ≈ 1.85 × 1015 says nothing about how many trajectories actually land in distinct codes.

🌐 ORBITAL SLOTS

Right ascension, altitude and argument of perigee also parametrize a torus, so a constellation could be plotted the same way. I'd like to see whether the resonance sum in T9 picks out slots where coverage, link geometry and ground visibility overlap. I haven't tested it, and Bθ says nothing about orbital stability.
