Research / Field note

Eighteen points

Past-month marijuana use and 2024 Democratic vote share

Past-month marijuana use and the 2024 presidential vote move together by a wide margin. The lowest-use fifth of regions cast 42% of its two-party vote for the Democrat. The highest-use fifth cast 60%. That span is the size of the factor. Two further curves take the published suggestibility and IQ results and carry them from the THC those studies actually sat on to the potency on the market now. The band on each curve is the prediction error.

Data and method

Region CSV Results JSON Regression script Full method

Method. NSDUH 2021–2023 substate small-area estimates of past-month marijuana use, ages 12 or older (SAMHSA shapefile attribute MRJMON), joined to 2024 presidential votes reallocated to ZCTAs by the land-cover method (Harvard Dataverse 10.7910/DVN/Z8TSH3), with ACS 2019–2023 age and education and Census 2020 ZCTA internal points. Vote-weighted least squares, 382 regions, HC1 standard errors. Weights are two-party votes. The region file’s marijuana column is mj. Fitted marijuana models are the region_* and zcta_* entries in results.json other than the alcohol keys.

The same regression’s alcohol results are a separate note: Past-month alcohol use and 2024 vote share.

Bar height tracks share above a 35% floor so the rise is readable. The national two-party Democratic share in this file is 49.2%. The top fifth sits 11 points above that line. The bottom fifth sits 7 points below it. 382 regions, 30,389 ZCTAs, 150.1 million allocated two-party votes.

The raw slope is 1.48 vote-share points per percentage point of use. Density and age take it to 1.13. A bachelor’s degree leaves 1.09. State indicators, which absorb legalization and the rest of statewide politics, leave 0.99. State and education together leave 1.14. The interquartile gap in use, 11.4% to 18.2%, maps to 7.8 points of share on the 1.13 slope. The full observed range, 6.2% to 28.3%, maps to about 25 points.

Density and a college degree correlate more strongly on their own (0.73 and 0.71, against 0.42 for marijuana). They do not eat the slope. Past-month alcohol, in the same density and age model, is 0.66 points per point of drinking. A point of marijuana use lines up with more vote share than a point of drinking. That comparison is written up on its own page, Past-month alcohol use and 2024 vote share.

Suggestibility

Accepting a detail that was planted, and reporting it as remembered, is the lab measure of suggestibility. That is the recorded piece of what people mean by being led into a false account. Kloft and colleagues (PNAS, 2020) vaporized 300 μg of THC per kilogram, about 21 mg at 70 kg. While still intoxicated, volunteers accepted a suggested false detail in an eyewitness scene 19% of the time (SE 0.04). On placebo the same people accepted it 8% of the time (SE 0.02). Cohen’s d for that contrast is 0.62. Related-word false alarms in the same session moved from 27% to 42% (d = 0.58).

The x-axis is flower potency. The lab dose is placed at 14% THC: half a gram of flower at that potency, with 30% of the THC inhaled, delivers 21 mg. That is an ordinary modern session, not a 1995 joint. The curve is linear on the logit between the placebo and that dose. Past the lab dose, the band adds a further 35% uncertainty on the extra climb, because nobody has rerun this test at concentrate strength. Dots are the measured means. Whiskers are their published standard errors, times 1.96.

Cognitive decline, study potency versus current potency

Meier and colleagues (PNAS, 2012) followed the Dunedin birth cohort. The most persistent adolescent-onset cannabis users lost 8 IQ points from childhood to age 38. The broader persistent-dependence group lost about 6. The study did not assay THC. Those people were using between the late 1980s and 2010. DEA-seized flower over 1995–2010 averaged 7.1% THC. That is the study rate used here.

The prediction multiplies the 8-point loss by the ratio of potency to 7.1%. Sampling error on the anchor is taken as 2.5 points, which is a within-group SD of about 12 IQ points divided by the square root of 23, the size of that adolescent-onset group. Past 7.1%, the band adds a 50% uncertainty on the extra loss, because the cohort never compared potencies. The dot is the measured 8. The rest of the curve is the scale-up.

The center of the current-rate prediction is 18 points, a little more than double the published 8. The band runs from 3 to 33. The width is real: potency was not a variable in Dunedin, so the scale-up is a prediction. The center of that prediction is not a small effect. The same scaling applied to the broader 6-point persistent group lands near 13 points at 16.1% THC.
