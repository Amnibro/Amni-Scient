EIGENSTRETCH TENSOR

CAUSAL-TOPOLOGICAL FINGERPRINT OF PARAMETER MANIFOLDS

A monitor for drift in the sensitivity pattern of a parameter search, recomputed at each checkpoint.

Status, 2026-09-09. The Laplacian and its eigendecomposition are standard and cited below; the trajectory graph, the stretch weights, and the digit encoding are my construction, with the 800 scale, the mode-to-dimension pairing, and the 0.9 and 0.7 correlation thresholds all hand-picked. Both charts on this page draw hand-set numbers so you can see the shapes; neither one runs a decomposition. The lead time between a digit spike and a score drop is something I've seen in my own optimizer runs but haven't logged, so there's no measured number on this page.

ABSTRACT

The Eigenstretch Tensor is the Reffelt Constant recomputed at every checkpoint of a parameter search so I can watch the digits move. The name is older than the math; what I actually compute is a vector of per-dimension stretch weights and a digit string, not a tensor. When the digit pattern shifts between checkpoints I read that as the score surface changing shape, and I use it as a flag to look at the run.

The graph is built from equity-curve similarity, where an equity curve is the running P&L of a strategy. I compare the whole output curve of one configuration to the whole output curve of another with an approximate Wasserstein-1 distance, so two configurations with very different parameter values still sit next to each other if they behave the same. That's the point: I want the graph to follow what the configurations do, not where they are.

CONSTRUCTION

STEP 1 — ELITE ARCHIVE SELECTION

From the population of evaluated configurations I keep the top N by fitness score. N=4096 is the size I planned for; there is no published implementation yet, so every constant on this page is a design choice. That keeps the spectral analysis on the region of parameter space I care about, which is the neighborhood of the best configurations.

STEP 2 — WASSERSTEIN k-NN GRAPH

I build a sparse k-nearest-neighbor graph over the archive with k=64 (a design choice), using an approximate Wasserstein-1 distance between output curves. For W1 I use the one-dimensional form: sort the values of each curve, and take the L1 distance between the two sorted cumulative curves. That's exact for 1-D distributions and it's the building block of sliced Wasserstein (Rabin, Peyré, Delon & Bernot, SSVM 2011). Edge weights also carry score similarity:

EQUATION E1 — TRAJECTORY SIMILARITY
$$w_{ij} = \frac{\exp\left(-\frac{|s_i - s_j|}{\sigma_s}\right)}{W_1(\text{traj}_i, \text{traj}_j) + \epsilon}$$

σs is the standard deviation of the scores across the archive and ε is a small constant I would set to keep the denominator away from zero. W1 compares the whole shape of two curves. Plain L2 on the samples also sees every point, but it punishes a time shift hard; W1 doesn't.

STEP 3 — LAPLACIAN EIGENDECOMPOSITION

I form the symmetric normalized Laplacian and take a rank-16 basis, where 16 is a design choice. The modes I want are the ones with the smallest nonzero eigenvalues, since those vary slowly across the graph. The current implementation gets its basis from a randomized SVD (Halko, Martinsson & Tropp, SIAM Review 53, 2011), which returns the top of the spectrum, and that's a known inconsistency I still have to fix; the right call is a shift-invert or a partial eigensolver on the small end.

EQUATION E2 — SPECTRAL BASIS EXTRACTION
$$L_{\text{norm}} = I - D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}, \qquad L_{\text{norm}}\psi_j = \lambda_j \psi_j, \qquad j = 1 \ldots 16$$

STEP 4 — PER-DIMENSION GRADIENT SENSITIVITY

For each parameter dimension d I sort the archive by that coordinate, take the finite difference of each eigenvector's entries between consecutive configurations in that order, and average the absolute value over the archive. πd is the sorting permutation:

EQUATION E3 — CAUSAL CONTRIBUTION VECTOR
$$g_{j,d} = \frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\left|\psi_j(\pi_d(i+1)) - \psi_j(\pi_d(i))\right|, \qquad \text{causal}_d = \frac{\sum_j g_{j,d}}{\sum_{d'} \sum_j g_{j,d'}}$$

The result is a vector with D entries that sum to one. Each entry says how much the smooth modes stretch along that parameter axis. That vector is the thing I keep and compare between checkpoints.

STEP 5 — DIGIT ENCODING

Digit j is eigenvalue j times a causal weight, scaled by 800, plus one, clamped to 1 through 8. Right now I pair mode j with dimension j mod D, which is a placeholder; the pairing isn't justified yet. A product over 1.0 prints as 9 so I can spot saturation, and a product under 0.01 prints as 0. The 800 is a hand-picked scale, same as on the Reffelt page.

EQUATION E4 — EIGENSTRETCH DIGITS
$$d_j = \operatorname{clamp}\left(\left\lfloor 800 \cdot \lambda_j \cdot \text{causal}_{j \bmod D} \right\rfloor + 1,\; 1,\; 8\right), \qquad d_j = 9 \text{ if } \lambda_j \text{causal}_{j \bmod D} > 1.0, \qquad d_j = 0 \text{ if } \lambda_j \text{causal}_{j \bmod D} < 0.01$$

The output is a 16-digit string using digits 0 to 9, with 0 and 9 reserved as flags.

DIAGNOSTIC SIGNALS

These are the four conditions I check at each checkpoint. Treat them as a checklist I wrote for myself, not as validated detectors.

OVERFITTING

In my optimizer runs the digits climb toward 8 and 9 shortly before the out-of-sample score drops. I haven't logged the lead time yet, so I'm not quoting one. When I see the climb I stop and look at the run.

REGIME CHANGE

If the digit pattern changes a lot between checkpoints, say from "84417588" to "35621844", I read that as the score surface having changed shape and the old best configurations no longer sitting in the same basin. I don't yet have a threshold for how much change counts, because I haven't measured how much the digits wander on their own.

DIMENSIONAL COLLAPSE

If several digits drop to 1 at once, the smooth modes have stopped moving along those dimensions. During a search that's what convergence looks like. After a search, on a deployed model, it would mean the model is losing sensitivity, and I haven't tested that case.

REFERENCE CORRELATION

I freeze a reference stretch vector at the start and take the correlation between it and the current one at each checkpoint. Above 0.9 I do nothing, below 0.7 I recompute the reference and look at the run. Both numbers are starting values I picked, not tuned.

THINGS I'D LIKE TO TRY

None of these have been run. I've used the digits on my own optimizer runs and nowhere else.

TRAINING STABILITY

I'd like to compute the stretch vector at checkpoint intervals during a training run and see whether a digit spike lines up with a learning-rate problem or with the start of overfitting, and whether a digit collapse lines up with mode collapse in a generative model. If it does, it could feed an early-stopping rule. Untested.

HYPERPARAMETER SWEEPS

Across a set of training runs with varied hyperparameters, the stretch vector should say which settings the quality depends on. The digit string has 16 digits, one per mode, and the stretch vector has one entry per hyperparameter; it's the vector, not the string, that would tell a team what to freeze. I haven't run this on anyone's sweep.

DRIFT ON A DEPLOYED MODEL

A monitor for drift in the sensitivity pattern of a parameter search, recomputed at each checkpoint. On a deployed model the idea would be to compare the current stretch vector against the one from training time and flag divergence. I haven't tried it on a deployed model, and I don't know how it compares with watching prediction confidence.

HARDWARE STRESS ENVELOPES

Untested idea. Run parameterized stress tests over clock, thermal, memory bandwidth and cache settings, compute the stretch vector over the results, and see whether the high-weight axes match the axes the hardware is actually bottlenecked on.

HOW TO REPRODUCE

There is no implementation of this page yet, on disk or published; the only "Reffelt" code I have is an unrelated base-17 addressing scheme in Amni-AI. Every number on this page is a design choice until a linked script says otherwise. The two charts above are the only runnable pieces on this page and both draw hand-set values. To rebuild the pipeline from the text: keep the top N configurations by score, compute the 1-D W1 distance between every pair of output curves, build the k-NN graph with the E1 weights, form the symmetric normalized Laplacian, take the 16 smallest nonzero eigenpairs, compute the sorted finite-difference weights from E3, and encode with E4. Then repeat at each checkpoint and compare the stretch vectors. The 800 scale, the j mod D pairing, and the 0.9 and 0.7 thresholds are the places where you'd be copying my guesses.

RELATED WORK

The job this page is trying to do, which is flagging a change in a stream of checkpoints, already has established tools. ADWIN (Bifet & Gavaldà, SDM 2007) keeps an adaptive window and flags when the two halves disagree. The Page-Hinkley test is a cumulative-sum test for a shift in the mean. For the overfitting case specifically, Prechelt's early-stopping criteria (“Early Stopping, But When?”, 1998) are the standard baseline. I haven't run any of them against the digits yet, and until I do I can't say the digits add anything.
