HOLOGRAPHIC RESISTANCE MEMBRANE

GPU-ACCELERATED PARTICLE DYNAMICS ON FRICTION FIELDS

Holographic stretch deformation generates resistance landscapes. Particles advect through the field to produce probabilistic density maps of system evolution.

Status, 2026-09-09. Nothing on this page is derived from first principles. Equations M1 to M6 are definitions I wrote down, with weights I picked by eye. The only thing that runs is the canvas demo below, which is a small JavaScript version of M4 to M6 with two extra terms I list in its caption. The Taichi build exists on my machine but isn't published, and I haven't benchmarked it or tested its density maps against real outcomes.

ABSTRACT

The Holographic Resistance Membrane models a system as particles flowing through a friction landscape. The "membrane" is a 2D resistance field I compute from three maps: spatial gradient magnitude, frame-to-frame change, and a curl-like warp term. I drop a few thousand particles on it and step them with damped semi-implicit Euler in Taichi, with friction set by the local resistance.

The output is a density map. Where particles pile up is where the model says the system is likely to go. I haven't tested that against real outcomes yet. What I can say is that the map is a distribution rather than a single point, so if the particles split into two piles you see both.

MEMBRANE CONSTRUCTION

HOLOGRAPHIC STRETCH FIELD

I build the stretch field H as a weighted blend of three normalized maps: gradient magnitude of f, its frame-to-frame change T, and a warp term W.

EQUATION M1 — HOLOGRAPHIC STRETCH
$$H(x,y) = 0.4\sqrt{(\partial_x f)^2 + (\partial_y f)^2} + 0.4 \cdot T(x,y) + 0.2 \cdot W(x,y)$$

Here f is the scalar state field on the grid. T is |f at this frame minus f at the last frame|, and W is a curl-like finite difference on the gradient field: I take $\mathbf{g} = \nabla f$ with the grid's forward differences, then set $W = |\partial_x g_y - \partial_y g_x|$ with backward differences. For a smooth scalar that's zero on paper; on the grid it isn't, and what it picks up is kinks and noise in f. So W is a roughness measure, not a vorticity, and "turbulence warping" was the wrong name for it. Each of the three maps is divided by its own maximum over the grid so it sits in [0,1] before I blend them. The weights 0.4/0.4/0.2 are ones I picked by eye; they aren't derived and I haven't fitted them to anything yet.

FRICTION FIELD DEFINITION

I define the resistance field from the local variance of H:

EQUATION M2 — FRICTION FIELD
$$R(x,y) = 0.8 \cdot \frac{\text{Var}_{\text{local}}[H]}{\max(\text{Var}_{\text{local}}[H])} + 0.2 \cdot R_{\text{boundary}}(x,y)$$

Varlocal is the variance of H over a square window around each cell; the window size is set in code, not on this page. Rboundary is a ramp that rises from 0 in the interior to 1 at the grid edge so particles slow down before they leave the field. The 0.8/0.2 split is a choice I made by eye, same as M1. The idea is that where H is choppy, particles should struggle, and where H is smooth they should run free. I haven't checked that this variance is the right choppiness measure.

STEERING FIELD

An optional bias field for known drift or external forcing:

EQUATION M3 — STEERING
$$\mathbf{F}_{\text{steer}}(x,y) = [0, \; S(x,y) \times 10]$$

S(x,y) is whatever scalar bias I feed in, in [-1,1]; in my runs it's a momentum indicator sampled onto the grid. The factor of 10 is a gain I picked so steering is visible next to the gradient force. It isn't derived. The steering force is added to the gradient force in M4.

PARTICLE DYNAMICS

I put n particles on the membrane and move each one with this force:

EQUATION M4 — FORCE LAW
$$\mathbf{F}_i = -\nabla R(\mathbf{x}_i) \cdot \alpha + \mathbf{F}_{\text{steer}}(\mathbf{x}_i)$$

α = 5.0 is the gradient gain. I picked it by eye so particles move at a pace I could watch; it isn't derived and I haven't swept it.

EQUATION M5 — DAMPED SEMI-IMPLICIT EULER
$$\mathbf{v}_i^{n+1} = (\mathbf{v}_i^n + \mathbf{F}_i \cdot \Delta t) \cdot \gamma \qquad \mathbf{x}_i^{n+1} = \mathbf{x}_i^n + \mathbf{v}_i^{n+1} \cdot \Delta t$$

Each step I add the force to the velocity, multiply by a damping factor γ, then move the particle. That's semi-implicit Euler with damping, not true Verlet. γ is 0.99 in the Taichi build and 0.97 in the demo below; both are values I picked by eye. Without the γ multiplier this is the symplectic Euler scheme in Hairer, Lubich and Wanner, Geometric Numerical Integration (Springer, 2006), ch. I.1; with it, the scheme loses that structure and just bleeds energy every step.

EQUATION M6 — DENSITY DEPOSITION
$$P(x,y) = \frac{\sum_i E_i \, \mathbb{1}[\mathbf{x}_i \in \text{cell}(x,y)]}{\sum_i E_i}$$

Each particle adds its energy Ei to the count of the grid cell it's in; P is that binned count divided by the total, so it sums to 1 over the grid. The indicator is just binning to the nearest cell, nothing finer. Cells where many high-energy particles sit come out as peaks.

GPU FIELD LAYOUT

All fields are stored as Taichi GPU tensors for parallel computation:

FIELDTYPESHAPEPURPOSE
resistancef32 scalar(res, res)Friction landscape R(x,y)
particlesf32 vec2(n_particles,)Position xi
velocitiesf32 vec2(n_particles,)Velocity vi
energiesf32 scalar(n_particles,)Carried energy Ei
steering_fieldf32 scalar(res, res)Directional bias S(x,y)
probability_mapf32 scalar(res, res)Output density P(x,y)

I haven't benchmarked this yet. When I do, I'll post the script and the numbers for my 9060 XT here.

WHERE I'D LIKE TO TRY IT

Everything in this section is conjecture. I haven't run the membrane on any of these problems, and I don't know yet whether a damped particle on a friction potential says anything useful about them.

💻 GPU WORKLOAD SCHEDULING

I think the membrane could model a cluster as a friction landscape, busy nodes high, idle nodes low, with jobs as particles that drift toward the low spots. I haven't tested it. I don't know if it would beat a plain queue, and I'd want to measure that before saying anything more.

⚛ BEAM DYNAMICS

I think the pieces line up with an accelerator picture: resistance as impedance and space charge, steering as dipole and quadrupole kicks, particle energy as beam energy. I haven't tested it. Real beam codes solve fields self-consistently and this doesn't, so at best it would be a sketch tool.

🌊 FLUID-LIKE FLOWS

I think the variance-based friction might act like a crude local viscosity. I haven't tested it. To be clear about what it is: a damped particle on a potential, which is much simpler than SPH (Monaghan, Ann. Rev. Astron. Astrophys. 30, 1992). There's no pressure, no continuity, and no closure here.

🛰 AI INFERENCE ROUTING

I think the membrane could stand in for a router, with compute as resistance, request size as particle energy, and model specialization as steering. I haven't tested it, and I have no throughput numbers against round-robin or hashing.

🚀 TRAJECTORY SEARCH

I think the resistance field could carry a gravitational potential plus drag and let a few thousand particles sniff out cheap corridors. I haven't tested it. It wouldn't replace a Lambert solver; if it works at all it would only point at regions worth handing to one.

HOW TO REPRODUCE

The Taichi source isn't published yet. The canvas demo above is the only runnable piece on this page; view source and you'll find it in the last script block, about eighty lines with no dependencies. It uses the same M4 to M6 update as the Taichi build, with the two extra terms listed in the caption, on a 60×40 procedural field instead of a computed H.
