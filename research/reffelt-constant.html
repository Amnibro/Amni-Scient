THE REFFELT CONSTANT

SPECTRAL FINGERPRINTING OF HIGH-DIMENSIONAL PARAMETER SPACES

A digit-string encoding of the low-eigenvalue subspace of a graph Laplacian, weighted by per-dimension sensitivity.

Status, 2026-09-09. The graph, the normalized Laplacian, and the eigendecomposition on this page are standard spectral graph theory and I cite the sources below. The digit encoding and the causal weights are my own construction, and the 800 scale, the mode-to-dimension pairing, and the 0.01 and 1.0 thresholds are hand-picked placeholders. The only thing that runs on this page is the digit encoder in the explorer, which takes eigenvalues and weights you type in; it doesn't build a graph. None of the application ideas below have been measured.

ABSTRACT

The Reffelt Constant (ℜ) is a short digit string I compute from a set of evaluated configurations in a D-dimensional parameter space. I build a graph over the configurations, take the low-eigenvalue modes of its normalized Laplacian, weight each mode by how much it moves along each parameter axis, and print one digit per retained mode. I use the string to see which dimensions the score depends on and which ones I can freeze.

When I say ground state on this page I mean the low-eigenvalue subspace of the Laplacian. That's an analogy to a physical ground state, and it stops there: there's no temperature and no partition function in the math.

CORE DERIVATION

STEP 1 — SCORE-WEIGHTED GRAPH CONSTRUCTION

I start with N evaluated parameter vectors pi ∈ ℝD and their fitness scores si. I rescale every coordinate to [0,1] first, so the distance d(pi,pj) is a Euclidean distance between normalized points and ε has no units to worry about. Then I build a k-nearest-neighbor graph, with k set in code, and weight each edge by distance and by score similarity:

EQUATION R1 — GRAPH WEIGHT
$$w_{ij} = \frac{\exp\left(-\frac{|s_i - s_j|}{\sigma_s}\right)}{d(p_i, p_j) + \epsilon}$$

σs is the standard deviation of the scores across the population and ε = 0.1 keeps the denominator away from zero. Two points that sit close together and score about the same get a heavy edge. Two points that sit close together but score very differently get a light one, so the graph follows the score surface and not only the geometry. The normalized Laplacian I use next is standard; see Chung, Spectral Graph Theory (1997) and von Luxburg, “A tutorial on spectral clustering,” Stat. Comput. 17 (2007).

STEP 2 — NORMALIZED GRAPH LAPLACIAN

From the adjacency matrix A with weights wij and degree matrix D = diag(Σjwij), I compute the symmetric normalized Laplacian:

EQUATION R2 — NORMALIZED LAPLACIAN
$$L_{\text{norm}} = I - D^{-1/2} A D^{-1/2}$$

The eigenvalues of Lnorm lie in [0, 2]. Small eigenvalues belong to modes that vary slowly across the graph, which is the global structure I want. Large eigenvalues belong to modes that flip sign between neighbors, which is local noise.

STEP 3 — SPECTRAL DECOMPOSITION

Take the k smallest nonzero eigenvalues of Lnorm and their eigenvectors. Those are the smooth modes.

EQUATION R3 — SPECTRAL BASIS
$$L_{\text{norm}} \psi_j = \lambda_j \psi_j, \qquad 0 < \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \cdots \le \lambda_k$$

Each ψj is a vector with one entry per configuration in the archive. The explorer further down doesn't decompose anything; it takes eigenvalues you type in, so there's no sort order to worry about there.

STEP 4 — CAUSAL GRADIENT SENSITIVITY

ψj lives on graph nodes, so there's no derivative along a coordinate axis to take directly. What I do instead, for each dimension d: sort the archive by coordinate d, take the finite difference of the ψj entries between consecutive configurations in that ordering, and average the absolute value. I sum that over the k modes and normalize across dimensions so the weights add to one:

EQUATION R4 — CAUSAL WEIGHTS
$$g_{j,d} = \frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\left|\psi_j(\pi_d(i+1)) - \psi_j(\pi_d(i))\right|, \qquad \text{causal}_d = \frac{\sum_j g_{j,d}}{\sum_{d'} \sum_j g_{j,d'}}$$

πd is the permutation that sorts the archive by coordinate d. A dimension with a large weight is one the smooth modes move along. A dimension with a weight near zero is one they ignore, and that's the one I freeze. Note that this gives me one weight per dimension d, and the digit in Step 5 is indexed by mode j. Pairing mode j with dimension j is a placeholder; I haven't worked out a justified pairing yet.

STEP 5 — DIGIT ENCODING

I take the eigenvalue of mode j and multiply it by the causal weight of dimension j. I scale that product by 800, add one, and clamp to 1 through 8. The 800 is a scale I picked by hand so that products from my runs land in the middle of the range. It isn't derived from anything. A product under 0.01 gets a 0 and a product over 1.0 gets a 9, so I can see dead and saturated dimensions at a glance.

EQUATION R5 — THE REFFELT CONSTANT
$$\Re_j = \operatorname{clamp}\left(\lfloor 800 \cdot \lambda_j \cdot \text{causal}_j \rfloor + 1,\; 1,\; 8\right), \qquad \Re_j = 0 \text{ if } \lambda_j \text{causal}_j < 0.01, \qquad \Re_j = 9 \text{ if } \lambda_j \text{causal}_j > 1.0$$

In code: p = abs(eigenvalue * causal_weight); digit = 0 if p < 0.01 else 9 if p > 1.0 else min(8, max(1, int(800 * p) + 1))

VALIDITY RULES

Two digit values are reserved as flags. Both thresholds are on the raw product λj × causalj, before the 800 scale:

DIGIT VALUECONDITIONWHAT I DO
1 – 80.01 ≤ product ≤ 1.0Keep the dimension in the search
0product < 0.01The smooth modes don't move along this dimension. Freeze it.
9product > 1.0The product is off the scale. I rescale or resample and recompute.

I call a constant valid when it has no 0 and no 9. That's a convention I use, not a law. When a 0 shows up I read it as a dimension I haven't sampled enough or one that doesn't matter; when a 9 shows up I read it as a product that has outgrown the hand-picked 800 scale.

PROPERTIES

LENGTH

The constant has one digit per retained mode, so its length is k from Step 3, and I choose k. It says nothing by itself about how many dimensions matter; that comes from counting the 0 digits. The explorer's DIMENSIONS control sets the same k.

WALK-FORWARD STABILITY

I haven't measured walk-forward stability yet. When I do, the dataset and the script will be linked here.

REGIME DIAGNOSTIC

If the digit pattern changes between two archives from the same system, I'd read that as the score surface having changed shape. I haven't tested how often the digits change on their own from sampling noise, so I don't know yet how big a change is meaningful.

DIGIT ENCODING

The digits run 0 to 9, with 0 and 9 reserved as flags. I picked digits because I can read them off a log line and say them out loud.

THINGS I'D LIKE TO TRY

None of these have been run. They're the places I think the constant could earn its keep, written down so I remember to test them.

NEURAL ARCHITECTURE SEARCH

An architecture search space has a lot of knobs and I'd like to know which ones the score depends on after a first sweep. The plan is to compute the constant on the sweep, freeze the dimensions that print a 0, and see whether the next sweep finds the same architectures with fewer evaluations. I haven't run this.

WORKLOAD FINGERPRINTING

I'd like to compute the constant on per-node resource traces and see whether two workloads that print the same string behave the same when I move them between machines. I don't know yet whether the string is stable enough to use as a key, so that's the first thing to check.

HYPERPARAMETER OPTIMIZATION

The idea is to compute the constant after an initial exploration phase, freeze the dimensions with 0 digits at their best-seen values, and spend the rest of the budget on the dimensions that print high digits. I expect this to cut evaluations, but I haven't run the comparison. No number until I do.

MOLECULAR DESCRIPTOR SCREENING

QSAR descriptor spaces are wide and most of the descriptors are correlated. I'd like to compute the constant over a descriptor set with binding affinity as the score and see how many dimensions survive. I don't have a number for that.

TRAINING DIAGNOSTICS

I'd like to compute the constant at checkpoints across a set of training runs with varied hyperparameters and watch which digits move as training goes on. If the pattern shifts before a run goes bad, that's useful. Whether it does is untested.

OUTPUT STRUCTURE

The computation returns a record with these fields. The string has k digits, one per retained mode:

RESULT SCHEMA
constant:              str              ← the ℜ string, k digits (e.g. "84417588")
eigenvalues:           List[float]      ← the k smallest nonzero eigenvalues (λ1...λk)
causal_weights:        List[float]      ← per-dimension weight, D entries, sum=1
effective_dim:         int              ← D minus the count of dimensions I froze
ground_state_params:   Dict[str,float]  ← best-seen values for the frozen dimensions
free_params:           List[str]        ← dimensions I keep tuning
frozen_params:         Dict[str,float]  ← dimensions I fixed
compression_ratio:     float            ← D / effective_dim
walk_forward_degradation: float         ← placeholder, not measured yet

HOW TO REPRODUCE

There is no implementation of this page yet, on disk or published; the only "Reffelt" code I have is an unrelated base-17 addressing scheme in Amni-AI. The explorer above is the one runnable piece, and every other number here is a design choice until a linked script says otherwise. The digit encoder in the explorer above is the only runnable piece on this page, and it starts from eigenvalues and weights you supply. If you want to rebuild the pipeline from the text: normalize the coordinates, build a k-NN graph with the R1 weights, form the symmetric normalized Laplacian, take the k smallest nonzero eigenpairs, compute the sorted finite-difference weights from R4, and encode with R5. The 800 scale and the mode-to-dimension pairing are the two places where you'd be copying my guesses.

RELATED WORK

The graph and the Laplacian are the same objects as in Laplacian eigenmaps (Belkin & Niyogi, Neural Computation 15, 2003) and diffusion maps (Coifman & Lafon, Appl. Comput. Harmon. Anal. 21, 2006); both keep the low-eigenvalue modes for the same reason I do. The per-dimension weights in R4 are a crude sensitivity measure, and the established tools for that job are Sobol indices, the Morris method, and, for hyperparameter importance specifically, fANOVA (Hutter, Hoos & Leyton-Brown, ICML 2014). I haven't compared my weights against any of them yet.
