HOSTILITY AGAINST HOUSES OF WORSHIP

FBI COUNTS THROUGH OCTOBER 2026

The FBI file refreshed on November 15, 2026 runs through October. FB-ISAO’s latest attack total is still calendar 2025. Family Research Council’s church file still ends in 2024.

From January through October 2026 the FBI file holds 1,932 single-bias religious incidents, 1,071 of them anti-Jewish. The same ten months of 2025 hold 2,057 and 1,311. Those 2026 months are still being filed: population coverage is 91% in January and 56% in October. The last complete FBI year is 2025, with 2,408 religious-bias incidents. FB-ISAO’s latest attack total is 915 in 2025. Family Research Council’s latest church file is still calendar 2024, with 415 incidents at 383 churches. Neither has published a 2026 total.

What the sources record

Perpetrator religion, political party, medication, and immigration status are absent from the Family Research Council incident file, from the FBI religion tables, and from both FB-ISAO year-in-review summaries. The state charts stay on the 2024 church file, joined to the 2024 resident population, the 2023 poverty rate, and the 2023 foreign-born share. Those are area-level associations. They do not identify a cause. The foreign-born share is a stock of residents, not an immigration rate in the weeks before an incident.

2026, through October

These counts come from the FBI Crime Data Explorer national hate-crime file, refreshed November 15, 2026. January through October sum to 8,512 incidents of every bias. The religious rows below are the single-bias slice and sum to 1,932. The same window in 2025, read from this same file, is 2,057 religious-bias incidents, including 1,311 anti-Jewish, 171 anti-Islamic, 150 anti-Sikh, 63 anti-Catholic, 48 anti-Protestant, 21 anti-Hindu, and 16 anti-Buddhist. Each 2025 month in that window covers about 97% of the population. The 2026 window covers 91% in January, 87% in June, 78% in September, and 56% in October, so a lower 2026 bar is not yet a finished decline. November is in the file and is not drawn: on the November 15 refresh it covers 21% of the population, and the month was not over.

Anti-Islamic incidents in the window are 215 against 171 a year earlier. Anti-Sikh incidents are 189 against 150. Anti-Hindu incidents are 21 in both windows. Anti-Buddhist incidents are 33 against 16. FB-ISAO has not published a 2026 attack total, and Family Research Council has not published a 2025 or 2026 church file, so those charts stay on their latest closed years.

Churches, by year

Family Research Council revised the 2020–2023 totals upward in the August 2025 edition after more open-source incidents were identified. The bars use the revised series: 50, 83, 55, 98, 198, 485, 415. That edition is still the latest church report. The count is incidents found in news and public reports. It is not a census of offenses, and underreporting is expected. The revised series sums to 1,384 incidents from January 2018 through December 2024, and to 1,196 from 2021 through 2024.

2024 church incidents by type

Vandalism is 284 of 415 incidents. Arson, including attempts and fires of unknown cause, is 55. Gun-related incidents are 28, up from 12 in 2023. Bomb threats are 14. Other aggression is 47. Thirteen incidents carry two marks, so the five marks sum to 428.

Each bar is a month of 2024. The tallest bar is June.

June is the high month in this file. September through November are the low months. About 22% of the June incidents were described as related to LGBT issues in the Family Research Council narrative. That share is not a column in the spreadsheet.

States

Larger states contribute more incidents. The correlation of the incident count with the log of the July 1, 2024 population is 0.74 across 50 states and the District of Columbia, with seven jurisdictions at zero. California has the most incidents (40) and a rate of 0.10 per 100,000 residents. West Virginia has the highest rate in this file, 15 incidents and 0.85 per 100,000. Hawaii, Idaho, Montana, Nevada, New Mexico, Wisconsin, and Wyoming have no row in the 2024 file.

Per 100,000 residents, the correlation with the poverty rate is 0.21 and the correlation with the foreign-born share is −0.20. The raw incident count correlates 0.16 with poverty and 0.47 with the foreign-born share. The count association with foreign-born share shrinks and changes sign once population is in the denominator, which is what a size confound looks like. Reporting effort, media coverage, and the open-source search also vary by state. None of these coefficients is a cause, and none of them is a perpetrator’s immigration status.

Identified themes in the church file

Most 2024 incidents have an unknown perpetrator and no confirmed theme. The counts below are the themes Family Research Council published. They are not a partition of the 415, and a 2022 anti-LGBTQ+ count is not in the 2025 narrative.

Houses of worship, any faith

FB-ISAO’s 2025 review counts 915 attacks on U.S. houses of worship, an 8.8% increase from 841 in 2024 and below the 1,030 it now prints for 2023. The 2024 review had printed 1,027 for 2023. Incidents with casualties rose from 69 in 2024 to 111 in 2025, with 48 people killed and 154 wounded. In 2025, 63% of attacks were at Protestant churches, 14% at Catholic churches, 12% at synagogues, and 5% at mosques. Latter-day Saint meetinghouses were 2%. Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, Quaker, and Orthodox sites were each under 2%, so this chart does not draw them as a fifth bar. The stock comparison is the 2021 Duke National Congregations Study share that FB-ISAO cites: 85% Protestant, 6% Catholic, 3.2% Jewish, and under 1% Muslim, printed here as 0.7%.

Each bar is FB-ISAO’s later count of attacks. The 2025 review prints 1,030 for 2023; the 2024 review printed 1,027.

FBI religious-bias hate crimes

These are incidents that participating agencies submitted as motivated by religious bias. A victim can be a person, a religious organization, or the public. The series is not a count of attacks on buildings, and agency participation is voluntary. The FBI released the 2025 file on August 14, 2026: 10,881 hate-crime incidents in all, from 16,791 agencies covering about 95% of the population. The religious rows below sum to 2,408.

Motives in 2025, and who could be classified in 2024

The 2025 review prints two motive shares for its 915 attacks: hate or bias 32%, and criminal 36%. Mischief, interpersonal or doctrinal disputes, accidents, and single-issue activity make up the rest, and those remaining shares are not printed separately. About 70% of the motive calls rest on direct evidence, a confession, or a later investigation. About 30% are inferred from the incident. Hate or bias is described as the stronger factor at synagogues, mosques, and Hindu temples. Theft is described as the leading motive at Protestant churches. Party, religion, medication, and immigration status of the person who did it are still not fields in the review.

Published shares of 2025 house-of-worship attacks. The two bars are not a complete partition.

Mental illness in that summary is not a medication record. The 2025 review does not reprint this split.
