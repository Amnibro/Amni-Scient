PROPRIETARY WEIGHT SPLIT — 1-BIT INDEX IN VRAM, EXACT RESIDUAL ON NVMe

Railgun keeps a 1-bit routing index in VRAM (1.21 GB for Qwen3.5-9B) and stores the exact fp16 residual on NVMe. The residual as I store it today is bigger than the original (1.75× the fp16 bytes, with an entropy of about 12.5 bits per weight), so this isn’t 16× compression; it’s a 16× smaller resident footprint with a bit-exact recovery path. Inference on the 1-bit tier alone is lossy and I haven’t measured its quality yet.

THE PROBLEM

The thing I keep running into is GPU memory. A 70-billion parameter model needs 140 GB of VRAM at fp16 just to load, which is almost double what one NVIDIA H100 has, so you’re paying for two cards (around $30,000 each at list, my assumption throughout this page) before a single token comes out.

The usual fix is GPTQ, AWQ, or GGML: round every weight to 4 bits and accept the quality loss for good. Bit-exact compressors like zstd, ZipNN, and DFloat11 keep the weights intact and shrink the file, but the resident footprint stays at or near the fp16 size (DFloat11 lands around 11 bits/weight with on-GPU decode).

What I wanted was a format where the resident part is tiny and the rest can be pulled back exactly when it’s needed. The cards below are the numbers behind that framing; the first two are assumptions, the last two are measured.

THE RAILGUN SOLUTION

Railgun splits each weight tensor into a 1-bit routing index that stays in VRAM and a residual that goes to NVMe. Decoding the index alone gives a lossy approximation; decoding index plus residual gives back the exact fp16 bytes.

Bars are Qwen3.5-9B: 1.21 GB VRAM against 19.31 GB fp16 (Proof 2), NVMe bytes 1.75× the fp16 bytes on the sampled tensors (Proof 7), full decode bit-exact on every tensor I’ve tried and byte-identical outputs on a full 0.5B model (Proof 3). The intermediate G and B tiers described in the white paper aren’t measured on this page.

WHAT’S DIFFERENT

BIT-EXACT RECOVERY (MEASURED)

At full tier the decoded weights are bit-exact with the original fp16; measured on 290/290 layers of a 0.5B model with byte-identical greedy outputs (Proof 3). zstd, ZipNN, and DFloat11 are also bit-exact and produce a smaller total store than my current residual; what they don’t do is drop the resident footprint to 1 bit per weight.

RESIDUE ARITHMETIC (BY CONSTRUCTION)

The encoding uses [PROPRIETARY ALGEBRAIC MECHANISM], and residues in a finite field don’t accumulate rounding error because there’s nothing to round. I ran 20 million ops and 10,000 pack/unpack cycles and got max error 0 (Proof 6). That’s a property of the container; it says nothing about attention arithmetic, which Railgun doesn’t touch.

MULTIPLEXING (ALLOCATION ONLY)

Three 9B routing tiers fit in 3.62 GB on my 16 GB card (Proof 5). Scaling that to “four 70B models per H100” is arithmetic on the 1-bit footprint, not something I’ve run, and it ignores the NVMe bandwidth those models would share.

SSD FABRIC (DESIGN IDEA)

If the residual lives on shared storage, any GPU could pull any model’s data over NVMe-oF or CXL. I haven’t built or measured any of this; it’s where I’d take the format next.

SILICON PATH (DESIGN, NOT MEASURED)

Every modern GPU has dedicated [CLASSIFIED HARDWARE UNITS] and a [DEDICATED CACHE PATHWAY] that mostly sit idle during transformer inference. The encoding format is laid out so that path could do the tier decode off the ALU pipeline.

Nothing in this section is measured. My current decoder runs as ordinary PyTorch ops and decodes before the matmul rather than fusing with it; the diagram and the three cards below are what I intend to build, and the numbers on them are estimates.

COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

The 4-bit formats trade precision for a smaller resident footprint and don’t give it back. Bit-exact compressors (zstd, ZipNN, DFloat11) give it back and produce a smaller total store than my current residual, but their resident footprint stays near fp16. Railgun’s position is a 1-bit resident tier plus a bit-exact recovery path, at the cost of a total store that’s currently larger than fp16. Column entries marked untested are design intent.

RESIDUE GRADIENT ACCUMULATION (UNTESTED)

Residue accumulation inside the encoding is exact within the [ALGEBRAIC STRUCTURE]. Whether that helps a training loop depends on mapping real-valued gradients into and out of the field without losing what matters, which I haven’t tried. Untested.

BOUNDED WEIGHT EVOLUTION

Each weight element maps to one of a [BOUNDED STATE SPACE], so a single-element mutation has a finite set of outcomes you could enumerate. That’s the appeal for evolutionary search. I haven’t run a search, so I don’t know whether the enumerable moves are the useful ones.

[CLASSIFIED]-SPACE CHECKPOINTING

Model state as [CLASSIFIED FORMAT], checkpoints as [CLASSIFIED] snapshots. The resident part would be 16× smaller; the full bit-exact checkpoint is 1.75× fp16 as I store it today, so a checkpoint isn’t smaller unless the residual gets entropy-coded (about 12.5 bits/weight by entropy, Proof R+I). Untested.

EVOLUTIONARY MODEL SEARCH

Combine encoding-space mutations with fitness selection, graft layers between models, try hybrid architectures, all in a [CLASSIFIED ALGEBRAIC DOMAIN] where a mutation can be undone exactly. I have no convergence result and no experiment; this is a plan.

In a [CLASSIFIED ALGEBRAIC STRUCTURE] each weight element has a [BOUNDED STATE SPACE], so the single-element moves can be listed and each one undone exactly. Whether that search converges on anything useful is an open question I haven’t tested, and I’m not claiming a proof.

Output text at the 1-bit tier is not shown because it’s not a quality benchmark.
