ABSTRACT

This page covers a family of quantization codecs I built on arithmetic in the Galois field GF(17) for compressing neural network weight matrices. Instead of working in floating point or a power-of-two integer grid, I map weights into a prime field where addition, multiplication, and inversion are exact residues.

There are five codecs spanning 2-bit to 16-bit operating points: GF(17) Legendre 4-bit, Contextual GF(17), Membrane GF(17), ATRP, and AmniTex Progressive. The Membrane GF(17) codec, which comes out of my Holographic Resistance Membrane framework, measures cosine similarity 0.939 at its 4-bit operating point on a synthetic heavy-tail tensor, against 0.373 for the uniform GF(17) Legendre codec in the same table (about 2.5×), and encodes in 17 ms on CPU via a [PROPRIETARY ACCELERATION TECHNIQUE].

I compare against Google’s TurboQuant (ICLR 2026) and the related QJL and PolarQuant papers [1–3]. TurboQuant is built for online KV-cache compression and comes with distortion bounds; my GF(17) codecs are for persistent weight storage and give exact arithmetic, [CLASSIFIED HARDWARE ACCELERATION], and progressive decoding. The TurboQuant numbers here come from my re-implementation, so read them as a sanity check on my own codecs rather than a verdict on theirs.

WHY GF(17)?

I use p = 17 because it’s the smallest prime above 16, so four GF(17) digits (83,521 states) cover every fp16 bit pattern (65,536). The 17 values {0, …, 16} form a complete field where every non-zero element has a multiplicative inverse. The cost is that 17 symbols don’t fit in 4 bits (log2 17 is about 4.09); the packing I use is proprietary ([ALGEBRAIC PARTITIONING METHOD]), and the bits/element figures in the tables are the measured packed size.

EXACT ARITHMETIC

Add, multiply, and invert are exact in mod-17. There’s nothing to round, so a chain of field operations can’t accumulate rounding error.

HARDWARE ACCELERATION

Values 0–16 map to a [CLASSIFIED HARDWARE FORMAT] so that GPU [CLASSIFIED HARDWARE UNITS] can do [CLASSIFIED OPERATION] off the ALU path. This is the design intent; I haven’t measured it on a GPU yet.

PROGRESSIVE REFINEMENT

Multi-layer residuals compose coherently in the same field. Decode at any layer for quality/speed tradeoff — from 2-bit preview to 16-bit exact.

EXACT RESIDUE SUMS

Cumulative sums cycle within {0, …, 16}, so a residue sum stays exact at any length. That’s a property of the field, and it says nothing about recovering the real-valued sum; see the Cumulative Drift section for what I did and didn’t test.

TurboQuant’s random rotation spreads tail energy across all coordinates, which is why my re-implementation barely moves between the two distributions (Δ = 0.001). On this synthetic pair, TurboQuant is still the more robust of the two.

CUMULATIVE DRIFT

Sums in GF(17) are exact residues; they don’t accumulate rounding error because there’s nothing to round. That’s not the same as recovering the real-valued sum, and production kernels already accumulate in fp32, so I don’t claim this beats them. LIMITATIONS

ENCODE OVERHEAD

Membrane GF(17) encodes at 17ms (CPU). TurboQuant reports “negligible” runtime overhead on H100 GPUs. Direct speed comparison is not meaningful across CPU vs. datacenter GPU.

DISTRIBUTION SENSITIVITY

Base GF(17) Legendre collapses on heavy-tail data (CosSim 0.37). The Membrane variant solves this but at higher encode cost. TurboQuant’s rotation preprocessing handles all distributions uniformly by design.

THEORETICAL GAP

TurboQuant has formal proofs of near-optimal distortion rates within constant factor of information-theoretic lower bounds. GF(17) codecs are empirically validated but lack equivalent formal analysis.

INFERENCE INTEGRATION

TurboQuant integrates directly into attention computation. GF(17) weight quantization requires dequantization before standard compute, unless [CLASSIFIED HARDWARE ACCELERATION] is used.

SYNTHETIC DATA ONLY

Every quality number here is from 896×896 synthetic tensors. No real model weights, no perplexity, no downstream task. My later work on other codecs showed synthetic tensors can flatter a codec, so I’d expect these numbers to move on real weights.

HOW TO REPRODUCE

The benchmark scripts and codec implementations aren’t published. The setup was CPU-only, Python 3.14, NumPy 2.4.3, normal and Student-t heavy-tail draws at 896×896. If you want to check a number, email me and I’ll run it with you under NDA.
