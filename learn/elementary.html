A LOOK AT THIS CATEGORY

Eleven games spanning reaction time, mental math, basic programming logic, classification, vocabulary, working memory, pattern recognition, geography, and casual puzzle play. The pack is sized so a child can rotate through several activities in a typical 20-minute session and still encounter variety the next day.

THE LEARNING BEHIND IT

Reflex Racer measures simple reaction time, building competitive baselines kids can chase day-over-day. Math Mountain stages arithmetic at increasing difficulty as the climb progresses. Block Mover introduces sequential commands (move, turn, repeat) — the conceptual root of programming. Sorting Hat and Word Bridge cover classification and translation respectively. Memory Sequence is the classic Simon-style pattern-repeat. Pattern Puzzle and Color Sort develop visual reasoning. Geography Explorer pairs flags with country names. Gem Crush is the match-3 mechanic, included as a low-stakes reward activity.

A Balanced 30-Minute Elementary Session

  1. 1Open with Reflex Racer or Memory Sequence (3 min). Brief warm-up that engages attention without taxing cognition.
  2. 2Move to Math Mountain (8 min). Climb 3–5 levels at the child's current arithmetic edge. Stopping at a level they can't pass yet is fine; it identifies the next teaching target.
  3. 3Block Mover (8 min). The sequential-command logic is the precursor to real programming. Children who play this regularly enter actual code instruction (Scratch, Python) with measurable head-start.
  4. 4Word Bridge or Word Scramble (5 min). Vocabulary games rotate; pick whichever child currently finds engaging.
  5. 5Geography Explorer or Sorting Hat closer (4 min). Classification and recognition games are good wind-down activities that still keep attention engaged.
  6. 6Save Gem Crush for explicit reward time (2 min). Treat it as the "dessert" of the session rather than the main course.

Skills Built At Each Age

  • Age 6Reaction time stabilizes (median ~350 ms). Can sort items by 1–2 visible properties. Reads CVC and short sight words. First sequential commands (forward, turn) understood.
  • Age 7Math fact recall to 20 fluent. Can plan 2–3 step programming sequences. Recognizes patterns of length 4–6.
  • Age 8Multiplication facts beginning. Geographic knowledge: continents and major countries. Programming with loops (forward 4 times).
  • Age 9Division and decimal numbers. Country-and-capital recognition for major nations. Conditional logic (if-then) in Block Mover.
  • Age 10Ready for STEM Labs and Brain Puzzles. Most Elementary Pack content becomes too easy; rotate to harder material.

Pitfalls At This Age

  • Mixed-difficulty sessionsChildren at this age handle one difficulty band per session. Mixing easy and hard activities in the same 30 minutes creates morale swings (frustration after a hard one, boredom after an easy one). Stay within one band per session.
  • Reaction-time fixationReflex Racer scores improve rapidly with practice but plateau quickly. Children who play it repeatedly to chase the high score don't get more learning from each session past 5–10 reps. Move on after the plateau hits.
  • Skipping Block Mover because "coding is hard"Block Mover at level 1 is age-6 appropriate. The sequence-command logic is what makes later programming intuitive. Skipping it because of age-stereotype assumption misses one of the highest-value activities in this pack.
  • Geography without contextFlags and country names are vocabulary — useful but not the geographic understanding the game name implies. Pair geography play with reading a map or watching a continent-focused video for spatial context.

Parent & Teacher FAQ

My child loves Gem Crush and ignores the other games. What should I do?
Set a session structure: educational games first, reward game (Gem Crush, idle games) last. This is a standard intrinsic-motivation pattern; let the activity they want be the reward for the activities they need.
How do I tell if my child is ready for STEM Labs?
When most Elementary Pack content (Math Mountain past level 5, Block Mover with loops) is too easy — they finish without effort. Typically age 9–11 depending on prior exposure. Try Circuit Builder first; if it's comprehensible, the rest of STEM Labs will be too.
Is there a teacher dashboard?
No. Amni-Learn is privacy-first: no accounts, no tracking, no telemetry. Sessions stay on the device. For classroom use, the practical pattern is shared classroom devices where the teacher rotates through stations rather than dashboarding remote progress.
