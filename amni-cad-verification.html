HOW TO READ THIS PAGE

Every table below comes from running Amni-CAD’s own automated tests on October 15, 2026, from the Amni-CAD source tree at commit e8b70978. The solver tests compare each result with a closed-form answer, a published correlation or a mesh-converged reference, and fail the run if the error is outside a stated tolerance. They are automated tests in the source tree and are run before each release. Each table row shows the case, the expected value, what the solver measured, the error and the tolerance the test enforces.

All 122 rows in the solver tables below passed, and no test failed. The cantilever and sphere examples have their own notes on what the test does and does not assert. The tests run in SI units because the reference solutions are published that way. Values and case names are converted to inches, psi and pounds-force first, with the metric number in parentheses. Errors are signed: a negative error means the solver is below the reference. These runs used the solvers’ CPU paths in Node. The GPU versions have their own in-browser tests, which this page does not report.

HEADLINE EXAMPLE: A CANTILEVER BEAM

A steel bar, fixed at one end with a force on the free end, has an exact answer that anyone can check with a calculator. The test builds the bar in the CAD kernel, meshes it, applies the load and solves it with the stress solver.

Bar: L = 3.937 in (100 mm), b = h = 0.394 in (10 mm), E = 29.0 Msi (200,000 MPa), ν = 0.3 Load: F = 22.48 lbf (100 N) on the free end, fixed face at the other end I = b h³ / 12 = 833.3 mm⁴ (0.002002 in⁴) Bending: δb = F L³ / (3 E I) = 100 × 100³ / (3 × 200,000 × 833.3) = 0.2000 mm Shear: δs = F L / (κ G A) = 100 × 100 / (5/6 × 76,923 × 100) = 0.0016 mm with G = E / 2.6 = 76,923 MPa, A = b h = 100 mm², κ = 5/6 Tip deflection = δb + δs = 0.2016 mm (0.007937 in) Root bending stress = F L (h/2) / I = 100 × 100 × 5 / 833.3 = 60.0 MPa (8,702 psi)

LIMITS THE APP STATES

  • Voxel FEA is good for comparing designs and finding hot spots. Sharp inside corners and points where loads or supports meet show inflated peaks; the 99.5 % value is the better guide.
  • Voxel hex meshes have stair-stepped surfaces, so stresses at curved faces are approximate. The app points contact-critical sections to the seal and forming studies.
  • Thin sheets and ribs switch to swept or adaptive hex meshes automatically, and the app reports which mesher it used.

LARGE DEFORMATION, RUBBER, PLASTICITY AND CONTACT

The nonlinear studies cover hyperelastic rubber, elastic-plastic sheet forming, O-ring seals and contact. They are checked against closed-form rubber stress laws, the Hertz contact solution, a Lamé thick cylinder, the Hill plastic collapse pressure and element patch tests, and against each other (3D against axisymmetric).

Where the solver and a reference disagree. The O-ring rows compare the finite-element line load with Lindley’s 1966 empirical formula. They differ by about 30 % (31 % to 34 % across the three squeeze levels). The test tolerance is 35 %, set wide on purpose. Lindley’s formula is an empirical fit, the FE result is mesh-converged (16 against 32 elements agree within 0.6 %), and the FE compliance at small squeeze trends to the classical Hertz value for a cylinder between plates. The first Hertz row’s half-width is 4.2 % low against a 5 % limit because that node-to-surface contact has about 8 nodes across it. The elastic closed-form checks (Lamé, the hyperelastic stress laws, Hill collapse after Richardson extrapolation) agree with the finite-element answer to well under 1 % apart from that.

LIMITS THE APP STATES

  • Seals: the elastomer is hyperelastic, with no viscoelasticity, stress relaxation or compression set, and is nearly incompressible (ν 0.4999). Hardware is rigid. Library moduli come from Shore hardness via Gent (1958); fit your own test data for strains past about 30 %. No fluid pressure, temperature or swell loading.
  • Forming: isotropic hardening with no Bauschinger effect, rate-independent, isothermal. The forming-limit curve is the Keeler-Brazier fit for low-carbon steel, extrapolated to other alloys, with no damage or fracture model. Tools are rigid.
  • Whole-part nonlinear: static, quasi-steady loading with no dynamics or creep. Loads follow the original surface directions, not follower pressure.

LIMITS THE APP STATES

  • 3D lattice Boltzmann: laminar, isothermal, incompressible. A 1 × 0.5 mm channel 8 cells deep is within 1 % of the series pressure drop and mean wall shear within about 4 %; peak shear is less certain. Ports are plenums with uniform pressure. Gas density is constant. No grid refinement, so thin features need a small window. Below 6 cells across a channel the pressure-drop error grows past about 3 %.
  • 2.5D plate solver: header momentum recovery is not modeled, so above Reynolds number 10 the split between parallel channels can be off by 20 % of the mean. Porous layer properties usually dominate the error, because permeability changes 2 to 10 times with compression.

LIMITS THE APP STATES

  • The model is generalized Hele-Shaw flow with Cross-WLF or Castro-Macosko viscosity, through-thickness heat conduction, viscous heating and convection, Tait PVT packing and Kamal cure.
  • Not modeled: fiber orientation and warpage, crystallization kinetics, cooling-channel layout (the mold wall is held at one temperature), fountain flow beyond front mixing, and inertia.
  • Sections over 0.24 in (6 mm) are thick for the 2.5D model; treat those results as approximate.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOUR WORK

These tests show that the solvers reproduce known answers on simple geometry. They do not replace your own validation on your own parts, a physical test where a failure would hurt someone, or review by a qualified engineer. Use the stress and flow tools to compare designs and find problems early, read the limits listed above for the study you run, and check any result that drives a safety or certification decision.
