WHAT IT IS

Helium Graphics is the 3D renderer built into Helium, our compiled language. It reads standard glTF models and draws them with physically based materials, light and shadow. The same code runs on Vulkan on your desktop and on WebGPU in a browser tab.

USE IT IN AMNI-CAD

Helium is one of three render engines in Amni-CAD. You don't need to install anything or write any code. You need a browser with WebGPU, such as Chrome or Edge.

Get a picture in four steps

  1. Open the render settings. In the Form workspace, click the Render tab on the right. For a solid part, use Export → Photo render… instead.
  2. Pick the engine. Next to Engine, choose Helium.
  3. Look around. Click Render view. Orbit and zoom as usual, and move any slider to see the change right away. Click Stop when you're done.
  4. Save it. Set the size under Output, then click Render image. A PNG downloads, and the finished picture stays on screen until you close it.

The settings that matter

SettingWhat it does
EnvironmentChoose Daylight sky for a real sun and clouds, a preset such as Studio softboxes or Sunset, or HDRI file for your own .hdr or .exr image.
Sun height, Sun directionLow sun gives long shadows and warm light. High sun gives short shadows and a blue sky.
Clouds, HazeHow much of the sky is covered, and how thick the air looks toward the horizon.
GroundA seamless backdrop or a colored floor to catch shadows, or none. Under Daylight sky, the backdrop becomes an open floor so you can see the horizon.
f-stop, FocusSet an f-stop for background blur, then click Pick on model to choose what stays sharp.
FinishFilm look sets the overall color response. Glow, Dark corners, Sharpen, Film grain, Color fringe, Saturation, Contrast and Warmth add the final polish.

Which engine should you pick?

EnginePick it whenKeep in mind
HeliumYou want a live view, a daylight sky, lots of finish controls, or a very large model.Needs WebGPU. Stills only for now, no animation. Light does not bounce as fully as in Path traced.
Path tracedYou want the most accurate light, reflections through glass, or an animation.Slower. It takes seconds to minutes per image, and noise clears over time.
RasterizedYou want a quick image on any device.Simplest lighting of the three, and it works without WebGPU.

The AI assistant can use it too: ask it to render with Helium, or call the form_render tool with engine: "helium". The Amni-CAD desktop app on Linux has no WebGPU yet, so open Amni-CAD in Chrome or Edge there.

WHAT IT DRAWS

Materials

Everything in the glTF metal and roughness model, plus clear coat, sheen for cloth, glass with thickness and color, adjustable index of refraction, colored highlights, oil-slick color shift, brushed metal and glowing surfaces. Texture transforms and compressed vertex data too. Baseline and progressive JPEG load, alongside PNG and HDR.

Light

Image-based lighting from any HDR photo, with accurate reflections at every roughness. Hundreds of small lights can share a scene, including sphere and tube lights that give soft, shaped highlights. The first 16 cast shadows and light up fog.

Sky and weather

A physically modeled sky that responds to the sun's height, so you get blue noon and orange dusk from one setting. Clouds, haze, wet surfaces and snow build-up.

Shadow and depth

Sun shadows that sharpen near the contact point and soften with distance, tiny contact shadows, ambient occlusion in the creases, screen-space reflections, and optional bounced light on graphics cards with ray tracing.

Surface detail

Height-mapped surfaces that keep their depth at low angles, and animated water with waves, sun glitter, foam at the shoreline and a clear view of the shallows.

Finish

Four film responses (Reinhard, ACES, AgX and Neutral), bloom, depth of field, motion blur, vignette, color fringe, film grain, sharpening, color grading tables, and fine dither so skies don't show bands.

THE NUMBERS

PSNR measures how close two images are, pixel by pixel. Higher is closer. Each extra 10 dB means ten times less squared error. Around 30 dB, differences are hard to spot without flipping between the two images.

How we measured, and what the numbers don't say

Reference images come from three.js r160 with ACES tone mapping and its standard environment filtering. Helium uses the same glTF file, camera, HDR image and exposure, on an AMD RX 9060 XT. We compare 8-bit pixels across the whole frame, background included. Both renderers are lit the same way, but three.js is a target for us here, not the truth: it is a rasterizer with its own approximations.

PSNR doesn't see everything. A sharp edge one pixel off costs more than a broad color error that looks worse. So we also compare by eye, and the differences we can still see are listed under what it can't do yet.

Each round of work also has to pass the Helium graphics tests: reference images, energy checks, native and browser output compared frame by frame, and every shader compiled for WebGPU.

USE IT FROM CODE

This program loads a model, sets up a sky and saves a finished picture. You can write it in Gold, which is plain English, or directly in Helium. Both forms below compile and run as shown.

Gold: still.gold

use std
into still.amni

Imports: gfx, gfx.linalg, gfx.gltf, gfx.envmap and gfx.scene3d. `main()` sets `gltf::lazy_pixels = true`, then `let model = gltf::load("model.glb")` (negative: print `gltf::error()` and return). Call `gfx::open_headless(1280, 720)` and `s3d::place(s3d::add(model), gfx::mat4_identity)`. Set `s3d::view` to an `s3d::Camera` with eye (2.0, 1.2, 3.0), target (0.0, 0.4, 0.0), up (0.0, 1.0, 0.0), fov_y 0.6, near 0.05, far 100.0. Call `s3d::atmosphere(linalg::unit3(float3 { 0.5, 0.7, 0.4 }), 3.5, 1.0, 0.3)`. Set `s3d::look.curve = 1`, `s3d::temporal = 1` and `s3d::ao_mode = 1`. Call `s3d::init(1280, 720)`, make `target` with `gfx::render_target(1280, 720, gfx::RGBA8)`, call `s3d::render(target)` 32 times, then `gfx::save_png(target, "out.png")` into `saved`, print `saved`, and call `gfx::close()`.

Helium: still.amni

import gfx;
import gfx.linalg;
import gfx.gltf;
import gfx.envmap;
import gfx.scene3d;
fn main() {
    gltf::lazy_pixels = true;
    let model = gltf::load("model.glb");
    if model < 0 {
        print(gltf::error());
        return;
    }
    gfx::open_headless(1280, 720);
    s3d::place(s3d::add(model), gfx::mat4_identity);
    s3d::view = s3d::Camera { eye: float3 { 2.0, 1.2, 3.0 }, target: float3 { 0.0, 0.4, 0.0 }, up: float3 { 0.0, 1.0, 0.0 }, fov_y: 0.6, near: 0.05, far: 100.0 };
    s3d::atmosphere(linalg::unit3(float3 { 0.5, 0.7, 0.4 }), 3.5, 1.0, 0.3);
    s3d::look.curve = 1;
    s3d::temporal = 1;
    s3d::ao_mode = 1;
    s3d::init(1280, 720);
    let target = gfx::render_target(1280, 720, gfx::RGBA8);
    for i in 32 {
        s3d::render(target);
    }
    let saved = gfx::save_png(target, "out.png");
    print(saved);
    gfx::close();
}

Build and run with gold run still.gold or hc run still.amni. For a window, use gfx::open(title, w, h) instead of gfx::open_headless. For a browser tab, see running in the browser.

Common controls

LineWhat it does
s3d::atmosphere(to_sun, 3.5, 1.0, 0.3)Physical sky: sun direction, sun power, haze, clouds. It sets the background, the sun color and the light.
envmap::load("studio.hdr")Light the scene from an HDR image. Also envmap::intensity and envmap::rotation.
s3d::add_light(pos, rgb, range)Add a small light. Use s3d::lights.push with radius and length for sphere and tube lights.
s3d::look.curve = 1Film response: 0 Reinhard, 1 ACES, 2 AgX, 3 Neutral.
s3d::look.vignette, .chroma, .grain, .sharpen, .saturation, .contrast, .warmthFinish controls.
s3d::wet, s3d::snow, s3d::world_timeWeather, and time for water and clouds.
s3d::temporal = 1Smooth edges over many frames. Render about 32 times for a still image.

Two Helium-only material settings sit in a glTF file's extras: {"amni": "water"} makes animated water, and {"height": {"index": 3, "scale": 0.04}} adds depth from a height map. Keep your scene near one unit across, because shadow settings work in meters. See the full reference in the API library.

WHAT IT CAN'T DO YET

Helium is getting close to three.js on stills, but it isn't at the level of Unreal Engine 5 yet. Here's what is still missing or still wrong.

AreaWhere it stands
Compared with Unreal Engine 5No Nanite-style geometry that scales to billions of triangles, no texture or mesh streaming for open worlds, and no fully dynamic bounced light at Lumen's quality.
Known differences from three.jsSheen on cloth looks brighter on the chair and toy car. Clear coat base colors come out brighter on one test, and we haven't found the cause yet.
Missing light featuresNo rectangular area lights (sphere and tube lights stand in), no caustics, no local reflection probes. Light falloff doesn't fully match three.js's physical mode.
Missing scene featuresNo decals, no alpha to coverage for foliage edges, no rain or snow particles, no hardware instancing.
Missing file formatsNo BC or KTX2 compressed textures, no WebP textures, and no Draco or meshopt compressed meshes.
In Amni-CADStills and live view only, so use Path traced for animations. Models and textures need to fit in the browser's memory, and Helium tells you when they don't. Reflections and bounced light are simpler than in Path traced.
