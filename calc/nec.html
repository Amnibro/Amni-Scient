NEC WIRE SIZING (310.16 & VD)

AMPACITY TABLES, VOLTAGE DROP, BREAKER SIZE

OPEN NEC CALCULATOR →

WHAT THIS MODULE SOLVES

The NEC (NFPA 70) Table 310.16 lists allowable ampacity for insulated copper and aluminum conductors by AWG/kcmil and insulation rating (60°C, 75°C, 90°C columns). Ampacity is derated for ambient temperature (Table 310.15(B)(1)) and conductor count in conduit (Table 310.15(C)(1)). Voltage drop is recommended <3% on a single feeder or branch, <5% combined; single-phase drop V_drop = 2·I·L·R / 1000, with L the one-way length in feet and R in Ω per 1000 ft. Conduit fill is limited per Table 1 of Chapter 9 (40% for more than 2 conductors); this module does not calculate fill, so check it separately.

CORE FORMULAS

  • I_design = 1.25 · I (continuous) or 1.00 · I (non-continuous)
  • VD = 2·I·L·R / 1000 (1φ, resistance only)
  • VD_3φ ≈ 0.866 · VD_1φ
  • %VD = VD / V_source · 100
  • I_allow = I_table · k_temp · k_count

References & StandardsNFPA 70 (NEC), Tables 310.16, 310.15(B)(1), Chapter 9 Table 1

WHEN TO REACH FOR IT

Use for branch-circuit and feeder sizing in residential, commercial, and industrial installations. Determine load current first (continuous loads at 125% per NEC 210.19(A)/215.2(A)), pick conductor for ampacity at the lowest applicable termination temperature (often 75°C), then verify voltage drop. Increase conductor size if voltage drop exceeds limits even though ampacity is satisfied. Check conduit fill before specifying raceway.

Branch-Circuit Sizing Procedure

  1. 1Determine load type and current: continuous (operating 3+ hours) or non-continuous, or a motor branch (use 430.22 motor rules).
  2. 2Apply NEC 210.19(A)(1)/215.2(A)(1): I_design = 125% of continuous + 100% of non-continuous current.
  3. 3Pick conductor type and insulation: THHN/THWN-2 is common copper for dry/wet locations, 90°C insulation rating but typically used per the 75°C ampacity column when terminations are 75°C rated.
  4. 4Look up base ampacity in NEC Table 310.16 for the conductor size and termination temperature column.
  5. 5Apply correction factors: ambient temperature (Table 310.15(B)(1)) and conductor count in raceway (Table 310.15(C)(1)). Ampacity = base × factors.
  6. 6Verify ampacity ≥ I_design after corrections.
  7. 7Check voltage drop separately: aim for <3% on a single branch or feeder, <5% combined. Use VD = 2·I·R·L for single phase, sqrt(3)·I·Z·L for three phase.
  8. 8Verify overcurrent protection per 240.4: standard breaker size at or below conductor ampacity (rounded up to nearest standard size in some cases per 240.4(B)/(C)).
  9. 9Verify conduit fill per Chapter 9 Tables 1, 4, and 5. 40% fill max for 3+ conductors; 31% for 2 conductors; 53% for single conductor.

Worked Example: 100 A Continuous Feeder

A 100 A continuous lighting and receptacle load needs a feeder from the panelboard. 60 m run, three-phase 208/120 V, 75°C THHN copper, 32°C ambient, four current-carrying conductors in conduit.

Design current: I_design = 1.25·100 = 125 A. From NEC 310.16 at 75°C: 1 AWG = 130 A. Check corrections: 32°C ambient gets correction factor 0.94 (Table 310.15(B)(1) at 30°C base). Four current-carrying conductors in raceway (counting only ungrounded, with neutral counted only if loaded with non-linear current): 310.15(C)(1) gives 0.80 for 4–6 conductors. Adjusted ampacity: 130·0.94·0.80 = 97.8 A < 125 A — insufficient.

Upsize to 2/0 AWG: base 175 A · 0.94 · 0.80 = 131.6 A. OK. Verify voltage drop: 60 m = 197 ft. 2/0 copper DC resistance is 0.0967 Ω/1000 ft (NEC Chapter 9 Table 8), so 0.0190 Ω one-way. VD = sqrt(3)·100·0.0190 = 3.30 V. %VD = 3.30/208 = 1.59% — under the 3% limit. Specify a standard 125 A breaker: it covers the 125 A design current and sits below the 131.6 A adjusted ampacity.

Common Pitfalls

  • Using 90°C column with 75°C terminationsMost equipment is listed for 75°C terminations. Even if THHN insulation is rated 90°C, ampacity is limited by termination rating per NEC 110.14(C). Use the 75°C column unless equipment is specifically labeled for 90°C terminations.
  • Forgetting the continuous-load 125% ruleNew code-checkers often size for the running load and miss the 125% multiplier for continuous duty. NEC 215.2 and 210.19 are mandatory.
  • Mis-counting conductors for derating310.15(C)(1) counts current-carrying conductors only. Grounded conductor (neutral) is counted in 4-wire wye if non-linear loads create third-harmonic current. Equipment grounding conductor is never counted. Three-phase 3-wire delta: only the 3 phases count.
  • Skipping conduit fill verificationEasy to overlook on small feeders. 3+ conductors limited to 40% fill prevents heat buildup and damage during pulling. Check Chapter 9 Tables 4 (conduit dimensions) and 5 (conductor cross-sectional area).
  • Ignoring the bonding/grounding rulesNEC Article 250 governs grounding and bonding — not directly an ampacity issue but a major audit finding. Verify equipment grounding conductor (EGC) size per Table 250.122 based on the largest overcurrent device upstream.

Engineer FAQ

When can I use the next-size-up breaker rule?
NEC 240.4(B): if the conductor ampacity does not match a standard device rating, you may use the next higher standard rating, provided that rating does not exceed 800 A and the conductors are not part of a multioutlet branch circuit supplying receptacles for cord-and-plug-connected loads. Above 800 A, 240.4(C) requires the conductor ampacity to be at least the device rating.
How do I size conductors for harmonic currents?
Triplen harmonics (3rd, 9th, 15th...) add in the neutral of a 4-wire wye system instead of canceling. Specify the neutral as the same size as phase conductors, or oversize 200% if K-factor > 6. Use K-rated transformers for non-linear loads (data centers, VFD groups).
Aluminum vs copper — when does aluminum win?
For ampacity above ~125 A and runs over ~10 m, aluminum is cost-competitive. Use compact-stranded XHHW or THHN-aluminum compatible with the lugs (verify "AL" or "AL/CU" marking). Smaller branch circuits (<50 A) almost always use copper for ease of termination and reliability at lug interfaces.

INPUTS, UNITS & ASSUMPTIONS

The tab has a wire designer, a derated sizing card and a voltage drop card. Base units are amperes, volts, feet and °C; the designer length and ambient fields have unit selectors. Sizing uses the 75 °C copper column of Table 310.16.

  • Wire designer: load, one-way length, system voltage (default 120 V), drop limit (default 3%) and duty. It returns the first size from 14 AWG to 750 kcmil whose ampacity covers 1.25 or 1.00 times the load and whose drop at the actual load stays under the limit. No derates are applied here.
  • Derated sizing: required ampacity = design current / (count factor × ambient factor), divided again by 0.75 for aluminum. Sizes run 14 AWG (20 A) to 4/0 (230 A); above that it reports ">4/0".
  • Count factor: 1.00 up to 3 conductors, 0.80 for 4 to 8, 0.70 for 9 or more. NEC 310.15(C)(1) gives 0.70 for 7 to 9 and 0.50 for 10 to 20, so correct larger bundles by hand.
  • Ambient rounds to the nearest 5 °C: 20 to 25 → 1.08, 30 to 35 → 1.00, 40 to 45 → 0.91, 50 to 55 → 0.82, 60 to 65 → 0.71, 70 to 75 → 0.58. Anything else, including cold ambients below 17.5 °C, gets 0.50.
  • Breaker: next standard rating at or above the design current, 15 to 400 A. The 240.4(D) small-conductor limits are not applied.
  • Voltage drop: single-phase, resistance only, Chapter 9 Table 8 DC values for uncoated copper.

Example with the designer defaults: 30 A continuous, 100 ft, 120 V, 3%. Ampacity needs 37.5 A, so 8 AWG (50 A) qualifies first, but it drops 2·30·0.764·100/1000 = 4.58 V (3.82%). 6 AWG drops 2.95 V (2.46%) and is the answer, leaving 117.1 V at the load. Drop set the size, not ampacity.
