WHAT IT MODELS

The materials database provides design properties for common engineering materials: structural steels (A36, 1018, 4140, 4340), stainless (304, 316, 17-4 PH), aluminums (6061-T6, 7075-T6, 2024-T3), copper alloys, titanium grades, and engineering polymers (HDPE, PEEK, PTFE, nylon, polycarbonate). Each entry includes yield strength Sy, ultimate Sut, elastic modulus E, density ρ, thermal conductivity k, specific heat cp, coefficient of thermal expansion α, and Poisson's ratio ν.

GOVERNING EQUATIONS

  • σ_allow = Sy / FoS (typical 1.5-3.0)
  • ΔL = α · L · ΔT (thermal expansion)
  • c = sqrt(E/ρ) (longitudinal sound speed)
  • κ = k / (ρ·cp) (thermal diffusivity)

References & StandardsMIL-HDBK-5J, ASM Handbook Vol. 1-2, ASTM material specs

Common Pitfalls

  • Using "typical" properties for designHandbook tables list typical values. Codes (ASME, AISC) require minimum specified values from the actual material spec. The 5–15% difference between typical and minimum can be the difference between code-compliant and not.
  • Ignoring temperature deratingAllowable stress drops sharply with temperature for most metals above 400°C. ASME II-D Table 1A is the authoritative source for code work; for non-code work, ASM Handbook Vol. 1 has curves. Aluminum loses 50% of strength by 200°C; steel by 500°C; nickel alloys retain strength to 800°C+.
  • Mixing imperial and SI inconsistentlyMIL-HDBK-5 reports stresses in ksi, ASM in MPa, JIS in kgf/mm². Always convert to a consistent system before substituting into equations. The calc module assumes consistent SI internally.
  • Forgetting heat-treatment dependenceAISI 4140 "annealed" Sy = 415 MPa; same material "Q&T to 32 HRC" Sy = 945 MPa. Both are "4140" — specify the heat treatment condition (anneal, normalize, Q&T, age) in any material call-out.
  • Specifying material that's impossible to sourceSome MIL-HDBK alloys are no longer produced. Verify commercial availability before specifying for production parts. AISI/SAE common grades, 300-series stainless, 6061/7075 aluminum, 4140 steel are reliably available globally.

Engineer FAQ

How do I pick between 304 and 316 stainless?
316 has ~2% molybdenum addition that improves chloride pitting resistance. For marine, brine, swimming-pool, or chlorinated environments: 316. For dry indoor service, food-grade non-saline contact, or cost-sensitive applications: 304. 316L (low carbon) for any welded service to avoid carbide precipitation.
When should I use a high-strength low-alloy (HSLA) steel?
When strength-to-weight matters but full Q&T heat treatment is impractical. HSLA grades (A572, A656) achieve 350–480 MPa yield in the as-rolled or normalized condition through controlled-rolling and microalloying. Common in structural steel, automotive frames, and pipeline.
How accurate are handbook thermal expansion values?
Within ±5% for the listed temperature range. Coefficient α varies with temperature (typically rises slightly above room temperature). For precision instruments operating over a wide temperature span, use a polynomial fit to vendor-supplied dilatometry data rather than a single α value.
