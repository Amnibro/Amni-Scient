SURFACE FINISH (RA, RZ, PROCESS MAP)

ROUGHNESS PARAMETERS, PROCESS CAPABILITY RANGES

OPEN FINISHES CALCULATOR →

WHAT IT COMPUTES

Surface roughness is quantified by parameters extracted from a profilometer trace. Ra (arithmetic average) is most common; Rz (max peak-to-valley over five sample lengths) is more sensitive to outliers. Each manufacturing process delivers a typical Ra range: turning 0.8-6.3 µm, milling 0.4-6.3, surface grinding 0.1-1.6, lapping 0.012-0.4, polishing <0.05. Tighter finishes cost exponentially more; specify only what the function requires.

KEY EQUATIONS

  • Ra = (1/L) · ∫₀ᴸ |y(x)| dx
  • Rz = avg of 5 max peak-to-valley
  • Rq (RMS) = sqrt((1/L) · ∫ y² dx)
  • Rt = max(peaks) - min(valleys)

References & StandardsASME B46.1, ISO 4287, ASME Y14.36M (symbols)

WHEN TO USE THIS MODULE

Pick surface finish based on function: dynamic seal contact (Ra 0.2-0.4 µm for O-rings), static seal (Ra 1.6 µm), bearing journal (Ra 0.4 max), fatigue-loaded surface (smoother is better; below 0.4 µm for high-cycle), or cosmetic. Cross-reference to standard symbols on drawings (per ASME Y14.36M) and ensure the spec is achievable by the available process.

Common Pitfalls

  • Specifying Ra when Rz mattersRa averages out occasional defects. A surface with one deep gouge can show acceptable Ra but fail in fatigue or sealing because the worst peak-to-valley (Rz, Rt) is the actual stress concentration. Use Rz for fatigue-critical or sealing surfaces.
  • Mismatching cutoff length λ_cSurface measurement uses a Gaussian or 2RC filter with cutoff λ_c (typically 0.8 mm for general work, 2.5 mm for coarse, 0.25 mm for fine). Same surface measured with different cutoffs gives different Ra. Specify λ_c on the drawing if it matters.
  • Forgetting that finish doesn't equal roundnessA perfectly smooth round bar (Ra = 0.1 μm) can still be 50 μm out-of-round. Geometric tolerance (GD&T per ASME Y14.5) and surface finish are independent specifications — both may be needed.
  • Specifying mirror finish for non-optical useBelow Ra 0.2 μm, the process slows dramatically and rejects rise. Most mechanical applications get nothing useful from finish < 0.2 μm. Specify the function (seal, bearing, fatigue, cosmetic) and let the manufacturing engineer pick the appropriate Ra range.

Engineer FAQ

How tight does a dynamic O-ring seal need its sealing surface?
Reciprocating: Ra 0.2–0.4 μm on the dynamic surface, Ra 0.8 μm on the static groove face. Rotating: Ra 0.1–0.2 μm on the dynamic surface (typically chrome-plated or hardened). Surfaces that are too smooth retain less lubricant and increase friction; too rough wears the seal. Specify a range.
When should I use shot-peening for finish?
Shot-peening produces a slightly rougher surface (Ra 1.6–4 μm) but adds compressive residual stress that more than compensates for the roughness in fatigue applications. Don't shot-peen surfaces that need smooth (sealing, bearing) — post-peen polish to recover smoothness if needed.
Are imperial μin and metric μm comparable?
1 μm = 39.4 μin. A typical machined finish Ra 32 μin = 0.8 μm; Ra 16 μin = 0.4 μm. US drawings often spec in μin; international in μm. Either is acceptable per ISO and ASME standards.
