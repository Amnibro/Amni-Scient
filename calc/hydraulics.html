HYDRAULIC CYLINDERS & ACCUMULATORS

CYLINDER BORE & ROD SIZING WITH EULER BUCKLING, EXTEND/RETRACT FORCE & SPEED, BLADDER ACCUMULATOR GAS VOLUME

SCOPE OF THIS MODULE

A hydraulic cylinder is F = P·A with plumbing. Extend force uses the full piston area, retract uses the annulus (piston minus rod), so every cylinder pulls weaker and faster than it pushes — the ratio φ = A_bore/A_annulus. Sizing walks the ISO 3320 bore series until pressure × area (with a mechanical-hydraulic efficiency) covers the load, then sizes the rod: a pushing rod is a slender column checked with Euler buckling at industry FoS 3.5 over the stroke, a pulling rod is simply in tension. Accumulators store energy in compressed gas; the required shell follows the gas law between precharge, minimum, and maximum system pressures — in absolute units.

CORE FORMULAS

  • F_extend = P·(π·D²/4)·η, F_retract = P·(π(D²−d²)/4)·η
  • v = Q / A (speed from flow)
  • φ = A_bore / A_annulus (intensification & speed ratio)
  • P_cr = π²·E·I / (K·L)² (rod buckling, FoS 3.5)
  • V₀ = ΔV / [(P₀/P₁)^{1/n} − (P₀/P₂)^{1/n}] (absolute pressures)
  • P₀ ≈ 0.9·P_min (bladder precharge)

References & StandardsISO 3320 / 4395 (bore & rod series), ISO/TS 13725 (cylinder buckling), Parker & HYDAC accumulator sizing method

WHEN TO REACH FOR IT

Size the cylinder for a press, a clamp, a tilting deck, a snow-plow lift. Then chase the two failure modes analysis actually catches: a long-stroke cylinder that buckles its rod at full extension, and a meter-out circuit that intensifies rod-side pressure past the seal rating (multiply by φ — a 2:1 cylinder metering out at 200 bar sees 400 on the rod side). Size the accumulator for the duty: isothermal for slow leakage make-up, adiabatic (bigger shell) for rapid discharge.

Worked Example: 5-Tonne Press Cylinder

A shop press needs 50 kN of push through an 800 mm stroke at 160 bar, pinned both ends. Size it.

Area demanded: A = 50,000 / (16 MPa × 0.9) = 3,472 mm² → Ø66.5 equivalent. Bore 63 delivers only 44.9 kN — fails; bore 80 gives 72.4 kN ✓.

Rod as a column (K = 1, L = 0.8 m, FoS 3.5 on 50 kN): I_req = 3.5×50,000×0.8² / (π²×210×10⁹) = 5.40×10⁻⁸ m⁴ → d ≥ 32.4 mm → rod 36 (rod 32 misses the factor). Retract force with that rod: 57.7 kN.

At 100 mm/s the pump must deliver Q = 5,027 mm² × 100 mm/s = 30.2 L/min, hydraulic power 8.0 kW — hand that to the Pumps module for the motor. If the circuit meters out on retract, the rod side sees φ = 1.25 × 160 = 201 bar — within rating here, but a 2:1 cylinder would not be.

Cylinder Sizing Procedure

  1. 1Compute required area from force, pressure, and an honest η ≈ 0.9 — seals and misalignment always eat some theory.
  2. 2Walk the standard bore ladder upward; the first bore whose (acting) area covers the demand is the buy. For pulling loads the acting area is the ANNULUS, so the rod choice feeds back into the bore choice.
  3. 3Size a pushing rod by Euler over the stroke with the mounting K factor — pinned-pinned 1.0, rigidly guided 0.5, unguided fixed-free 2.0 doubles effective length and quarters capacity.
  4. 4Check the rod-side pressure under meter-out or load-holding: multiply system pressure by φ and compare to the cylinder rating.
  5. 5Convert target speed to flow (Q = A·v) and take that number to pump selection; remember extend and retract flows differ by φ.
  6. 6Cushion or externally decelerate any load moving faster than ~0.5 m/s into the end caps.

Common Pitfalls

  • Gauge pressure in the accumulator gas lawP·Vⁿ works in absolute pressure. Sizing with gauge values undersizes the shell by roughly the atmospheric fraction of the lowest pressure — small at 200 bar, catastrophic at 5.
  • Buckling checked at mid-strokeThe rod is a column at FULL extension — check the whole stroke plus any stops overtravel. A cylinder that has never buckled at 600 mm can fold the day someone fits a 100 mm spacer.
  • Forgetting the annulus on regeneration circuitsA regen circuit routes rod-side oil back to the cap side: extend speed multiplies by φ/(φ−1) but force drops to rod-area × pressure. Both numbers change together; sizing on either alone is wrong.
  • Precharging to system pressureA bladder precharged at or above P_min slams the poppet on every cycle and dies young. 0.9·P_min keeps the bladder floating; below 0.25·P_max risks bladder over-extension. Check precharge cold, gas side only, nitrogen only.
  • Rod finish and side loadEuler assumes axial load. A cylinder used as a guide rail (side load through the rod bearing) fails gland and rod long before the column check matters — external guides carry side loads, cylinders push.

Engineer FAQ

Where does the rod-buckling FoS of 3.5 come from?
Industry practice consolidated in catalog sizing charts and ISO/TS 13725: it absorbs eccentric mounting, real end fixity between the ideal K cases, and dynamic loads. Treat it as a floor, not a target — mobile equipment with shock loading often uses more.
Isothermal or adiabatic for my accumulator?
Match the discharge time to the heat-transfer time: minutes-scale duty (thermal expansion, leakage make-up) is isothermal (n = 1); seconds-scale (dampening, emergency actuation) is adiabatic (n = 1.4) and needs the bigger shell the calculator shows. In between, real behavior lands mid-band — size adiabatic to be safe.
Why is my cylinder faster on retract?
Same flow into a smaller (annulus) area: v = Q/A. The φ ratio that weakens the pull speeds it up identically. If equal speeds matter, use a double-rod cylinder or valve-throttle the retract.
Can I use compressed air formulas here?
Force and speed math is identical (F = P·A cares nothing for the fluid). What changes: air compressibility makes position control spongy, achievable pressures are 10-16 bar not hundreds, and stored-energy hazards differ. The buckling and intensification checks apply to both.
