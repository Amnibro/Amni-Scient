COMBUSTION (AFR, FLAME TEMP, LHV)

STOICHIOMETRIC AFR, ADIABATIC FLAME TEMPERATURE, EXCESS AIR

OPEN COMBUSTION CALCULATOR →

WHAT YOU CAN CALCULATE

Stoichiometric air-fuel ratio (AFR) is the minimum air mass per fuel mass for complete combustion. For C_xH_y: AFR_stoich = (x + y/4)·4.76·28.84 / (12x + y). Excess air λ > 1 ensures complete combustion at the cost of dilution; λ < 1 produces CO. Adiabatic flame temperature is the temperature reached when all heat release goes into raising product enthalpy; real flames sit below this due to dissociation and heat loss. LHV (lower heating value) excludes water vapor latent heat; HHV (higher) includes it.

EQUATIONS AT WORK

  • CxHy + (x + y/4)(O₂ + 3.76 N₂) → xCO₂ + (y/2)H₂O + 3.76(x+y/4)N₂
  • AFR_stoich = mass_air / mass_fuel at λ=1
  • λ = AFR_actual / AFR_stoich
  • LHV = HHV - n_H2O·h_fg / m_fuel

References & StandardsNFPA 86, API 560, AGA-3, Turns "Combustion" Ch. 2

TYPICAL APPLICATIONS

Use for burner sizing, furnace energy balance, IC engine analysis, gas-turbine combustor design. Compute AFR for the fuel composition, set excess air per code (boilers typically 5-15%, gas turbines 200-400% lean), then estimate flame temperature for material selection in the combustion chamber. Check NOx production sensitivity to peak temperature.

Worked Example: Natural Gas Burner Air-Fuel Ratio

Natural gas (assume pure methane CH₄, MW = 16.04) burns with 15% excess air in a residential furnace. Compute AFR and find products composition (volumetric).

Stoichiometric: CH₄ + 2O₂ + 7.52N₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O + 7.52N₂. Moles air per mole fuel: 2 + 7.52 = 9.52. Mass basis: AFR_stoich = 9.52·28.96 / 16.04 = 17.2 kg air/kg fuel.

With 15% excess: λ = 1.15, AFR_actual = 1.15·17.2 = 19.8 kg/kg. Excess oxygen in products: 0.15·2 = 0.30 mol O₂ per mole fuel. Total dry product moles: 1 (CO₂) + 0.30 (O₂) + 1.15·7.52 (N₂) = 9.95. Volumetric O₂ in dry flue gas: 0.30 / 9.95 = 3.0%. This is the value seen on a flue-gas O₂ analyzer (the standard combustion-tuning diagnostic). For natural gas, 3% O₂ in dry flue = 15% excess air = typical residential setpoint.

Common Pitfalls

  • Insufficient excess air causing COBelow λ ≈ 1.05, combustion goes sub-stoichiometric in pockets even if overall AFR is lean. Result: carbon monoxide emissions and unburned hydrocarbons. Always run 5–15% excess air for fuel-flexible burners.
  • Excessive excess air wasting fuelEach 10% extra air carries off 1–2% of combustion energy as stack heat. Industrial boilers tune to 2–4% O₂ for natural gas, 4–6% for fuel oil, 6–10% for coal — the optimum balances CO emissions vs stack loss.
  • Using LHV vs HHV inconsistentlyLHV excludes condensation enthalpy of product water (2440 kJ/kg-H₂O); HHV includes it. Methane LHV = 50.0 MJ/kg, HHV = 55.5 MJ/kg — 11% difference. Condensing boilers report HHV efficiency; non-condensing report LHV. Always check basis before comparing.
  • Assuming adiabatic flame temperature is achievableAFT calculations assume zero heat loss. Real flames lose 10–40% of energy to chamber walls, lowering peak gas temperature substantially. Heat-transfer calculations using AFT overpredict radiation; use a derated temperature or detailed CFD.
  • Ignoring NOx-temperature dependenceThermal NOx (Zeldovich mechanism) scales exponentially above 1500°C. Burner designs that hit 2000°C local temperatures produce 10× the NOx of staged-combustion or low-NOx designs operating at 1600°C peak. NOx regulations drive most modern burner design.

Standards Reference

NFPA 86 (Standard for Ovens and Furnaces) governs industrial heating equipment, with explicit requirements for ventilation, purge cycles, flame supervision, and safety interlocks. Compliance is enforced by AHJs in most US jurisdictions.

API 560 covers fired heaters for petroleum refining and chemical service, including thermal design, mechanical design, and tube-life evaluation. Companion API 530 covers tube-thickness calculation for elevated-temperature service.

AGA-3 (gas measurement) is the dominant standard for orifice metering of natural gas, used for custody transfer and process control. ISO 5167 is the international parallel.

Turns' "An Introduction to Combustion" is the standard graduate-level text covering kinetics, transport, premixed and diffusion flames, and pollutant formation in detail.

Engineer FAQ

What's the difference between LHV and HHV in practice?
When designing a condensing boiler that captures latent heat in the flue gas, use HHV-based efficiency — you can claim >90% efficiency. For non-condensing equipment (flue gas exits above 100°C), use LHV; HHV-based efficiency caps near 90% because the latent heat is lost.
How do I estimate adiabatic flame temperature?
Solve enthalpy balance: H_reactants(T_in) = H_products(T_AFT) where product composition assumes complete combustion. For typical hydrocarbons at stoichiometric: AFT ≈ 2000–2200°C. Excess air lowers AFT roughly linearly; 50% excess drops AFT by ~500°C. Online tools and gas-turbine textbooks tabulate AFT for common fuels.
Why does my burner produce yellow flames?
Yellow comes from soot — carbon particles glowing thermally. Two causes: (1) diffusion flame (fuel and air mix at the flame surface, slow mixing produces soot), (2) premixed flame with too little air (rich mixture). For clean blue combustion, ensure premixing and λ ≥ 1.0 throughout the burner.
