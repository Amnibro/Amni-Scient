LIMITS, FITS & PRESS-FIT DESIGN (ISO 286)

HOLE/SHAFT LIMITS, CLEARANCE & INTERFERENCE, LAMÉ PRESS-FIT PRESSURE, ASSEMBLY TEMPERATURE, TOLERANCE STACK-UP

OPEN FITS CALCULATOR →

SCOPE OF THIS MODULE

ISO 286 encodes every fit as a hole letter + IT grade over a shaft letter + IT grade — 25H7/g6 fully defines four limit dimensions. The hole letter sets the fundamental deviation (H = zero line), the IT grade sets the tolerance width from published tables. Clearance fits (H/g, H/f) slide; transition fits (H/k, H/n) locate; interference fits (H/p, H/s) transmit torque through contact pressure alone. That pressure comes from Lamé thick-cylinder theory, and from it follow holding force, torque capacity, hub stress, and the heating ΔT needed to assemble. The stack-up card sums a chain of toleranced dimensions worst-case and RSS.

FORMULAS USED

  • Hole limits: EI = 0 (H), ES = EI + IT_grade
  • Clearance = hole − shaft (min & max from the four limits)
  • p = E·δ_d/d · (d_o² − d²)/(2·d_o²) (solid shaft, same materials)
  • F_axial = μ·p·π·d·L, T = μ·p·π·d²·L/2
  • σ_hoop,hub = p·(d_o² + d²)/(d_o² − d²)
  • ΔT_assembly = (δ_max + clearance)/(α·d)
  • Stack: worst-case Σt_i, RSS √(Σt_i²)

References & StandardsISO 286-1/-2, ANSI B4.2, Shigley 3-16 & 7-8 (Lamé), Machinery's Handbook "Allowances and Tolerances"

WHERE THIS APPLIES

Reach for this module whenever two cylindrical parts meet: a bearing seat (the bearing maker dictates k5/m5 shaft, H6/J6 housing), a dowel in a jig plate, a bushing pressed into a bore, a gear shrunk on a shaft. Pick the preferred fit by function first, read the limits, then — for interference — verify three things: enough minimum interference to carry the torque, hub hoop stress below yield at maximum interference, and an assembly method (press force or oven temperature) the shop can actually execute.

Worked Example: Shrink-Fit Torque Capacity

A steel ring (OD 100 mm) is shrunk onto a 50 mm solid steel shaft with 0.05 mm diametral interference. Both parts E = 200 GPa. Find the contact pressure, torque capacity for a 40 mm long hub (μ = 0.12), and the assembly temperature.

Lamé, same materials, solid shaft: p = E·δ/d · (d_o²−d²)/(2·d_o²) = 200,000 × (0.05/50) × (10,000−2,500)/(2×10,000) = 200 × 0.375 = 75 MPa.

Torque: T = μ·p·π·d²·L/2 = 0.12 × 75 × π × 50² × 40 / 2 = 1,414 N·m. Hub hoop stress at the bore: σ = p·(d_o²+d²)/(d_o²−d²) = 75 × 12,500/7,500 = 125 MPa — comfortable for any hub steel.

Assembly: to pass over the shaft with 0.03 mm working clearance, ΔT = (0.05+0.03)/(11.7×10⁻⁶ × 50) = 137 °C — a 160 °C oven soak does it. Above ~250 °C required, re-think: tempered hubs lose hardness and the fit is probably too heavy.

Choosing a Fit

  1. 1Name the function first: free running (H8/f7), precise sliding (H7/g6), location with easy assembly (H7/h6), keyed location (H7/k6), light press for accessories (H7/p6), torque-carrying shrink (H7/s6).
  2. 2Read the four limit dimensions from ISO 286 and compute min/max clearance or interference — design decisions hang on the extremes, not the nominals.
  3. 3For interference fits: verify torque capacity at MINIMUM interference (the weakest assembly that ships) and hub stress at MAXIMUM interference (the tightest).
  4. 4Derate for surface finish: pressing flattens asperities and eats real interference — budget several µm of loss on ordinary turned finishes, less on ground surfaces.
  5. 5Pick the assembly method: press force = μ·p·π·d·L at max interference (check the shop press), or thermal with ΔT from the equation plus a working clearance.
  6. 6If the joint also sees rotation at speed, check that centrifugal growth of the hub does not eat the interference — high-speed rotors lose grip as they spin up.

Common Pitfalls

  • Verifying torque at nominal interferenceTolerances make interference a RANGE. A H7/s6 fit at Ø50 can vary its interference nearly 2:1 across the limits — the drive must survive the loosest legal assembly, so all capacity math uses δ_min.
  • Bursting a thin hubHub hoop stress rises steeply as wall thins: at d_o/d < 1.5 an s-class fit can pass yield at δ_max. Check σ_hoop against hub Sy before committing; a keyed lighter fit may serve better.
  • Ignoring dissimilar materialsAn aluminum hub on a steel shaft: different E and α change both the pressure equation (full two-cylinder Lamé) and the temperature behavior — an interference fit sized at 20 °C can walk loose at 120 °C because aluminum grows twice as fast.
  • Worst-case stacking long chainsAdding ten worst-case tolerances predicts a spread that essentially never occurs. RSS reflects reality for independent dimensions; save worst-case for chains of three or fewer, or for safety-critical clearances.
  • Measuring at the wrong temperatureISO 286 limits are defined at 20 °C. A 300 mm aluminum part measured in a 30 °C shop reads 70 µm oversize — more than an entire IT7 band.

Engineer FAQ

H7/g6 or H7/h6 for a sliding fit?
g6 guarantees clearance at every legal combination — parts always slide, with a whisper of play. h6 can produce line-to-line contact at the extremes, so assemblies may need a push. Choose g6 for mechanisms that must never bind, h6 for location where occasional hand-press is acceptable.
Do I still need a key with an interference fit?
A properly sized shrink fit carries torque without one, and high-speed balance prefers no keyway. Keys get added for de-ratable drives (fit loosens with temperature or speed), for positive timing, or where codes demand mechanical redundancy. If you add a key, the keyway stress concentration lands in the most stressed fiber of the hub — check it.
What does the 0.8 in 25H7 ⌀0.8 surface callout have to do with the fit?
Roughness eats interference: peaks crush on assembly, so the effective interference is less than measured over peaks. Heavy press fits on rough surfaces can lose a meaningful share of their grip — grind the seats for serious shrink fits.
When is a transition fit the right answer?
When location accuracy matters and torque comes from elsewhere (a key, a clamp, bolts). H7/k6 centers a bearing or gear precisely and still assembles with light tooling — the occasional light interference is a feature, not a defect.
