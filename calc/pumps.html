CENTRIFUGAL PUMPS (NPSH, AFFINITY)

NPSH AVAILABLE VS REQUIRED, AFFINITY LAWS, SPECIFIC SPEED

OPEN PUMPS CALCULATOR →

WHAT YOU CAN CALCULATE

NPSH (Net Positive Suction Head) prevents cavitation: NPSHa must exceed NPSHr (from the pump curve) by a margin (typically 1 m or 1.5×). NPSHa = (P_atm - P_vap)/ρg + z_static - h_friction. Affinity laws scale flow Q∝N, head H∝N², power P∝N³ with impeller speed N (or diameter for trim). Specific speed Ns characterizes pump type (radial < 2000 < mixed < 5000 < axial in US units).

THE MATH BEHIND IT

  • NPSHa = (Patm - Pvap)/ρg + zs - h_fs
  • Q₂ = Q₁ · (N₂/N₁)
  • H₂ = H₁ · (N₂/N₁)²
  • P₂ = P₁ · (N₂/N₁)³
  • Ns = N · sqrt(Q) / H^0.75

References & StandardsANSI/HI 1.6, ANSI/HI 9.6.1 (NPSH), API 610

WHEN TO USE THIS MODULE

Use for centrifugal pump selection and operating-point analysis. Compute NPSHa for the suction-side piping and verify the margin over NPSHr at design flow. Use affinity laws to predict performance after a speed change (VFD) or impeller trim. Pair with the fluids module to develop the system curve and find the intersection with the pump curve. Always operate near best-efficiency point (BEP) to maximize life.

Pump Selection Procedure

  1. 1Build the system curve: total dynamic head (static + friction + velocity) vs flow rate. Use the Fluids module for friction loss.
  2. 2Pick a candidate pump from a manufacturer's 50 Hz or 60 Hz catalog with operating point near the best-efficiency-point (BEP).
  3. 3Verify NPSHa > NPSHr + 1 m margin at design flow. Tight margin invites cavitation under upset conditions.
  4. 4Check operation range: stay between 70% and 120% of BEP flow for normal duty. Below 70% causes recirculation, hydraulic radial load, and bearing wear; above 120% drops efficiency and risks NPSH issues.
  5. 5Verify driver power: required shaft power = (Q·H·ρ·g) / η_pump. Add 10–15% margin and round up to a standard motor.
  6. 6Specify materials: cast iron casing + bronze impeller for clean water, 316SS for corrosive, duplex for seawater, hardened steel for slurries.
  7. 7Select a mechanical seal compatible with the fluid, temperature, and pressure. API 682 Plan 53 (pressurized barrier) for hazardous service.

Worked Example: NPSH Margin Check

A pump draws 200 m³/h of 70°C water from an open tank 2 m above the pump centerline through 15 m of 4" pipe. NPSHr at design = 5.0 m. Verify NPSH available.

Atmospheric pressure: P_atm = 101,325 Pa. Saturation vapor pressure of water at 70°C: P_vap = 31,200 Pa. Density: ρ = 977 kg/m³. Suction-side velocity: with 4" ID = 102 mm, Q = 200 m³/h = 0.0556 m³/s, v = 6.81 m/s (high — consider oversizing).

Suction-side friction: assume f = 0.022 in turbulent regime, h_fs = 0.022·(15/0.102)·(6.81²/19.62) = 7.62 m. Velocity head term in NPSH calc — often included separately.

NPSHa = (101,325 − 31,200) / (977·9.81) + 2 − 7.62 = 7.32 + 2 − 7.62 = 1.70 m. NPSHr = 5.0 m. NPSHa < NPSHr by 3.3 m — cavitation guaranteed. Either oversize the suction line to 6" (drops v to 3.0 m/s, h_fs to ~1.5 m), or raise the tank, or pick a low-NPSHr pump (some impeller designs achieve NPSHr < 2 m at this flow).

Common Pitfalls

  • Designing far off BEPPump efficiency falls steeply away from BEP. Operating at 50% of BEP doubles shaft seal wear, halves bearing life, and adds 30–50% energy cost. Select for BEP near design flow, not just hitting the head requirement.
  • Ignoring NPSHa derating with temperatureP_vap rises exponentially with temperature. NPSHa for the same suction geometry drops from 8 m at 20°C to 3 m at 80°C and goes negative above ~90°C. Hot-water and boiler-feed pumps need explicit attention to NPSH.
  • Sizing for nominal flow onlyReal operation includes startup, low-flow turndown, and upset conditions. Plot the entire operating envelope on the pump curve. Many failures originate from operation in the low-flow region the design never visited.
  • Specifying parallel pumps without verifying flat curveParallel pumps share flow equally only if their curves are nearly identical at the operating head. With a flat (or rising-then-flat) head-flow curve, one pump can take most of the flow while the other idles or even backflows. Use rising-to-shutoff curves for parallel operation.
  • Forgetting affinity-law cubic for powerSlowing a pump by VFD cuts flow linearly but power cubically. A 50% speed reduction cuts power to 12.5% — the dominant energy saving in variable-load installations. Verify the motor and VFD can handle the speed range.

Engineer FAQ

Should I oversize or use a VFD?
VFD wins on energy when flow demand varies >25% from nominal. Oversize wins on capital cost when flow is steady and the motor would otherwise run at one speed. Hybrid: pick a pump with a slightly large impeller and trim or VFD for fine-tuning.
Why does my pump shaft seal leak?
Common causes: face flatness lost (overheating from dry running), elastomer chemical attack, vibration above the seal's rating, or particle ingress through the seal flush. Always provide seal flush water for slurries; verify cooling water flow for hot service; check shaft runout before suspecting the seal itself.
When do I need a positive-displacement pump instead?
High-pressure low-flow (hydraulics, fuel injection), viscous fluids (polymer, asphalt), precise metering (chemical dosing), or self-priming requirement. Centrifugal pumps need 0.5–5 cP viscosity range and gradually degrade with thicker fluids. Above ~500 cP, positive displacement (gear, lobe, screw) is standard.
