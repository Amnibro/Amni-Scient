PRESSURE VESSELS (THIN & THICK WALL)

HOOP, LONGITUDINAL, LAMÉ, ASME VIII-1 MINIMUM THICKNESS

OPEN PV CALCULATOR →

WHAT THIS MODULE SOLVES

Thin-wall (D/t > 20) cylindrical vessels under internal pressure see hoop stress σ_h = P·r/t and longitudinal stress σ_L = P·r/(2t); hoop is twice longitudinal. For thick walls, Lamé equations give radial and tangential stress varying through wall thickness. ASME BPVC Section VIII Division 1 prescribes minimum thickness with allowable stress S, joint efficiency E, and corrosion allowance: t = P·R / (S·E - 0.6·P) for cylindrical shells.

GOVERNING EQUATIONS

  • σ_h = P·r / t (thin)
  • σ_L = P·r / (2t) (thin)
  • σ_t,r = (Pi·ri² - Po·ro²) / (ro² - ri²) ± (Pi - Po)·ri²·ro² / [r²(ro²-ri²)] (Lamé)
  • t_min = P·R / (S·E - 0.6·P) (ASME UG-27)

References & StandardsASME BPVC Section VIII Div 1, ASME B31.3

REAL-WORLD USE CASES

Size pressure vessels, pipe walls, hydraulic cylinders. Choose thin-wall formula for D/t > 20 (most piping); use Lamé for thick wall (gun barrels, high-pressure dies). For ASME-coded equipment, design to VIII-1 with appropriate weld joint efficiency (E=1.0 for full radiography, 0.7 for spot, 0.6 for none). Always include corrosion allowance (often 1/16" or 3 mm) and verify external pressure (buckling) separately.

Worked Example: ASME UG-27 Cylindrical Shell

Size the wall thickness of a 2 m ID, 5 m long cylindrical pressure vessel for 2.5 MPa internal design pressure at 200°C using SA-516 Gr. 70 plate. Joint efficiency 0.85 (spot radiography). Corrosion allowance 3 mm.

Allowable stress for SA-516-70 at 200°C: S = 138 MPa (ASME II-D table 1A). Inside radius: R = 1000 mm.

ASME UG-27(c)(1) circumferential stress (governs for thin cylinders):

t = P·R / (S·E − 0.6·P) = 2.5·1000 / (138·0.85 − 0.6·2.5) = 2500 / (117.3 − 1.5) = 2500 / 115.8 = 21.6 mm

Longitudinal stress (verify it doesn't govern): t = P·R / (2·S·E + 0.4·P) = 2500 / (234.6 + 1.0) = 10.6 mm — smaller, so circumferential controls.

Add corrosion allowance: total wall = 21.6 + 3 = 24.6 mm. Round up to next standard plate thickness — 25 mm. Verify D/t = 2000/25 = 80, well above 20 so thin-wall assumption is valid.

For end heads, use ASME UG-32 for ellipsoidal (2:1 ratio): t = P·D / (2·S·E − 0.2·P) = 2.5·2000 / (234.6 − 0.5) = 21.4 mm + 3 mm corrosion = round to 25 mm matching the shell.

Common Pitfalls

  • Confusing internal radius R with outer radiusASME UG-27 uses R = inside radius for thin-cylinder formulas. R_o would give different answer; nozzles use ID at the bore. Always verify which radius is intended by the specific code section.
  • Forgetting corrosion allowance earlyCode-minimum thickness is for the corroded condition at end of life. Specify nominal thickness = calculated + corrosion. For 25 mm calculated with 3 mm allowance, order 28 mm plate, not 25.
  • Skipping the external-pressure checkVacuum jackets and steam-jacketed vessels can see external pressure. External design uses UG-28 with B-factor charts; cylinder buckling load is much smaller than yield-limited internal pressure capacity. A thick wall designed for high internal pressure can still fail by buckling under modest vacuum.
  • Ignoring weld joint efficiency rulesE = 1.0 requires 100% radiography of butt welds (UW-11(a)). E = 0.85 for spot radiography. E = 0.70 for visual only. Reduce E if the inspection plan doesn't support the assumption — this is a frequent audit finding on legacy vessels.
  • Sizing nozzles like the main shellNozzle wall thickness has its own ASME UG-37 reinforcement rules. Bore in the shell removes a metal section that must be replaced by extra thickness in the nozzle, pad reinforcement, or thicker shell locally. Pure nozzle-thickness UG-27 is insufficient.

ASME Code Details

ASME BPVC Section VIII Division 1 is the workhorse pressure-vessel code for industrial vessels up to ~21 MPa. Division 2 covers higher-rigor design with finite-element-style analysis (sometimes lower factor of safety, but more analysis required). Division 3 covers ultra-high pressure.

Section II-D provides allowable stresses for all listed materials as a function of temperature. Allowables are based on the lesser of yield/4.0, ultimate/3.5, or creep limits at temperature.

B31.3 (Process Piping) is the parallel code for piping rather than vessels. Stress limits are stricter than VIII-1, and the design equations are similar but distinct. Pipe spools welded to a vessel are typically B31.3 right up to the vessel weld neck.

Engineer FAQ

When do I need Division 2 instead of Division 1?
When vessel size, material, or duty (cyclic loading, large diameter, high cost) justifies the more detailed analysis. Division 2 allows higher allowable stress (lower FoS) in exchange for elastic-stress analysis, fatigue analysis, and tighter manufacturing tolerances. Typically chosen for large process vessels and pressurized hydraulic accumulators above ~3 MPa.
What's the difference between MAWP and MAP?
MAWP (Maximum Allowable Working Pressure) is the design pressure plus any liquid head, at the design temperature. MAP (Maximum Allowable Pressure) is the same calc using nominal (not corroded) thickness at ambient temperature — the highest pressure the new vessel can theoretically hold. Code marks MAWP on the nameplate.
Can I derate a vessel for higher pressure at lower temperature?
Yes — if you can document that operating temperature is below design and the inspection record supports the new MAWP, ASME UG-99 allows pressure-temperature derating curves. Verify with your local jurisdiction (most states require pressure boundary modifications to be filed with the National Board).
