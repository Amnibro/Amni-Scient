FILLET WELDS (AWS)

THROAT SIZING, WELD-GROUP POLAR MOMENT, ALLOWABLE STRENGTH

OPEN WELDS CALCULATOR →

WHAT YOU CAN CALCULATE

Fillet welds are sized by their throat dimension t = 0.707·w where w is the leg length. AWS D1.1 limits allowable shear stress on the throat to 0.30·Fexx for the electrode tensile strength. For eccentric loads (load applied off the weld-group centroid), the elastic vector method computes weld stress as the combination of direct shear plus a polar-moment shear that varies around the group. Treat each weld segment as a line and compute J_w accordingly.

THE MATH BEHIND IT

  • t = 0.707 · w
  • τ_allow = 0.30 · Fexx
  • τ_direct = P / A_w
  • τ_torsion = T·r / J_w
  • τ_resultant = sqrt(τ_direct² + τ_torsion² + 2τ_d·τ_t·cosθ)

References & StandardsAWS D1.1, AISC 360 Ch. J

WHEN TO USE THIS MODULE

Use for any structural weld: brackets, lifting lugs, weldments, pressure-vessel attachments. For pure axial or shear load, throat sizing is direct. For eccentric loads, build the weld group and compute the resultant on the highest-stressed point. Specify electrode (E70xx, E80xx) consistent with base metal. Avoid intermittent welds in fatigue applications.

Worked Example: Eccentrically Loaded Bracket

A steel bracket is welded to a column with two horizontal fillet welds, each 200 mm long, separated vertically by 250 mm. A 25 kN load is applied 150 mm out from the column face. Size the fillet weld leg w for E70 electrode (Fexx = 480 MPa).

Treat welds as lines. Total weld length: L = 2 × 200 = 400 mm. Centroid is at the midpoint between the welds. Eccentricity from centroid to load: e = 150 mm. Torque on weld group: T = 25,000 × 0.150 = 3,750 N·m.

Polar moment of weld group (per unit throat): J_w = 2·(L·d²/4 + L³/12) where d is the spacing. J_w = 2·(0.200·0.125² + 0.200³/12) = 2·(0.003125 + 0.000667) = 0.00758 m³. Top corner of the upper weld is the highest-stressed point: r_x = 100 mm, r_y = 125 mm.

Direct shear: τ_d = P / L = 25,000 / 0.400 = 62.5 kN/m. Torsional shear components: τ_tx = T·r_y / J_w = 3750·0.125 / 0.00758 = 61.8 kN/m, τ_ty = T·r_x / J_w = 3750·0.100 / 0.00758 = 49.5 kN/m. Total y-direction: 62.5 + 49.5 = 112 kN/m; x-direction: 61.8 kN/m. Resultant: sqrt(112² + 61.8²) = 128 kN/m per millimeter of throat.

Allowable per unit throat: 0.30 × 480 = 144 MPa. Required throat: t = 128,000 / 144×10⁶ = 0.89 mm. Fillet leg: w = t / 0.707 = 1.26 mm. Minimum practical fillet for 12 mm thick plate is 5 mm per AWS D1.1 Table 5.7, so specify 5 mm fillet legs — the weld is governed by minimum size rules, not strength.

Common Pitfalls

  • Ignoring the minimum-size tableAWS D1.1 Table 5.7 specifies minimum fillet legs based on the thicker of the joined plates. A 25 mm plate demands at least 8 mm fillet regardless of computed stress — this prevents rapid cooling and the resulting hard, brittle HAZ.
  • Welding through paint or oilSurface contamination causes porosity, slag inclusions, and lack-of-fusion defects. Clean to bright metal in a 25 mm zone around the joint before welding. Mill scale is acceptable; rust, paint, and oil are not.
  • Sizing for nominal shear only on eccentric jointsEccentric loads create torsion on the weld group. The far corner sees the resultant of direct + torsional shear, often 2–3× the nominal. Use the elastic vector method or the AISC instantaneous-center method.
  • Specifying an electrode mismatched to base metalUse undermatched electrodes to limit residual stress and crack risk in high-strength steels (A514 with E80 not E110). Use matched electrodes for mild steel. Always check the AWS filler-metal selection chart.
  • Intermittent fillet welds in fatigue applicationsStop-start points are stress concentrations and fatigue crack initiation sites. Use continuous welds in fatigue-critical structures; reserve intermittents for static service where weight or distortion matters more than fatigue life.

Standards Detail

AWS D1.1 (Structural Welding Code — Steel) is the dominant US standard for welded steel structures. It governs prequalified joint geometry, electrode selection, welder qualification, inspection procedures, and acceptance criteria. The "prequalified" joints in Annex K can be used without joint-specific procedure qualification, saving significant cost.

AISC 360 Chapter J specifies allowable stresses and design strengths for connections, including welds. AISC uses LRFD (load-resistance factor design) with φ = 0.75 on weld strength. ASD (allowable stress design) uses Ω = 2.00; both arrive at similar nominal sizes for typical buildings.

For pressure vessels, ASME BPVC Section IX governs welding procedure and welder qualification, with weld joint efficiency E (1.0 full radiography, 0.85 spot, 0.70 visual) applied to allowable stress in Section VIII design.

Engineer FAQ

When should I use a butt weld instead of fillet?
Whenever full-strength joint capacity is needed without the eccentricity that fillets introduce. Butt welds match base-metal strength when full penetration is achieved; they require beveled edges and more skill. Use butts for pressure-boundary welds, fatigue-loaded joints, and load-path-critical structural connections.
Is partial-penetration (PJP) weld design different from CJP?
Yes. PJP welds have an effective throat smaller than the bevel depth, defined by AWS D1.1 Table 2.2 based on welding process and joint angle. PJP allowable stress is lower than CJP because the unfused root is a stress raiser. Avoid PJP in tension and fatigue.
Why does my weld crack along the toe?
Toe cracks come from high residual tension, stress concentration at the weld toe (Kt ~ 2), and possibly hydrogen pickup during welding. Mitigation: preheat the base metal, use low-hydrogen electrodes (E7018 not E6010), grind the weld toe smooth for fatigue applications.
