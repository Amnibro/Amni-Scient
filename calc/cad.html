CAD STUDIO: BROWSER PARAMETRIC MODELER

CSG SOLIDS FROM PRIMITIVES, EXACT MASS PROPERTIES, BINARY STL EXPORT — NO INSTALL, NO UPLOAD

OPEN CAD CALCULATOR →

SCOPE OF THIS MODULE

CAD Studio is a parametric solid modeler that runs entirely in the browser. Models are feature stacks: add boxes, cylinders, cones, spheres, tori, and tubes; flip any feature to CUT and it booleans away from the running solid via BSP (binary space partitioning) constructive solid geometry. Every rebuild reports exact mass properties — volume, surface area, center of gravity, and mass for a chosen material — computed by the divergence theorem over the actual triangle mesh, so the numbers are exact for the solid you see. Export is binary STL of the same triangles, ready for a slicer or CAM package.

GOVERNING EQUATIONS

  • V = Σ v₁·(v₂×v₃)/6 over all mesh triangles (divergence theorem)
  • CoG = Σ (v₁+v₂+v₃)/4 · V_tet / V
  • m = ρ·V (six built-in material densities)
  • Binary STL = 84 + 50·n bytes (n triangles)
  • Booleans: BSP clip sequences (union for ADD, subtract for CUT)

References & StandardsBSP CSG per E. Wallace csg.js (MIT); binary STL de-facto standard (3D Systems); densities from standard material tables

REAL-WORLD USE CASES

Model the bracket while the idea is hot: a drilled plate, a flanged bushing, a counterweight whose mass you need before ordering stock. The mass-properties card answers "how heavy" and "where's the CoG" — feed the CoG straight into the Rigging module for the lift plan, or the volume into a casting quote. It will not replace a full parametric CAD seat for production drawings; it will beat opening one for the 90% of quick questions that end at volume, mass, and a printable STL.

Worked Example: Checking the Modeler by Hand

The "plate with 4 holes" preset: a 100×10×60 mm plate minus four Ø8 through-holes.

Hand calculation: plate = 60,000 mm³; each hole removes π×4²×10 = 502.7 mm³ (the modeler's 32-segment cylinders remove 499.4, the inscribed-polygon deficit of 0.64%). Net: 58,002 mm³ ≈ 58.00 cm³. Steel at 7.85 g/cm³: 455.3 g.

The mass-properties card reports exactly this, because the divergence-theorem integral is exact for the tessellated solid — the only approximation in the whole chain is polygonal circles, and the card tells you so. Symmetric holes leave the CoG at the plate center; move one hole off-pattern with its POS fields and watch the CoG walk away from it.

Modeling Workflow

  1. 1Start from the biggest positive volume — the stock, the plate, the body. The first feature is always ADD.
  2. 2Add joining volumes next (bosses, flanges, ribs), then cut holes and pockets last — booleans are order-independent for disjoint cuts, but cut-last keeps the tree readable.
  3. 3Position features by their centers: a through-hole in a 10 mm plate is a cylinder of height 20 centered on the plate mid-plane, so both faces clear with margin.
  4. 4Rebuild and read the stats line: polygon count and rebuild time grow with every boolean — keep models under a dozen features. Segment counts on curved primitives are fixed.
  5. 5Pick the material and read mass and CoG; screenshot or note them for the drawing.
  6. 6Export STL only when the solid looks right from several orbits — the exporter writes exactly what you see.

Limits To Respect

  • Curved faces are polygonsCylinders, cones and tubes use 32 segments (0.64% under the smooth volume); spheres use 24×12 facets (about 2.8% under) and tori 32×16 (about 3.2% under) and printed holes come out marginally faceted. Drill or ream printed holes that need to be round.
  • Coplanar boolean facesCutting with a face exactly flush to a face of the solid puts the BSP on a knife edge — the result may keep or drop the sliver either way. Overlap cuts by a margin (the workflow above) and the ambiguity never arises.
  • STL has no unitsThe file stores bare numbers; this modeler works in millimeters. Tell the slicer or CAM import dialog "mm" — the same file read as inches comes out 25.4× too large.
  • No fillets or chamfers yetThe primitive set is box/cylinder/cone/sphere/torus/tube with booleans. Stress-relieving fillets and lead-in chamfers must be added downstream, or approximated with cone cuts.

Engineer FAQ

Is the exported STL watertight?
The surface is closed with no gaps, which is what the volume integral needs. A boolean splits faces without splitting their neighbors, so the mesh can contain T-junctions that a strict manifold check reports as open edges; run a mesh repair if a slicer or CAM tool complains. If a boolean with coplanar faces produced an ambiguous sliver (see limits), re-model with overlap margins rather than repairing downstream.
Why STL and not STEP?
STEP carries exact analytic geometry and needs a full B-rep kernel — megabytes of code and a different accuracy contract. STL carries triangles, which is exactly what this modeler produces, what printers consume, and what the mass-properties math is exact FOR.
How accurate are the mass properties?
Exact for the mesh, by construction — the divergence theorem is an identity, not an approximation. The only gap to the ideal solid is polygonal circles, stated on the card (a 32-segment circle has 0.64% less area than πr²). Boxes and plates are exact to floating point.
Does any geometry leave my machine?
No. Modeling, booleans, mass properties, and STL generation are local JavaScript; the only network fetch is the 3-D display library. Refresh the page and unrendered models are gone — export the STL to keep the part.

INPUTS, UNITS & ASSUMPTIONS

Each feature is one primitive with its own size fields, a position (POS X, Y, Z) and a rotation (ROT X, Y, Z in degrees). All lengths are millimeters. The position is the primitive's center, and rotations are applied about X, then Y, then Z. The first feature is always ADD. Each later feature is ADD (union) or CUT (subtract), applied in list order. Editing any field rebuilds the solid after a 400 ms pause.

  • Primitives and their defaults: box SX, SY, SZ (60, 20, 40); cylinder R, H (15, 40); cone R1 at the bottom, R2 at the top, H (20, 8, 35); sphere R (18); torus R major, r minor (25, 8); tube RO, RI, H (20, 15, 40). RI is capped at 0.98 × RO. Cylinders, cones and tubes run along the Y axis.
  • Curves are faceted with fixed counts. Cylinders, cones and tubes use 32 segments, which puts their volume 0.64% under the smooth shape. Spheres use 24 × 12 facets (about 2.8% under) and tori 32 × 16 (about 3.2% under).
  • Material densities in g/cm³: steel 7.85, aluminum 2.70, stainless 7.90, brass 8.50, Ti-6Al-4V 4.43, ABS/PLA 1.05. Mass is density × volume, so the part is treated as solid and uniform. No infill or hollow shell is modeled.
  • Outputs: volume in cm³, mass in g, surface area in cm², and center of gravity in mm, measured from the model origin.
  • The STL file (amni-cad.stl) is binary, 84 + 50 bytes per triangle, with float32 coordinates in millimeters and one facet normal per triangle.
  • Points within 0.00001 mm of a splitting plane count as lying on it. That tolerance is why flush, coplanar cuts are unreliable.
