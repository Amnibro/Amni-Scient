SCOPE OF THIS MODULE

CAD Studio is a parametric solid modeler that runs entirely in the browser. Models are feature stacks: add boxes, cylinders, cones, and spheres; flip any feature to CUT and it booleans away from the running solid via BSP (binary space partitioning) constructive solid geometry. Every rebuild reports exact mass properties — volume, surface area, center of gravity, and mass for a chosen material — computed by the divergence theorem over the actual triangle mesh, so the numbers are exact for the solid you see. Export is watertight binary STL, ready for a slicer or CAM package.

GOVERNING EQUATIONS

  • V = Σ v₁·(v₂×v₃)/6 over all mesh triangles (divergence theorem)
  • CoG = Σ (v₁+v₂+v₃)/4 · V_tet / V
  • m = ρ·V (six built-in material densities)
  • Binary STL = 84 + 50·n bytes (n triangles)
  • Booleans: BSP clip sequences (union, subtract, intersect)

References & StandardsBSP CSG per E. Wallace csg.js (MIT); binary STL de-facto standard (3D Systems, 1989); densities from standard material tables

REAL-WORLD USE CASES

Model the bracket while the idea is hot: a drilled plate, a flanged bushing, a counterweight whose mass you need before ordering stock. The mass-properties card answers "how heavy" and "where's the CoG" — feed the CoG straight into the Rigging module for the lift plan, or the volume into a casting quote. It will not replace a full parametric CAD seat for production drawings; it will beat opening one for the 90% of quick questions that end at volume, mass, and a printable STL.

Worked Example: Checking the Modeler by Hand

The "plate with 4 holes" preset: a 100×10×60 mm plate minus four Ø8 through-holes.

Hand calculation: plate = 60,000 mm³; each hole removes π×4²×10 = 502.7 mm³ (the modeler's 32-segment cylinders remove 502.0 — the inscribed-polygon deficit of 0.14%). Net: 57,992 mm³ ≈ 58.0 cm³. Steel at 7.85 g/cm³: 455 g.

The mass-properties card reports exactly this, because the divergence-theorem integral is exact for the tessellated solid — the only approximation in the whole chain is polygonal circles, and the card tells you so. Symmetric holes leave the CoG at the plate center; drag one hole off-pattern and watch the CoG walk away from it.

Modeling Workflow

  1. 1Start from the biggest positive volume — the stock, the plate, the body. The first feature is always ADD.
  2. 2Add joining volumes next (bosses, flanges, ribs), then cut holes and pockets last — booleans are order-independent for disjoint cuts, but cut-last keeps the tree readable.
  3. 3Position features by their centers: a through-hole in a 10 mm plate is a cylinder of height 20 centered on the plate mid-plane, so both faces clear with margin.
  4. 4Rebuild and read the stats line: polygon count and rebuild time grow with every boolean — keep models under a dozen features and 32-segment circles unless you need more.
  5. 5Pick the material and read mass and CoG; screenshot or note them for the drawing.
  6. 6Export STL only when the solid looks right from several orbits — the exporter writes exactly what you see.

Limits To Respect

  • Curved faces are polygonsCylinders and spheres are 32/24-segment approximations: volumes read ~0.1-0.5% under the smooth ideal and printed holes come out marginally faceted. Drill or ream printed holes that need to be round.
  • Coplanar boolean facesCutting with a face exactly flush to a face of the solid puts the BSP on a knife edge — the result may keep or drop the sliver either way. Overlap cuts by a margin (the workflow above) and the ambiguity never arises.
  • STL has no unitsThe file stores bare numbers; this modeler works in millimeters. Tell the slicer or CAM import dialog "mm" — the same file read as inches comes out 25.4× too large.
  • No fillets or chamfers yetThe primitive set is box/cylinder/cone/sphere with booleans. Stress-relieving fillets and lead-in chamfers must be added downstream, or approximated with cone cuts.

Engineer FAQ

Is the exported STL watertight?
Yes — BSP output is a closed 2-manifold re-triangulated by fans, and slicers accept it directly. If a boolean with coplanar faces produced an ambiguous sliver (see limits), re-model with overlap margins rather than repairing downstream.
Why STL and not STEP?
STEP carries exact analytic geometry and needs a full B-rep kernel — megabytes of code and a different accuracy contract. STL carries triangles, which is exactly what this modeler produces, what printers consume, and what the mass-properties math is exact FOR.
How accurate are the mass properties?
Exact for the mesh, by construction — the divergence theorem is an identity, not an approximation. The only gap to the ideal solid is polygonal circles, stated on the card (~0.3% under πr² at 32 segments). Boxes and plates are exact to floating point.
Does any geometry leave my machine?
No. Modeling, booleans, mass properties, and STL generation are local JavaScript; the only network fetch is the 3-D display library. Refresh the page and unrendered models are gone — export the STL to keep the part.
