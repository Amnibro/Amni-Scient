O-RING & GASKET SEAL DESIGN

SQUEEZE, GLAND GEOMETRY, FRICTION, EXTRUSION-GAP, MATERIALS

OPEN SEALS CALCULATOR →

SCOPE OF THIS MODULE

Elastomer seals rely on compression (squeeze) of the cross-section to fill machining marks and resist pressure. Typical squeeze is 15-30% of free cross-section diameter, lower for dynamic seals to limit friction, higher for static. Groove fill should be 60-85% to allow for elastomer swell and thermal expansion. Extrusion gap (clearance between the two sealed surfaces) must stay below the limit set by elastomer hardness and pressure differential, otherwise the seal extrudes into the gap and fails.

FORMULAS USED

  • Squeeze % = (CS_free - CS_compressed) / CS_free × 100
  • Gland fill % = (CS_area) / (groove area) × 100
  • Friction F = f_c · π · D · CS
  • Max gap = f(durometer, ΔP) per Parker/Apple Rubber chart

References & StandardsAS568, ISO 3601, Parker O-Ring Handbook ORD 5700

WHERE THIS APPLIES

Use this module for any static or dynamic O-ring, X-ring, or square-cut seal: hydraulic cylinders, pneumatic actuators, pressure-vessel flanges, vacuum chambers. Pick the cord diameter from a standard size table (AS568 for inch, ISO 3601 for metric), set squeeze and gland fill, and verify extrusion gap. For elevated pressure, add an anti-extrusion backup ring or use a harder durometer.

Top Sealing Failures (And Their Causes)

  • Extrusion (nibbling)Elastomer flows into the clearance gap under pressure and chunks tear off. Cause: gap too large for the durometer/pressure combination. Fix: tighter clearance, harder durometer (Shore 90A), or add an anti-extrusion backup ring on the low-pressure side.
  • Spiral failure (rolling)Dynamic seals rotate in the groove and twist into a corkscrew shape. Cause: high friction, low surface finish, or non-symmetric squeeze. Fix: lubricate, polish the dynamic surface to Ra < 0.4 μm, switch to a square-cut or X-ring profile.
  • Compression setSeal loses its round cross-section and stops sealing. Cause: temperature spike past elastomer limit, or sustained squeeze with no relaxation periods. Fix: use a higher-temperature elastomer (FKM/Viton for 200°C, FFKM/Kalrez for 300°C+), or reduce installed squeeze.
  • Chemical attackSeal swells, hardens, or dissolves. Cause: incompatible fluid. Fix: verify elastomer compatibility against the actual process fluid — Parker compatibility charts are authoritative; do not extrapolate by similarity.
  • Installation damageSeal nicked or twisted during assembly. Cause: sharp edges, missing lead-in chamfers, or twisting during groove insertion. Fix: 15° lead-in chamfers, generous radii on all corners, install with assembly lube and avoid stretching past 50% of free ID.

Worked Example: Static Face Seal

Specify an O-ring for a static face seal: 100 mm OD flange sealing 10 MPa hydraulic oil at 80°C. Use AS568 standard sizing.

Pick a 75 Shore A nitrile O-ring. From the AS568 catalog, size −244 (ID = 104.14 mm, CS = 3.53 mm) sits naturally inside a 100 mm flange. With a typical 22% squeeze for static face seals: squeeze = 0.22 × 3.53 = 0.78 mm, so groove depth d = 3.53 − 0.78 = 2.75 mm. Per Parker handbook, groove width for static face = w = CS·1.42 = 5.01 mm, giving gland fill = π·CS²/4 / (w·d) = 9.78 / 13.78 = 71% — inside the 60–85% target band.

Check extrusion gap: for 10 MPa and 75 Shore A nitrile, Parker chart allows max diametral gap of 0.13 mm. Specify the flange face flatness to within 25 μm and the bolt circle tight enough to keep the joint closed at full pressure. If maintained, the seal is rated for years of service.

Picking a Seal

  1. 1Identify static vs dynamic. Dynamic seals (rotating, reciprocating) need lower squeeze and tighter surface finish than static seals.
  2. 2List the fluid, pressure, temperature, and duty cycle. Cross-check elastomer compatibility on the Parker compatibility chart.
  3. 3Pick a cord diameter from the AS568 (inch) or ISO 3601 (metric) catalog — not arbitrary. Off-standard cords cost 10× more and have long lead times.
  4. 4Set squeeze: 18–25% for static seals, 8–16% for dynamic, 30%+ for vacuum service.
  5. 5Set gland fill at 60–85% to leave room for thermal swell and chemical swell.
  6. 6Verify extrusion gap against the Parker durometer/pressure chart. Add a PTFE or PEEK backup ring on the low-pressure side if marginal.
  7. 7Specify surface finish: Ra 0.8 μm static, Ra 0.4 μm dynamic, Ra 0.2 μm rotary high-pressure.

Standards Detail

AS568 (Aerospace Standard 568A) is the dominant inch-series O-ring sizing standard, used by aerospace, hydraulics, and most US industrial equipment. Sizes range from −001 (1/32" ID) to −475 (26" ID), with consistent cross-sections within each dash range.

ISO 3601 is the metric counterpart, used widely in European equipment and increasingly in global standards. ISO 3601-1 covers dimensions, -2 tolerances, -3 quality acceptance, and -5 specific applications (high pressure, vacuum, hygienic).

The Parker O-Ring Handbook (ORD 5700) is the single most-cited industry reference. The free PDF includes compatibility charts, gland-design tables, troubleshooting guides, and worked examples. Highly recommended even if you don't buy Parker seals.

Engineer FAQ

When should I use a quad-ring (X-ring) instead of an O-ring?
Reciprocating dynamic seals where rolling is a problem — the four lobes resist spiraling, reduce friction at high squeeze, and provide twice the sealing line. Cost is ~30% more than equivalent O-rings.
How do I seal vacuum?
Use higher squeeze (30%+) than for pressure, use vacuum-grade elastomer (low outgassing FKM or FFKM), polish the sealing surface to Ra 0.4 μm or better, and verify the joint closes flat with no twist. Vacuum sealing leaks from the atmospheric side, not the vacuum side — the air pressure differential is your driving force.
Is silicone a good general-purpose elastomer?
No. Silicone has excellent temperature range (−60 to +200°C) but poor tear strength, poor abrasion resistance, and is incompatible with most hydrocarbons. Use it for static high-temperature seals, food-contact applications, or compression-relaxation-critical service. For general industrial, nitrile (NBR) or fluoroelastomer (FKM) is the default.
