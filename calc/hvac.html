HVAC & PSYCHROMETRICS

MIXING, SENSIBLE/LATENT, COOLING COIL, EVAPORATIVE COOLING

OPEN HVAC CALCULATOR →

WHAT THIS MODULE SOLVES

Moist-air properties depend on dry-bulb T, wet-bulb T_wb, humidity ratio W, relative humidity φ, dew point, and enthalpy h. Sensible heat changes T (q_s = ṁ·cp·ΔT); latent heat changes W (q_L = ṁ·h_fg·ΔW). Cooling coils typically operate "wet" with simultaneous sensible cooling and dehumidification. Evaporative cooling drops dry-bulb toward wet-bulb at constant enthalpy. Mixing two airstreams gives an outlet on the line connecting their state points, weighted by mass flow.

FORMULAS USED

  • W = 0.622 · Pw / (P - Pw)
  • h = cp·T + W·(2501 + 1.86·T) (kJ/kg)
  • q_s = ṁ_a · cp · ΔT
  • q_L = ṁ_a · h_fg · ΔW
  • SHR = q_s / (q_s + q_L)

References & StandardsASHRAE Fundamentals Ch. 1, ARI 410, AHRI 410

WHERE THIS APPLIES

Use for HVAC system load calculations, cooling-coil sizing, humidifier/dehumidifier design, energy-recovery wheels, and outdoor-air mixing. Compute sensible heat ratio SHR = q_s/q_total to pick coil bypass factor. For arid climates, evaporative cooling delivers significant capacity at a fraction of mechanical-cooling power.

Worked Example: Mixing Outdoor And Return Air

An HVAC system mixes 25% outdoor air (32°C dry-bulb, W = 0.018 kg/kg) with 75% return air (24°C, W = 0.010 kg/kg). Find the mixed-air condition.

By mass-weighted mixing: T_mix = 0.25·32 + 0.75·24 = 26°C. Humidity ratio: W_mix = 0.25·0.018 + 0.75·0.010 = 0.012 kg/kg. Enthalpy: h_mix = cp·T + W·(2501 + 1.86·T) = 1.005·26 + 0.012·(2501 + 1.86·26) = 26.13 + 30.59 = 56.7 kJ/kg.

Plot on psychrometric chart: mixed state sits on the line connecting outdoor and return state points, 25% of the way from return toward outdoor. Verify relative humidity: at 26°C, P_sat ≈ 3.36 kPa, P_w = W·P/(0.622+W) = 0.012·101.3/0.634 = 1.92 kPa, so RH = 1.92/3.36 = 57%. Comfortable starting point for the cooling coil.

Common Pitfalls

  • Confusing dry-bulb, wet-bulb, and dew-pointDry-bulb (T_db) is ordinary thermometer reading. Wet-bulb (T_wb) is the temperature reached by adiabatic saturation (wet-wick thermometer with airflow). Dew-point (T_dp) is the temperature at which moisture condenses out at constant W. All three give different numbers; specify which the design uses.
  • Assuming SHR is constantSensible Heat Ratio depends on the coil ADP (apparent dewpoint) and bypass factor. At part-load, coil SHR rises (more dry cooling, less dehumidification). Many comfort issues come from sizing for design-day SHR and finding moisture control inadequate at part-load.
  • Mixing psychrometric units carelesslyHumidity ratio W is dimensionless (kg moisture / kg dry air) — not grams or percent. Relative humidity φ is dimensionless ratio of partial pressures. Specific humidity = W/(1+W), often confused with W. Always carry units explicitly through equations.
  • Treating exhaust air as worthlessEnergy recovery ventilation (ERV) heat exchangers reclaim 50–80% of the enthalpy difference between exhaust and outdoor air. For climate extremes, this halves the HVAC load — pays back capital cost in 2–4 years for commercial buildings.
  • Underestimating latent load in humid climatesCoastal and tropical climates can have latent load equaling or exceeding sensible load. Standard cooling coils with low ADP and high bypass don't dehumidify adequately. Use direct expansion with reheat, desiccant wheels, or two-stage cooling for these climates.

Engineer FAQ

When should I use evaporative cooling?
In hot dry climates (Phoenix, Las Vegas, central interior of any continent). Indirect evaporative cooling can deliver 10°C drop at 30% of the energy of mechanical refrigeration. Direct evaporative adds moisture, useful in <30% RH environments. Combined indirect-direct units work well in moderate humidity.
How do I size a cooling coil for both sensible and latent load?
Find the design SHR (sensible / total). Pick a coil with matching ADP: low SHR (high latent) needs lower ADP (~7°C), high SHR needs higher ADP (~12°C). Coil bypass factor (BF) determines outlet conditions; commercial coils typically BF = 0.05–0.20.
What's the difference between ASHRAE Fundamentals and ASHRAE 90.1?
Fundamentals is the engineering handbook — correlations, properties, methods. 90.1 is the energy standard adopted as building code in most US states. Fundamentals teaches how, 90.1 mandates what minimum performance is allowed for new construction or major renovation.
