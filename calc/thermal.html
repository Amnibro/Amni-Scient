HEAT TRANSFER (CONDUCTION, CONVECTION, RADIATION)

1D CONDUCTION, NU CORRELATIONS, RADIATION, FIN EFFICIENCY

OPEN THERMAL CALCULATOR →

WHAT IT COMPUTES

Three modes carry heat. Conduction: q = -k·A·dT/dx, governed by thermal conductivity k. Multi-layer walls add resistances in series: R = L/(k·A). Convection: q = h·A·ΔT, with h from Nusselt-number correlations (Dittus-Boelter for turbulent pipe flow, Churchill-Chu for natural convection). Radiation: q = ε·σ·A·(Ts⁴ - T∞⁴). Fins extend surface area; efficiency η = tanh(mL)/mL where m = sqrt(hP/kA).

EQUATIONS AT WORK

  • q = -k·A·dT/dx (conduction)
  • q = h·A·(Ts - T∞) (convection)
  • q = εσA(Ts⁴ - T∞⁴) (radiation)
  • η_fin = tanh(mL)/mL, m = sqrt(hP/(kA))
  • τ = ρ·V·cp / (h·A) (lumped)

References & StandardsIncropera "Fundamentals of Heat Transfer", ASHRAE Fundamentals

TYPICAL APPLICATIONS

Use for any temperature-driven heat-flow problem: building walls, pipe insulation, electronic-component cooling, furnace walls, heat-sink design. Identify dominant mode(s) and build a thermal resistance network. For transient problems with Bi < 0.1, use lumped-capacitance: T(t) = T∞ + (T0 - T∞)·exp(-t/τ). For higher Biot number, use the Heisler charts or finite-difference solution.

Worked Example: Composite Wall Heat Loss

A storage tank wall consists of 6 mm carbon steel (k = 50 W/m·K), 100 mm mineral wool insulation (k = 0.04 W/m·K), and 1 mm aluminum cladding (k = 200 W/m·K). Inside fluid at 90°C with h_i = 200 W/m²·K; outside air at 25°C with h_o = 15 W/m²·K (natural convection). Find heat flux and outer cladding temperature.

Build the resistance network per unit area: R_i = 1/200 = 0.005 m²·K/W, R_steel = 0.006/50 = 0.00012, R_ins = 0.100/0.04 = 2.50, R_alum = 0.001/200 = 5×10⁻⁶, R_o = 1/15 = 0.0667. Total: R_tot = 2.572 m²·K/W.

Heat flux: q'' = ΔT / R_tot = (90 − 25) / 2.572 = 25.3 W/m². Without insulation, R_tot would be ~0.07 and q'' ≈ 930 W/m² — the 100 mm of mineral wool cuts heat loss by 97%.

Outer cladding temperature: T_clad = T_air + q''·R_o = 25 + 25.3·0.0667 = 26.7°C. Cool to the touch, as expected. The inside-wall surface temperature: T_i,wall = T_fluid − q''·R_i = 90 − 0.127 = 89.9°C — nearly the fluid temp, since R_i is negligible vs R_ins.

Common Pitfalls

  • Confusing thermal conductivity k with overall Uk is a material property (W/m·K). U is a system property combining conduction through layers + convection at boundaries (W/m²·K). Use U·A·ΔT for system-level heat transfer; use k·A·dT/dx for a single material slab.
  • Treating natural convection like forcedNatural-convection h depends on ΔT and orientation (vertical wall vs horizontal floor vs ceiling). Use Churchill-Chu for vertical walls, McAdams for horizontal. Typical h ranges: 5–25 W/m²·K natural air, 25–250 forced air, 50–2000 condensing/boiling.
  • Ignoring radiation in "low temperature" problemsRadiation scales as T⁴ but the equivalent h_rad ≈ 4·ε·σ·T_avg³ is comparable to natural convection h_nat above ~50°C. Always check; for hot uninsulated pipes, radiation often equals or exceeds convection.
  • Using lumped capacitance outside its validityLumped analysis requires Biot number Bi = h·L_c/k < 0.1. For thick or low-k materials, this is violated and you must use distributed analysis (1D Heisler charts, or numerical FD). A 25 mm thick steel plate at Bi = 0.02 is lumped-OK; 25 mm thick mineral wool at Bi = 30 is not.
  • Neglecting fin efficiencyAdding fins doesn't multiply heat transfer by the area ratio — the fin tip is colder than the base, so it removes less heat per unit area. Fin efficiency η = tanh(mL)/mL ranges 0.5–0.95 for typical aluminum fins. Surface area enhancement is fin_area·η + base_area.

Industry References

Incropera, DeWitt, Bergman, Lavine — "Fundamentals of Heat and Mass Transfer" is the dominant undergraduate text and engineering reference. Chapter 3 covers 1D conduction with resistance networks; Chapter 6–9 covers convection correlations.

ASHRAE Fundamentals Handbook is the practical reference for HVAC and building heat transfer, including detailed U-factor tables for wall assemblies, glazing, and infiltration.

VDI Heat Atlas (English edition) is the most comprehensive correlation compendium — if you can't find a Nusselt correlation for your specific geometry there, it probably doesn't exist in literature. Highly recommended for unusual configurations (jet impingement, packed beds, fluidized beds).

Engineer FAQ

How do I pick insulation thickness?
Optimum is the thickness where marginal cost of insulation equals marginal benefit of energy saved over the asset life. For typical industrial steam piping, this is 50–150 mm depending on size and temperature; for cold piping, 25–75 mm to prevent condensation. ASTM C680 has explicit economic optimization procedure.
When does radiation dominate vs convection?
High temperatures (T > 400°C surface), vacuum environments (no convection), or large temperature differences. Boiler walls, electric heaters, and exhaust manifolds often have radiation = 2–5× convection. Below 100°C surface temperature, radiation is usually a 10–30% effect.
Why does my fin not dissipate as predicted?
Most common causes: fin base contact resistance (poor solder, loose mechanical attachment) cuts effective heat transfer 30–70%. Fouling on the fin surface adds resistance. Air-side flow bypass channels around fins, not through them. Verify mechanical contact, clean surfaces, and confirm flow direction matches design.
