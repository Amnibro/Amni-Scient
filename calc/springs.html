COIL & BELLEVILLE SPRINGS

WAHL CORRECTION, ALMEN-LASZLO BELLEVILLE, STACK CONFIGURATIONS

OPEN SPRINGS CALCULATOR →

WHAT IT COMPUTES

Helical compression springs follow F = k·δ with stiffness k = G·d⁴/(8·D³·N). The Wahl correction factor accounts for the stress concentration on the inner edge of the wire due to curvature. Belleville (disc) springs use the Almen-Laszlo equations to capture their nonlinear load-deflection curve, which can be flat or even regressive depending on the height-to-thickness ratio h/t. Stacks in series add deflections; stacks in parallel add forces.

KEY EQUATIONS

  • k = G·d⁴ / (8·D³·N)
  • τ = K_W · 8·F·D / (π·d³)
  • K_W = (4C-1)/(4C-4) + 0.615/C, C = D/d
  • σ_BV = 4E·δ / [(1-ν²)·M·De²]·[h - δ/2 + t]
  • f_n = 0.5·sqrt(k·g/W)

References & StandardsSAE HS-795, DIN 2092 (Belleville), Shigley Ch. 10

WHEN TO USE THIS MODULE

Coil springs handle linear load-deflection problems: valves, suspension, seal preload, return springs. Belleville washers handle high-load short-deflection cases: bolted joints needing constant clamp force as gaskets relax, machine-tool spindles, or thermal-compensation in fastener stacks. Verify solid-height clearance, buckling for slender coils (Lf/D > 5.26 for free ends), and fatigue life if cyclic.

Helical Compression Spring Design Procedure

  1. 1Define application loads: F_min (installed preload) and F_max (working high), and the allowable deflection between them.
  2. 2Compute required stiffness k = (F_max − F_min) / δ_work. This is the spring constant target.
  3. 3Pick a candidate wire diameter d and mean coil diameter D. Spring index C = D/d is typically 6–12; below 4 the coils are hard to wind and stress concentration spikes; above 14 buckling becomes a risk.
  4. 4Solve for active coils: N = G·d⁴ / (8·D³·k). Total coils = N + 2 for squared-ground ends.
  5. 5Check shear stress at maximum load: τ = K_W·8·F_max·D / (π·d³). Must stay below the allowable (typically 0.45·Sut for static, 0.30·Sut for fatigue).
  6. 6Verify solid height (all coils touching): L_solid = (N+2)·d, and free length L_free clears L_solid + δ_max with margin.
  7. 7For long springs (L_free / D > 5.26 with free ends), add a buckling check or specify guided ends.

Worked Example: Valve Return Spring

An engine valve spring must hold 250 N at installed length 35 mm and 600 N at fully compressed 25 mm. Pick d, D, N for chrome-silicon wire (G = 79.3 GPa, Sut = 1800 MPa).

Required stiffness: k = (600 − 250) / (35 − 25) = 35 N/mm. Try d = 3.0 mm, D = 22 mm (C = 7.33). Wahl factor: K_W = (4·7.33−1)/(4·7.33−4) + 0.615/7.33 = 1.20.

Active coils: N = G·d⁴ / (8·D³·k) = 79,300·81 / (8·10,648·35) = 2.16. Round to 2.25 active coils, add 2 for squared-ground ends → 4.25 total. Recalc k with N = 2.25: k = 79,300·81 / (8·10,648·2.25) = 33.6 N/mm — slightly soft, adjust to N = 2.10 if exactly 35 is needed.

Peak shear at F = 600 N: τ = 1.20·8·600·22 / (π·3³) = 1495 MPa. Allowable for static = 0.45·1800 = 810 MPa. Stress exceeds allowable — either increase d to 3.5 mm, or downsize D, or pick a higher-strength alloy (music wire ASTM A228 reaches Sut 2200 MPa in this diameter). For valve duty with cyclic loading, the actual fatigue allowable is tighter still; this is why production valve springs use shot-peened wire and oil tempering.

Common Pitfalls

  • Ignoring the Wahl correctionPlain torsion theory τ = 8·F·D / (π·d³) underestimates stress on the inner coil edge by 15–30% depending on spring index. The Wahl factor captures curvature; skip it and the spring fails earlier than analysis predicts.
  • Forgetting solid-height clearanceA spring must reach maximum design deflection without coils touching. Specify L_free so that L_free − δ_max ≥ L_solid + 10% margin. Hitting solid generates massive shock stress that breaks springs and surrounding parts.
  • Treating series and parallel stacks symmetricallySprings in series add deflections at equal force: k_eq = 1/(1/k1 + 1/k2). Springs in parallel add forces at equal deflection: k_eq = k1 + k2. Mixing them up wrecks the stiffness target by orders of magnitude.
  • Choosing the wrong end conditionPlain ends behave like a free column for buckling; squared-and-ground ends double the effective stiffness against side-loading. Pneumatic and gas-strut springs need very specific guide geometries.

Why Spring Index Matters

Spring index C = D/d is the single most important geometry ratio in helical spring design. Low C (tight coils) causes severe stress concentration on the inner fiber, raising the Wahl factor and forcing a stronger wire or larger diameter. High C (loose coils) lowers stiffness rapidly and increases buckling risk. The sweet spot, 6 ≤ C ≤ 12, balances manufacturability, stress, and stability.

The fourth-power dependence of k on d makes wire diameter the dominant design lever: bumping d from 3 to 4 mm at fixed D increases stiffness 3.16×. Mean coil diameter D acts with a third-power inverse relationship — halving D multiplies k by 8. Active coil count N is the easy fine-tuning knob with linear effect.

Engineer FAQ

When are Belleville (disc) springs better than coils?
Three cases: (1) very high force in a short stroke — one stack of Bellevilles can replace 10 coil springs in the same envelope. (2) non-linear or constant-force profiles — tuning h/t ratio gives flat or even decreasing force-deflection. (3) thermal compensation in bolted joints, where Belleville stack absorbs gasket relaxation without losing preload.
How do I prevent spring surge?
Surge happens when excitation frequency approaches the spring's own natural frequency f_n = 0.5·sqrt(k·g/W). For valve springs, the excitation can be cam-driven harmonics that resonate the active coils. Solutions: keep f_n well above the highest excitation, use a damper coil (variable pitch), or use a "stranded" multi-wire spring.
Why do springs fail in fatigue more often than overload?
Surface tensile stress under cyclic compression drives fatigue cracks at the inner-coil fiber. Shot-peening induces residual compressive stress at the surface that doubles or triples fatigue life. Combine shot-peening with stress-relief annealing and a corrosion-resistant coating for maximum life.
