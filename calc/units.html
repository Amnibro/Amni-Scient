WHAT THE CALCULATOR HANDLES

Bidirectional conversion between SI and US customary units across all engineering domains: length (mm ↔ in), force (N ↔ lbf), pressure (Pa, kPa, bar ↔ psi), energy (J, kJ, kWh ↔ BTU, ft-lbf), power (W, kW ↔ hp), torque (N·m ↔ in-lbf, ft-lbf), viscosity (Pa·s ↔ cP, lb/(ft·s)), thermal conductivity (W/m·K ↔ BTU/(hr·ft·°F)), and temperature (°C, K, °F, °R). Includes density, flow, and concentration conversions.

KEY EQUATIONS

  • 1 lbf = 4.4482 N
  • 1 psi = 6894.76 Pa
  • 1 BTU = 1055.06 J
  • 1 hp = 745.7 W
  • T(°F) = T(°C)·1.8 + 32
  • 1 cP = 0.001 Pa·s

References & StandardsNIST SP 811, ASTM SI 10, BIPM SI Brochure

WHEN THIS IS THE RIGHT TOOL

Use whenever mixing unit systems: a US specification with European hardware, an ASTM material rating quoted in ksi being matched to an ISO bolt rated in MPa, or converting handbook constants to a consistent system before plugging into a formula. Always verify the conversion by tracking units through the equation rather than trusting a "feel" for the magnitude.

Common Unit Pitfalls

  • Mass vs force confusion1 lbm of mass weighs 1 lbf only at standard gravity. Mass × g = force; this is where the units factor 32.174 lbm·ft/(lbf·s²) enters US-customary equations. SI is much cleaner: 1 kg·1 m/s² = 1 N. Switch to SI when the math gets confusing.
  • Imperial vs metric horsepower1 mechanical hp = 745.7 W. 1 metric hp (PS in Germany, CV in France) = 735.5 W. Difference is 1.4% — matters for engine ratings comparison and fuel economy.
  • BTU/hr vs ton of refrigeration1 ton refrigeration = 12,000 BTU/hr = 3517 W. HVAC engineers in the US still use "tons" for capacity; international uses kW. Easy 12,000× multiplication error when switching mid-calculation.
  • Absolute vs gauge pressureBar(g) (gauge) vs bar(a) (absolute): differ by 1 atm. Compressor discharge of 7 bar(g) = 8 bar(a). Process design uses gauge; thermodynamic calcs (Nernst, ideal gas) use absolute. Always check which.
  • Celsius vs Kelvin in differential equationsTemperature differences are identical: 1°C step = 1 K step. But absolute temperature (in T⁴ radiation, ideal gas PV = nRT) must be K. Engineering formulas often look ambiguous; convert to K when in doubt.

Engineer FAQ

Why use SI when US Customary works fine?
Two reasons: (1) Cleaner math — no gc unit-conversion factor between mass and force. (2) Global team compatibility — mechanical drawings, FEA models, and CAD files share more easily across SI-default and US-default tools. Most US engineering firms now publish in SI even when the customer specifies US.
When do I need to worry about cgs vs MKS units?
Older textbooks and some physics-oriented references use cgs (cm, g, dyne, erg). Modern engineering is MKS (m, kg, N, J). Mixing creates 10⁵ scaling errors. If you're unsure, verify by computing a known quantity: density of water = 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³ tells you which system you're reading.
How accurate are typical conversion factors?
NIST SP 811 lists conversions to 10 significant figures. Engineering tables typically round to 4–5 figures. For 0.1% accuracy in final answer, carry 5+ figures through intermediate calcs — rounding errors compound through long calculations.
