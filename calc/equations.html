Equation Reference FAQ

How do I cite an equation in a design report?
Reference the original code or textbook section: "Per ASME VIII-1 UG-27(c)(1), required wall thickness t = P·R/(S·E − 0.6·P)." Auditors and reviewers expect this traceability. The Amni-Calc module pages link each equation to its standard or textbook source.
Why do similar-looking equations give different answers?
Most engineering equations have implicit sign conventions, dimensional assumptions, and validity ranges. For example, Bernoulli (incompressible, frictionless) vs Darcy (incompressible with friction) vs Fanno (compressible, adiabatic): all describe pipe flow, but each only applies under specific conditions. The module pages call out each equation's validity domain.
Can I trust a one-line "rule of thumb"?
Rules of thumb (1/4 horsepower per CFM for fans, 100 gal/min per 10 HP for water pumps) are decent for first-pass sizing. They embed unstated assumptions about efficiency, operating point, and service factor. For final design, always run the proper equation with actual operating conditions.
