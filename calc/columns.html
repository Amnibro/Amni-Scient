COLUMN BUCKLING (EULER & JOHNSON)

SLENDERNESS RATIO, END-CONDITION K, EULER-JOHNSON TRANSITION

OPEN COLUMNS CALCULATOR →

WHAT IT COMPUTES

Long compression members fail by buckling rather than yielding. Euler's formula P_cr = π²EI / (KL)² governs slender columns where buckling stress stays below yield. K is the end-condition factor: 0.5 (fixed-fixed), 0.7 (fixed-pinned), 1.0 (pinned-pinned), 2.0 (fixed-free). For shorter columns the actual failure stress curves below Euler's prediction; the Johnson formula handles this transition region using yield strength. The crossover slenderness is (KL/r)_t = sqrt(2π²E/Sy).

EQUATIONS AT WORK

  • (KL/r)_t = sqrt(2π²E / Sy)
  • Euler: P_cr = π²EI / (KL)²
  • Johnson: σ_cr = Sy - (Sy·KL / (2π·r))² / E
  • FoS = P_cr / P_applied

References & StandardsAISC 360 Ch. E, Shigley 4.11, Norton 4.16

TYPICAL APPLICATIONS

Apply column analysis to any compression member: pneumatic-cylinder rod extended fully (treat as fixed-pinned with K=0.7), forklift mast, screw-jack lead screw, structural strut, scaffolding leg. Compute slenderness ratio KL/r first; if above (KL/r)_t use Euler, otherwise use Johnson. Apply factor of safety 2-4 depending on dynamic loading and uncertainty in end conditions.

The Euler-Johnson Transition

Euler's formula assumes the column buckles while still elastic. As columns get shorter, the predicted critical stress σ_cr exceeds the material yield — impossible, since the column would yield before buckling. The transition slenderness (KL/r)_t marks where Euler's prediction equals half the yield stress. Below this, the Johnson formula (a parabolic fit) gives realistic short-column failure.

Three regimes: long columns (KL/r > (KL/r)_t) fail by elastic buckling, predicted by Euler. Intermediate columns (KL/r between (KL/r)_t and ~30) fail by inelastic buckling, predicted by Johnson. Short columns (KL/r < 30) fail by yielding, predicted by P = Sy·A. Always compute KL/r first and dispatch to the correct formula.

Worked Example: Hydraulic Cylinder Rod

A double-acting hydraulic cylinder has a 25 mm diameter chrome-plated rod extending 750 mm fully stroked. Cylinder rated for 12 kN push. Check buckling for A36 steel rod (Sy = 250 MPa, E = 200 GPa).

End conditions: rod fixed at the piston-end bushing, free at the work end — K = 2.0 (worst case). Effective length: KL = 2.0 × 0.750 = 1.5 m. Section properties: A = π·(0.0125)² = 4.91×10⁻⁴ m², I = π·(0.0125)⁴/4 = 1.92×10⁻⁸ m⁴, r = sqrt(I/A) = 6.25×10⁻³ m. Slenderness: KL/r = 1.5 / 0.00625 = 240.

Transition slenderness: (KL/r)_t = sqrt(2π²·200×10⁹ / 250×10⁶) = 126. Since 240 > 126, Euler applies: P_cr = π²·200×10⁹·1.92×10⁻⁸ / 1.5² = 16,840 N = 16.8 kN. Factor of safety: FoS = 16.8 / 12 = 1.4. For hydraulic applications with shock loading, FoS 2–4 is standard — this rod is undersized. Increase to 32 mm diameter or add a guide bushing at mid-stroke to halve the effective length.

Common Pitfalls

  • Using the wrong K factorEnd conditions matter enormously. Fixed-fixed (K = 0.5) gives 16× the buckling load of fixed-free (K = 2.0). Real-world end conditions are rarely ideal; AISC recommends K = 0.65 for fixed-fixed (not 0.5) and K = 0.80 for fixed-pinned (not 0.7) to account for realistic joint flexibility.
  • Ignoring local buckling in thin-walled columnsThin tubes and built-up sections can fail by local wall buckling well before Euler. Width-to-thickness ratio limits in AISC 360 Table B4.1a govern this; the calc module assumes compact sections.
  • Treating dynamic loads as staticSudden compression (impact, shock loading) amplifies the static load by 2× or more. Apply a dynamic magnification factor or use peak transient load in the buckling check.
  • Missing the imperfection sensitivityEuler's formula assumes a perfectly straight column. Real columns have eccentricity that lowers the critical load by 10–30%. The secant formula handles this explicitly: σ_max = (P/A)·[1 + (ec/r²)·sec(KL/(2r)·sqrt(P/EA))]. For critical applications, design to this rather than pure Euler.

Engineer FAQ

What K value should I use for a column that frames into a beam?
AISC Specification provides alignment charts (Fig. C-A-7.1 and 7.2) for sidesway-uninhibited and sidesway-inhibited frames. Most steel framed buildings use computed K values from these charts — never just assume K = 1.0 for "pinned-pinned" because the beam stiffness affects the column.
How do I handle a column with intermediate lateral support?
Lateral bracing creates an additional pinned point. The column buckles in the smaller of two modes: between supports, or in a sweep across all supports. Compute both KL values and use the higher slenderness for design.
When does compression yielding govern instead of buckling?
For very short columns where KL/r < ~30, the Johnson parabola flattens and short-column failure approaches pure yielding: P = Sy·A. Cubes and short blocks always fail by yielding or compression-shear, never by buckling.
