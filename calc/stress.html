STRESS ANALYSIS & MOHR'S CIRCLE

PRINCIPAL STRESSES, MAX SHEAR, VON MISES, TRESCA YIELD

OPEN STRESS CALCULATOR →

WHAT THIS MODULE SOLVES

Stress analysis converts a multi-axial state of stress (σx, σy, τxy) into a single scalar that can be compared to a material yield strength. Two principal directions exist where shear vanishes and normal stress is maximum or minimum; these are the principal stresses σ1 and σ2. The maximum in-plane shear stress sits at 45° to those axes. Mohr's circle plots normal stress on the x-axis and shear stress on the y-axis, parameterizing the rotation of the stress tensor as you change reference frame.

GOVERNING EQUATIONS

  • σ_avg = (σx + σy)/2
  • R = sqrt(((σx - σy)/2)² + τxy²)
  • σ1, σ2 = σ_avg ± R
  • τ_max = R
  • σ_vM = sqrt(σ1² - σ1·σ2 + σ2²)
  • FoS = Sy / σ_vM

References & StandardsShigley 9.6, Norton 5.4, ASME BPVC VIII-1 UG-23

REAL-WORLD USE CASES

Use this module any time a part sees combined loading: a pressure-vessel wall (hoop + longitudinal + radial), a shaft under torque + bending, a weld throat under shear + tension, or a bolted bracket where eccentric load creates simultaneous tension and shear at the same fastener. The von Mises criterion gives a single number to compare to yield for ductile materials; the maximum-shear (Tresca) criterion is more conservative. For brittle materials, use the maximum principal-stress criterion instead.

Worked Example: Shaft Under Torsion + Bending

A 40 mm diameter solid shaft transmits 250 N·m of torque while carrying a 1.8 kN transverse load 300 mm from the nearest bearing. Find the maximum von Mises stress and factor of safety against yield for AISI 1045 steel (Sy = 530 MPa).

Bending moment at the critical section: M = 1800 N × 0.3 m = 540 N·m. Section modulus S = π·d³/32 = π·(0.040)³/32 = 6.28×10⁻⁶ m³, so bending stress σx = M/S = 86.0 MPa. Polar section modulus Z_p = π·d³/16 = 12.57×10⁻⁶ m³, so shear stress τxy = T/Z_p = 19.9 MPa. The shaft surface sees σx = 86, σy = 0, τxy = 19.9 MPa.

Mohr's circle: σ_avg = 43.0 MPa, R = sqrt(43² + 19.9²) = 47.4 MPa, giving σ1 = 90.4 MPa, σ2 = -4.4 MPa. Von Mises: σ_vM = sqrt(90.4² + 4.4² + 90.4·4.4) = 92.7 MPa. Factor of safety: FoS = 530 / 92.7 = 5.7. The shaft is conservative for static load — but verify fatigue separately, because the rotating bending stress fully reverses every revolution.

Step-By-Step Workflow

  1. 1Identify the critical cross-section where bending moment and torque are simultaneously high (usually near a support or step change).
  2. 2Compute σx, σy, τxy at the surface fiber facing the highest stress. Use signed values — tension positive.
  3. 3Build Mohr's circle: center σ_avg, radius R. Read off σ1 (top of circle) and σ2 (bottom).
  4. 4Pick the failure criterion before computing: von Mises for ductile metals under static load, max-shear (Tresca) for conservative ductile design, max-principal for brittle materials (cast iron, ceramics, glass).
  5. 5Compute the equivalent stress and divide into Sy to get FoS. Compare against the required factor for your industry (1.5 for aerospace, 2–3 for general industrial, 4+ for crane and lifting).
  6. 6For cyclic loading, recompute mean and amplitude separately and run the Fatigue module afterward — static FoS alone is insufficient.

Common Pitfalls

  • Using von Mises on brittle materialsCast iron, ceramics, and glass fail in tension long before they reach distortion-energy yield. Use the maximum-principal-stress criterion (Rankine) with the brittle ultimate, not Sy.
  • Ignoring the third principal stress in 2D analysis2D Mohr's gives σ1 and σ2 in-plane, with σ3 = 0. Out-of-plane shear can still control: τ_max,3D = (σ_max − σ_min)/2 where σ_min may be zero, not σ2.
  • Treating a stress concentration as average stressHoles, fillets, and keyways multiply nominal stress by Kt. Find Kt from Peterson's charts before applying yield criterion, especially for brittle materials where Kt acts at full value.
  • Confusing nominal yield with proof strength on bolted jointsBolt manufacturers spec proof stress (0.2% offset) typically 85–90% of Sy. Use proof stress for preload limits, not yield.
  • Skipping the sign on τxyMohr's circle radius is invariant to sign, but principal-direction angle 2θp is not. If you need the actual orientation (for fiber-orientation analysis or composite layup), keep the sign consistent throughout.

Why Mohr's Circle Works

The 2D stress tensor at a point has three independent components — σx, σy, τxy. Rotating reference frames by an angle θ transforms these via a sinusoid in 2θ. Plotting normal stress on the x-axis and shear on the y-axis as θ sweeps generates a circle: center at (σ_avg, 0), radius equal to the maximum in-plane shear. The two intersections with the horizontal axis are the principal stresses, where shear vanishes by definition.

The distortion-energy (von Mises) criterion assumes ductile yielding initiates when the energy of shape distortion reaches a critical value — not the energy of volume change. Pure hydrostatic stress contributes no distortion energy, so a deep-ocean diver is safe from yield at 1000 atm even though σ_h is enormous. This is why von Mises is the default ductile criterion: it isolates the part of the stress state that actually causes yield.

Engineer FAQ

When should I use Tresca instead of von Mises?
Tresca (maximum shear stress) is more conservative — it predicts yield earlier by about 15% for pure shear. Use it for pressure-vessel codes that mandate it (ASME VIII Div 2 uses Tresca for primary stress limits), for safety-critical design where conservatism is wanted, or in early hand-calcs where simpler math is acceptable.
How do I handle thermal stresses?
Compute thermal strain ε_t = α·ΔT first. If constrained, this becomes thermal stress σ_t = E·α·ΔT, which is added to the mechanical stress tensor (using signed values) before applying any yield criterion. Free expansion produces zero stress.
Why does my finite-element output report σ_vM but not σ1/σ2?
Most FEA post-processors default to von Mises because it's the single scalar most engineers want. Look for the "principal stresses" or "stress tensor components" output option — every solver computes these internally, you just have to ask for them.
Is FoS = 1.5 enough?
Depends on what's uncertain. With well-characterized material, well-measured load, and a benign failure mode, 1.5 is enough (aerospace structural). For unknown overloads or brittle materials, design FoS climbs to 3–6. For crane and lifting equipment, codes typically mandate 4 or 5 on the breaking strength.
